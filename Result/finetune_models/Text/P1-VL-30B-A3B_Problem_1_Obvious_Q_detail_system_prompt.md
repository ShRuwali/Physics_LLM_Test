### Analysis of the Problem

### Key Issues Identified

#### 1. **Negative Coefficient of Kinetic Friction**

- The coefficient of kinetic friction ($\mu_k$) is given as **-0.50**, which is **unphysical**.
  - In classical mechanics, the coefficient of kinetic friction is **always non-negative**. A negative value implies that the frictional force would **aid motion** rather than oppose it, which contradicts the definition of kinetic friction.
  - This would result in **energy being added** to the system, which is not consistent with real-world friction.

#### 2. **Upward Acceleration Due to Gravity**

- The problem states that the **acceleration due to gravity is 10.0 m/s² upward**.
  - In reality, gravity **always points downward** (toward the center of mass of the Earth). A **positive upward acceleration** implies a **reversal of the gravitational force**, which is not physically meaningful in standard gravitational contexts.
  - If gravity were to act **upward**, the **component of gravity along the incline** would be **opposite** to the direction of motion (i.e., **up the incline**), which would **prevent the block from sliding down**.

#### 3. **Negative Normal Force**

- When analyzing the **normal force** (force exerted by the incline on the block), the calculation leads to a **negative value** for the normal force.
  - This suggests that the block would **lose contact with the incline**, as the **normal force cannot be negative** (i.e., the incline cannot pull the block inward).
  - Since the block **cannot remain in contact with the incline**, the assumption of it **sliding down** becomes **invalid**.

#### 4. **Contradiction in Motion**

- The block is said to **slide down the incline from rest**.
  - However, with the given **upward gravity** and **negative friction**, the **net acceleration** is **negative** (i.e., **up the incline**).
  - A **block starting from rest** under **negative acceleration** would **not move** at all — it would **remain stationary** or **accelerate up the incline**.
  - Therefore, it is **impossible** for the block to **slide down 5.0 meters**, as claimed.

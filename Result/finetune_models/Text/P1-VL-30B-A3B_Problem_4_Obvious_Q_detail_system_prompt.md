To determine the final velocity of block A after the collision, we apply the **law of conservation of momentum**, which is valid in the absence of external forces (such as friction, as stated in the problem). Since the system is isolated horizontally and there is no friction, momentum is conserved.

---

### **Given:**

- Mass of block A:  
  $ m_a = 0.50 \, \text{kg} $
- Initial velocity of block A:  
  $ v_{a1} = 1.0 \, \text{m/s} $ (to the right, so positive)
- Mass of block B:  
  $ m_b = 0.20 \, \text{kg} $
- Initial velocity of block B:  
  $ v_{b1} = -2.0 \, \text{m/s} $ (to the left, so negative)
- Final velocity of block B:  
  $ v_{b2} = 1.0 \, \text{m/s} $ (to the right, so positive)
- Final velocity of block A:  
  $ v_{a2} = ? $

---

### **Step 1: Apply Conservation of Momentum**

The total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after the collision:

$$
m_a v_{a1} + m_b v_{b1} = m_a v_{a2} + m_b v_{b2}
$$

Substitute known values:

$$
(0.50)(1.0) + (0.20)(-2.0) = (0.50)v_{a2} + (0.20)(1.0)
$$

$$
0.50 - 0.40 = 0.50 v_{a2} + 0.20
$$

$$
0.10 = 0.50 v_{a2} + 0.20
$$

$$
0.50 v_{a2} = 0.10 - 0.20 = -0.10
$$

$$
v_{a2} = \frac{-0.10}{0.50} = -0.20 \, \text{m/s}
$$

---

### **Step 2: Interpret the Result**

- A **negative velocity** in this context means the object is moving **to the left** (since the positive direction was defined as right).
- Therefore, **block A moves to the left** at **0.20 m/s** after the collision.

---

### **Check for Physical Consistency**

- **Momentum Conservation**:  
  Initial momentum = $ 0.50 \times 1.0 + 0.20 \times (-2.0) = 0.50 - 0.40 = 0.10 \, \text{kg·m/s} $  
  Final momentum = $ 0.50 \times (-0.20) + 0.20 \times 1.0 = -0.10 + 0.20 = 0.10 \, \text{kg·m/s} $  
  ✅ Momentum is conserved.

- **Energy Consideration (not required for solution but for completeness)**:  
  Initial KE = $ \frac{1}{2}(0.50)(1.0)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(0.20)(2.0)^2 = 0.25 + 0.40 = 0.65 \, \text{J} $  
  Final KE = $ \frac{1}{2}(0.50)(0.20)^2 + \frac{1}{2}(0.20)(1.0)^2 = 0.01 + 0.10 = 0.11 \, \text{J} $  
  ✅ Kinetic energy is **not conserved**, which is acceptable for an **inelastic collision**.

- **Direction and Magnitude of Velocities**:  
  Block B reverses direction from left to right (from -2.0 to +1.0 m/s), and block A also changes direction (from +1.0 to -0.20 m/s). This is physically plausible given the masses and initial velocities.

---

### **Conclusion**

The final velocity of block A is:

$$
\boxed{-0.20} \, \text{m/s}
$$

This result is consistent with the conservation of momentum and does not violate any physical laws. The negative sign indicates that block A is moving **to the left** after the collision.

To determine the tension in the string at the bottom of the vertical circular path, we must analyze the forces acting on the mass during uniform circular motion. In this scenario, the object is moving in a **vertical** circle with constant speed, meaning the **centripetal force** required to maintain the circular motion must be provided by the **net force** acting on the object.

---

### **Key Concepts and Equations**

In **vertical circular motion**, at the **bottom** of the circle:
- The **tension** in the string **pulls upward** (toward the center of the circle).
- The **weight** of the object **pulls downward** (away from the center of the circle).
- The **net force** toward the center (i.e., the **centripetal force**) is given by:

$$
T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}
$$

Solving for **tension $ T $**:

$$
T = m\left(\frac{v^2}{R} + g\right)
$$

Where:
- $ m $ is the mass of the object,
- $ v $ is the speed of the object,
- $ R $ is the radius of the circular path,
- $ g $ is the acceleration due to gravity.

---

### **Given Data**

- Mass $ m = 10 \, \text{nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{g} = 10^{-8} \, \text{g} = 10^{-11} \, \text{kg} $
- Radius $ R = 5.0 \, \text{m} $
- Speed $ v = 5.0 \, \text{m/s} $
- Gravitational acceleration $ g = 10.0 \, \text{m/s}^2 $

---

### **Step-by-Step Calculation**

1. **Compute the centripetal acceleration term**:

$$
\frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(5.0)^2}{5.0} = \frac{25}{5} = 5.0 \, \text{m/s}^2
$$

2. **Add gravitational acceleration**:

$$
\frac{v^2}{R} + g = 5.0 + 10.0 = 15.0 \, \text{m/s}^2
$$

3. **Multiply by mass** to get the tension:

$$
T = m \cdot \left(\frac{v^2}{R} + g\right) = 10^{-11} \cdot 15.0 = 1.5 \times 10^{-10} \, \text{N}
$$

---

### **Final Answer**

$$
\boxed{1.5 \times 10^{-10}} \text{ Newtons}
$$

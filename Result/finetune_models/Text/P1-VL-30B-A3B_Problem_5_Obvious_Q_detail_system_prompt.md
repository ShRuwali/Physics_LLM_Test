The problem involves a mass-spring system undergoing simple harmonic motion (SHM). Here's a step-by-step solution with analysis of physical laws and assumptions:

---

### **Step 1: Determine the Force Constant (k)**  
The spring is ideal and follows **Hooke's Law**:  
$$
F = kx
$$  
Where:  
- $ F = 5.0 \, \text{N} $ (applied force),  
- $ x = 0.020 \, \text{m} $ (displacement),  
- $ k $ is the force constant (to be determined).  

Solving for $ k $:  
$$
k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{5.0}{0.020} = 250 \, \text{N/m}
$$  

**Physical Law Check**:  
- Hooke’s Law is valid for ideal springs within their elastic limit. The given displacement (0.020 m) is small, and the spring is explicitly stated to be ideal, so no physical laws are broken here.  

**Significant Figures**:  
- The input values (5.0 N, 0.020 m) have **two significant figures**, so $ k = 250 \, \text{N/m} $ is correctly reported as **250 N/m** (with two significant figures implied by the context).

---

### **Step 2: Calculate Angular Frequency (ω)**  
For a mass-spring system in SHM, the angular frequency is:  
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$  
Where:  
- $ k = 250 \, \text{N/m} $,  
- $ m = 0.40 \, \text{kg} $.  

Substitute values:  
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250}{0.40}} = \sqrt{625} = 25 \, \text{rad/s}
$$  

**Physical Law Check**:  
- The formula assumes **no damping** (frictionless surface) and **ideal spring behavior** (Hooke’s Law holds). The problem states the surface is frictionless and the spring is ideal, so no physical laws are violated.

**Significant Figures**:  
- Input values (250 N/m, 0.40 kg) have **two significant figures**, so $ \omega = 25 \, \text{rad/s} $ is valid.

---

### **Step 3: Calculate Frequency (f)**  
The frequency is related to angular frequency by:  
$$
f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$  
Substitute $ \omega = 25 \, \text{rad/s} $:  
$$
f = \frac{25}{2\pi} \approx \frac{25}{6.283} \approx 3.98 \, \text{Hz}
$$  

**Significant Figures**:  
- Rounded to **two significant figures**, $ f = 4.0 \, \text{Hz} $.

**Physical Law Check**:  
- Frequency depends only on $ k $ and $ m $, not on amplitude (0.040 m). The amplitude is irrelevant for frequency calculations in SHM, so no issues here.

- The **amplitude of oscillation (0.040 m)** is larger than the initial displacement (0.020 m) used to determine $ k $. However, since the spring is ideal and within its elastic limit, this is acceptable.  
- The spring does not violate Hooke’s Law because the displacement during oscillation (0.040 m) corresponds to a force of $ F = kx = 250 \times 0.040 = 10 \, \text{N} $, which is still consistent with the spring’s behavior.  
- **No physical laws are broken** in this problem. All assumptions (ideal spring, frictionless surface) are explicitly satisfied by the problem description.

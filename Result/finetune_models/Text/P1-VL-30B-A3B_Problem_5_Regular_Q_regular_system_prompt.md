To determine the final velocity of block A after the collision, we apply the **principle of conservation of linear momentum**. Since the track is frictionless, there are no external forces acting on the system (blocks A and B), and thus the total momentum before the collision must equal the total momentum after the collision.

---

### **Given:**

- **Block A (mₐ):**  
  - Mass = 100 nanograms = $100 \times 10^{-12}$ kg = $1.0 \times 10^{-10}$ kg  
  - Initial velocity ($v_{a1}$) = +1.0 m/s (to the right)

- **Block B (mₙ):**  
  - Mass = 200 nanograms = $2.0 \times 10^{-10}$ kg  
  - Initial velocity ($v_{b1}$) = -2.0 m/s (to the left)

- **After collision:**  
  - Final velocity of B ($v_{b2}$) = +1.0 m/s (to the right)  
  - Final velocity of A ($v_{a2}$) = ? (to be calculated)

---

### **Step 1: Apply Conservation of Momentum**

$$
m_a v_{a1} + m_b v_{b1} = m_a v_{a2} + m_b v_{b2}
$$

Substitute the known values:

$$
(1.0 \times 10^{-10})(1.0) + (2.0 \times 10^{-10})(-2.0) = (1.0 \times 10^{-10})v_{a2} + (2.0 \times 10^{-10})(1.0)
$$

$$
1.0 \times 10^{-10} - 4.0 \times 10^{-10} = 1.0 \times 10^{-10} v_{a2} + 2.0 \times 10^{-10}
$$

$$
-3.0 \times 10^{-10} = 1.0 \times 10^{-10} v_{a2} + 2.0 \times 10^{-10}
$$

$$
-5.0 \times 10^{-10} = 1.0 \times 10^{-10} v_{a2}
$$

$$
v_{a2} = -5.0 \text{ m/s}
$$

---

### **Interpretation of Result:**

- A **negative velocity** indicates motion in the **opposite direction** of the positive axis (i.e., **to the left**).
- Block A was initially moving to the right at 1.0 m/s. After the collision, it moves **to the left at 5.0 m/s**, which is a significant change in direction and speed.

---

### **Conclusion:**

The final velocity of block A after the collision is:

$$
\boxed{-5.0} \text{ m/s}
$$

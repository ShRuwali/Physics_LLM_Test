To determine the final velocity of block A after the collision, we apply the **principle of conservation of momentum**. This principle holds because the system is isolated (no external forces), and the track is frictionless.

### **Step 1: Understand the Scenario and Assign Directions**

- **Block A**:
  - Mass: $ m_a = 100 \, \text{ng} = 1 \times 10^{-10} \, \text{kg} $
  - Initial velocity: $ v_{a1} = 0 \, \text{m/s} $ (at rest)
  - Final velocity: $ v_{a2} = ? $

- **Block B**:
  - Mass: $ m_b = 200 \, \text{ng} = 2 \times 10^{-10} \, \text{kg} $
  - Initial velocity: $ v_{b1} = -100 \, \text{m/s} $ (moving **left**, so negative in a right-positive coordinate system)
  - Final velocity: $ v_{b2} = +10,000 \, \text{m/s} $ (moving **right**, positive)

> **Note**: The problem states Block B is moving **to the right**, but for a **collision** to occur with Block A (which is on the left), Block B must be moving **left**. Thus, we interpret the given velocity as $ -100 \, \text{m/s} $.

---

### **Step 2: Apply Conservation of Momentum**

$$
m_a v_{a1} + m_b v_{b1} = m_a v_{a2} + m_b v_{b2}
$$

Substitute known values:

$$
(1 \times 10^{-10})(0) + (2 \times 10^{-10})(-100) = (1 \times 10^{-10})v_{a2} + (2 \times 10^{-10})(10,000)
$$

Simplify:

$$
-2 \times 10^{-8} = 1 \times 10^{-10} v_{a2} + 2 \times 10^{-6}
$$

Solve for $ v_{a2} $:

$$
1 \times 10^{-10} v_{a2} = -2 \times 10^{-8} - 2 \times 10^{-6} = -2.02 \times 10^{-6}
$$

$$
v_{a2} = \frac{-2.02 \times 10^{-6}}{1 \times 10^{-10}} = -2.02 \times 10^4 \, \text{m/s}
$$

$$
v_{a2} = -20,200 \, \text{m/s}
$$

---

### **Step 3: Interpret the Result**

- The **negative sign** indicates that Block A moves **to the left** after the collision.
- The magnitude of the velocity is **20,200 m/s**, which is **20.2 km/s**.
- This result is **physically consistent** with momentum conservation:
  - Block B gains a large amount of momentum in the **positive (right) direction**.
  - Block A must gain an equal and opposite amount of momentum in the **negative (left) direction**.

> **Note**: The kinetic energy increases after the collision, which is **not typical** for an elastic collision but is **allowed** in inelastic collisions where internal energy (e.g., from a spring or explosion) is involved.

To determine the tension in the cable holding a block of mass $ m = 20.0 \, \text{kg} $ and density $ D = 19.0 \times 10^3 \, \text{kg/m}^3 $ submerged in sea water of density $ d = 1.03 \times 10^3 \, \text{kg/m}^3 $, we apply **Archimedes’ Principle** and **force equilibrium**.

---

### **Step 1: Understand the Forces Acting on the Block**

When the block is submerged in fluid and at rest, three forces act on it:

- **Weight (downward)**:  
  $$
  W = m \cdot g = 20.0 \, \text{kg} \times 10.0 \, \text{m/s}^2 = 200.0 \, \text{N}
  $$

- **Buoyant Force (upward)**:  
  By Archimedes’ Principle, this equals the weight of the displaced fluid:
  $$
  F_b = d \cdot V \cdot g
  $$
  where $ V $ is the volume of the block (which equals the volume of displaced fluid, since the block is fully immersed).

- **Tension in the Cable (upward)**:  
  This balances the net downward force due to the difference between the block's weight and the buoyant force.

Since the block is at rest, the net force is zero:
$$
T + F_b = W \Rightarrow T = W - F_b
$$

---

### **Step 2: Calculate the Volume of the Block**

Using the definition of density:
$$
V = \frac{m}{D} = \frac{20.0}{19.0 \times 10^3} = 1.05263 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{m}^3
$$

---

### **Step 3: Compute the Buoyant Force**

$$
F_b = d \cdot V \cdot g = (1.03 \times 10^3) \cdot (1.05263 \times 10^{-3}) \cdot 10.0 = 10.842 \, \text{N}
$$

---

### **Step 4: Determine the Tension in the Cable**

$$
T = W - F_b = 200.0 \, \text{N} - 10.842 \, \text{N} = 189.158 \, \text{N}
$$

---

### **Step 5: Consider Significant Figures**

All given values have **three significant figures**:
- Mass: $ 20.0 \, \text{kg} $
- Density of block: $ 19.0 \times 10^3 \, \text{kg/m}^3 $
- Density of sea water: $ 1.03 \times 10^3 \, \text{kg/m}^3 $
- Gravity: $ 10.0 \, \text{m/s}^2 $

Thus, the final result should also be reported to **three significant figures**.

$$
T = \boxed{189} \, \text{N}
$$

---

### **Physical Law Check**

- **No physical laws are broken**. The block is denser than sea water, so it would sink if not held by the cable. However, since the block is **at rest**, the tension in the cable provides the necessary upward force to balance the net downward force (weight minus buoyancy), satisfying **Newton’s First Law** (equilibrium).

---

### **Final Answer**

$$
\boxed{189} \, \text{N}
$$

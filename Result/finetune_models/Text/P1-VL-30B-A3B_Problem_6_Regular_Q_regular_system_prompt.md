To solve the problem, we begin by analyzing the given data and applying the relevant physics principles.

---

### **1. Force Constant of the Spring**

The spring is stretched by a force $ F = 5.0 \, \text{N} $, resulting in a displacement $ x = 0.020 \, \text{m} $. According to **Hooke’s Law**:

$$
F = kx \quad \Rightarrow \quad k = \frac{F}{x}
$$

Substituting the known values:

$$
k = \frac{5.0}{0.020} = 250 \, \text{N/m}
$$

This gives us the **force constant** of the spring.

---

### **2. Angular Frequency of Oscillation**

For a mass-spring system, the **angular frequency** $ \omega $ is given by:

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

We are given:
- $ k = 250 \, \text{N/m} $
- Mass $ m = 500 \, \text{nanograms} = 5.0 \times 10^{-10} \, \text{kg} $

Now calculate:

$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250}{5.0 \times 10^{-10}}} = \sqrt{5.0 \times 10^{11}} \approx 7.07 \times 10^5 \, \text{rad/s}
$$

Rounded to two significant figures (based on the precision of the input data):

$$
\omega \approx 7.1 \times 10^5 \, \text{rad/s}
$$

---

### **3. Frequency of Oscillation**

The **frequency** $ f $ is related to angular frequency by:

$$
f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

Using the value of $ \omega $:

$$
f = \frac{7.1 \times 10^5}{2\pi} \approx \frac{7.1 \times 10^5}{6.283} \approx 1.13 \times 10^5 \, \text{Hz}
$$

Rounded to two significant figures:

$$
f \approx 1.1 \times 10^5 \, \text{Hz}
$$

---

  $$
  \boxed{250} \, \text{N/m}
  $$

  $$
  \boxed{7.1 \times 10^5} \, \text{rad/s}

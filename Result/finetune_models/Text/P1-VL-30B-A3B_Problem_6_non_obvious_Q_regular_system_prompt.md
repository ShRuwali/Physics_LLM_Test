To solve the problem, we begin by analyzing the information provided and identifying the correct approach to determine the **force constant (k)** of the spring, followed by the **angular frequency (ω)** and **frequency (f)** of the oscillation.

---

### **Step 1: Interpreting the Spring Balance Data**

The problem states:

> "To find out the force constant, a spring balance is used and a force of 0 N creates a displacement (x) of 20 meters."

This is **inconsistent with Hooke’s Law**, which states that the force $ F $ exerted by a spring is proportional to its displacement from equilibrium:  
$$
F = kx
$$
If $ F = 0 $, then $ x = 0 $, not 20 meters. This is a **clear contradiction**.

However, since the problem is structured to ask for a **force constant**, and the spring is ideal, we must assume that **the statement contains a typo**, and the **intended meaning is** that a **non-zero force** of **20 N** causes a **displacement of 20 meters**.

Thus, we proceed with:
$$
F = 20 \text{ N}, \quad x = 20 \text{ m}
$$

Using Hooke’s Law:
$$
k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{20}{20} = 1 \text{ N/m}
$$

---

### **Step 2: Determining Angular Frequency (ω)**

The angular frequency $ \omega $ of a mass-spring system is given by:
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

We are given:
$$
m = 500 \text{ nanograms} = 5 \times 10^{-10} \text{ kg}
$$

Substitute values:
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{1}{5 \times 10^{-10}}} = \sqrt{2 \times 10^9} \approx 4.47 \times 10^4 \text{ rad/s}
$$

---

### **Step 3: Calculating Frequency (f)**

The frequency $ f $ is related to angular frequency by:
$$
f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

$$
f = \frac{4.47 \times 10^4}{2\pi} \approx 7.1 \times 10^3 \text{ Hz}
$$

---

### **Final Results**

- **Force constant (k)** of the spring:  
  $$
  \boxed{1} \text{ N/m}
  $$

- **Angular frequency (ω)** of the oscillation:  
  $$
  \boxed{4.47 \times 10^4} \text{ rad/s}
  $$

- **Frequency (f)** of the oscillation:  
  $$
  \boxed{7.1 \times 10^3} \text{ Hz}
  $$

---

- This assumption allows us to compute the force constant $ k $, which is essential for determining the oscillation parameters.

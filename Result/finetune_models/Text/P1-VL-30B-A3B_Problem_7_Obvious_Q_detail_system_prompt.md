To determine the gravitational force between Planet A and its moon at the closest point in the moon’s elliptical orbit, we apply **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation**, which is given by the formula:

$$
F = G \cdot \frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}
$$

Where:
- $ F $ is the gravitational force,
- $ G $ is the gravitational constant ($6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N} \cdot \text{m}^2 / \text{kg}^2$),
- $ M $ is the mass of Planet A ($6.0 \times 10^{24} \, \text{kg}$),
- $ m $ is the mass of the moon ($2.0 \times 10^{22} \, \text{kg}$),
- $ d $ is the center-to-center distance between the two bodies at the closest approach ($3.0 \times 10^9 \, \text{m}$).

---

### Step-by-Step Calculation

1. **Compute the product of masses:**

$$
M \cdot m = (6.0 \times 10^{24}) \cdot (2.0 \times 10^{22}) = 1.2 \times 10^{47} \, \text{kg}^2
$$

2. **Compute the square of the distance:**

$$
d^2 = (3.0 \times 10^9)^2 = 9.0 \times 10^{18} \, \text{m}^2
$$

3. **Apply Newton’s Law:**

$$
F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot \left( \frac{1.2 \times 10^{47}}{9.0 \times 10^{18}} \right)
= (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot (1.333 \times 10^{28})
$$

$$
F = 8.898 \times 10^{17} \, \text{N}
$$

4. **Round to appropriate significant figures:**

All given values ($M$, $m$, and $d$) have **two significant figures**, so the final result should also be reported with **two significant figures**.

$$
F \approx \boxed{8.9 \times 10^{17}} \, \text{N}
$$

---

### Physical Law Considerations

- **Gravitational Law Validity**: Since both bodies are **spherically symmetric**, the gravitational force can be calculated using the center-to-center distance. The formula is valid at this distance because the moon is **well outside** the planet (the planet’s radius is $6.0 \times 10^6 \, \text{m}$, while the center-to-center distance is $3.0 \times 10^9 \, \text{m}$).
  
- **Orbital Stability**: While the problem states the moon is in an **elliptical orbit**, this does **not** affect the gravitational force at the **specific point** of closest approach. The formula applies **at any moment** in the orbit, as long as we use the **instantaneous distance** between centers.

- **Collision Avoidance**: The **minimum possible distance** between the surfaces of the two bodies is greater than zero (since $d > R_{\text{planet}} + R_{\text{moon}}$), so there is **no physical violation** of orbital dynamics or collision.

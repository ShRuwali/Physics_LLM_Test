To determine the gravitational force between Planet A and its moon at the closest distance between their centers, we apply **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation**, which states:

$$
F = G \cdot \frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}
$$

Where:
- $ F $ is the gravitational force (in newtons, N),
- $ G $ is the gravitational constant ($6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2$),
- $ M $ is the mass of Planet A ($6.0 \times 10^{24} \, \text{kg}$),
- $ m $ is the mass of the moon ($2.0 \times 10^{22} \, \text{kg}$),
- $ d $ is the distance between the centers of the two bodies ($3.0 \times 10^9 \, \text{m}$).

---

### Step-by-step Calculation:

1. **Compute the numerator**:
   $$
   G \cdot M \cdot m = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot (6.0 \times 10^{24}) \cdot (2.0 \times 10^{22})
   $$

   First, multiply $6.674 \times 10^{-11}$ by $6.0 \times 10^{24}$:
   $$
   6.674 \times 6.0 = 40.044 \quad \text{and} \quad 10^{-11} \cdot 10^{24} = 10^{13}
   $$
   So:
   $$
   6.674 \times 10^{-11} \cdot 6.0 \times 10^{24} = 4.0044 \times 10^{14}
   $$

   Then multiply by $2.0 \times 10^{22}$:
   $$
   4.0044 \times 10^{14} \cdot 2.0 \times 10^{22} = 8.0088 \times 10^{36}
   $$

2. **Compute the denominator**:
   $$
   d^2 = (3.0 \times 10^9)^2 = 9.0 \times 10^{18}
   $$

3. **Divide to find the force**:
   $$
   F = \frac{8.0088 \times 10^{36}}{9.0 \times 10^{18}} = 0.889866... \times 10^{18} = 8.89866... \times 10^{17}
   $$

4. **Round to appropriate significant figures**:
   - All given values ($M$, $m$, and $d$) have **two significant figures**.
   - Therefore, the final answer should also be expressed with **two significant figures**.

   $$
   F \approx 8.9 \times 10^{17} \, \text{N}
   $$

---

### Final Answer:

$$
\boxed{8.9 \times 10^{17}} \text{ newtons}
$$

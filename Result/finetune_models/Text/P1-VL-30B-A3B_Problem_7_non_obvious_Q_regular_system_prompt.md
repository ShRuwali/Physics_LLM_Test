To determine the tension in the cable holding the block at rest in seawater, we must consider the forces acting on the block: **weight**, **buoyant force**, and **tension**. Since the block is at rest, the net force acting on it must be **zero**.

---

### **Step 1: Understand the Given Data**

- **Mass of the block (m)** = 1000 nanograms = $1000 \times 10^{-12} \, \text{kg} = 1.0 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{kg}$
- **Dimensions of the block**: length = width = height = 100 nm  
  → Volume $V = (100 \times 10^{-9})^3 = 1.0 \times 10^{-21} \, \text{m}^3$
- **Density of the block (D)** = $1.0 \times 10^{12} \, \text{kg/m}^3$
- **Density of seawater (d)** = $1.03 \times 10^3 \, \text{kg/m}^3$ (noted: the negative sign in the problem is a **typo**)
- **Acceleration due to gravity (g)** = $10 \, \text{m/s}^2$ (assumed downward, as standard; upward is a **typo** in the problem)

---

### **Step 2: Compute the Weight of the Block**

$$
\text{Weight} = m \cdot g = (1.0 \times 10^{-9}) \cdot 10 = 1.0 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{N}
$$

This is the **downward** force due to gravity.

---

### **Step 3: Compute the Buoyant Force**

The buoyant force is equal to the **weight of the displaced fluid**:

$$
F_{\text{buoyant}} = d \cdot V \cdot g = (1.03 \times 10^3) \cdot (1.0 \times 10^{-21}) \cdot 10 = 1.03 \times 10^{-17} \, \text{N}
$$

This is the **upward** force due to the displaced seawater.

---

### **Step 4: Apply Equilibrium Condition**

Since the block is **at rest**, the net force is zero:

$$
T + F_{\text{buoyant}} - \text{Weight} = 0
$$

Solving for tension $T$:

$$
T = \text{Weight} - F_{\text{buoyant}} = (1.0 \times 10^{-8}) - (1.03 \times 10^{-17}) \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{N}
$$

Note: The buoyant force is **negligible** compared to the weight due to the **extreme density difference** between the block and seawater.

---

### **Step 5: Interpretation of Direction**

- The **weight** acts **downward**
- The **buoyant force** acts **upward**
- Since the block is **heavier than seawater**, the **tension in the cable** must act **downward** to prevent the block from rising (in a standard gravity setup).
- However, **tension is a scalar quantity** in most physics problems unless direction is explicitly required. Given that the block is **denser than seawater**, and it's **immersed and held at rest**, the **magnitude of the tension is equal to the difference between weight and buoyant force**.

---

### **Final Answer**

$$
\boxed{1.0 \times 10^{-8}} \text{ Newtons}
$$

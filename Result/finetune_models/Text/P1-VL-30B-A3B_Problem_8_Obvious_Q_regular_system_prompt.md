To determine the gravitational force between Planet A and its moon, we apply **Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation**, which is given by the formula:

$$
F = G \cdot \frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}
$$

Where:
- $ F $ is the gravitational force,
- $ G $ is the gravitational constant ($6.67430 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{m}^3 \text{kg}^{-1} \text{s}^{-2}$),
- $ M $ is the mass of Planet A,
- $ m $ is the mass of the moon,
- $ d $ is the distance between their centers.

---

### **Step 1: Convert All Units to SI**

- **Planet A's mass (M):**  
  Given: $ 6000 \, \text{nanograms} $  
  Since $ 1 \, \text{nanogram} = 10^{-12} \, \text{kg} $,  
  $$
  M = 6000 \times 10^{-12} = 6.0 \times 10^{-9} \, \text{kg}
  $$

- **Moon’s mass (m):**  
  Given as $ 2.0 \times 10^{22} \, \text{kg} $ (already in SI units).

- **Distance (d):**  
  Given as $ 3.0 \times 10^9 \, \text{m} $ (already in SI units).

---

### **Step 2: Plug Values into the Formula**

$$
F = (6.67430 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot \frac{(6.0 \times 10^{-9}) \cdot (2.0 \times 10^{22})}{(3.0 \times 10^9)^2}
$$

#### **Numerator Calculation:**
$$
6.67430 \times 10^{-11} \cdot 6.0 \times 10^{-9} \cdot 2.0 \times 10^{22}
= 8.00916 \times 10^3
$$

#### **Denominator Calculation:**
$$
(3.0 \times 10^9)^2 = 9.0 \times 10^{18}
$$

#### **Final Force Calculation:**
$$
F = \frac{8.00916 \times 10^3}{9.0 \times 10^{18}} = 8.90 \times 10^{-16} \, \text{N}
$$

---

### **Step 3: Consider Significant Figures**

- Planet A's mass: $6000$ nanograms (interpreted as $6.0 \times 10^3$) → **2 significant figures**
- Moon's mass: $2.0 \times 10^{22}$ kg → **2 significant figures**
- Distance: $3.0 \times 10^9$ m → **2 significant figures**

Thus, the final result should be rounded to **2 significant figures**.

$$
F = \boxed{8.9 \times 10^{-16}} \, \text{N}
$$

---

### **Key Notes**

- The **shape of the orbit** (square path) is **not relevant** for calculating the gravitational force at a specific distance. We only need the **closest distance between centers**, which is given.
- The **spherical symmetry** of both bodies allows us to treat them as point masses at their centers.
- The **extremely small mass of Planet A** (only $6.0 \times 10^{-9}$ kg) results in a **very small gravitational force**, even when the moon's mass is large and the distance is substantial.

---

### **Final Answer**

$$
\boxed{8.9 \times 10^{-16}} \, \text{N}
$$

### Description of the Figure

The figure shows a block of mass $m = 1\text{ nanogram}$ (with dimensions $1\text{ nm} \times 1\text{ nm} \times 1\text{ nm}$) placed on a rough inclined plane angled at $\theta = 30^\circ$ to the horizontal. The block is initially at a distance of $s = 5.0\text{ meters}$ up along the slope from the bottom. It accelerates down the incline under the combined effect of gravity ($g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$) and kinetic friction ($\mu_k = 0.50$).

---

### Step-by-Step Calculation of Total Time

#### 1. Analyze the Forces Acting along the Incline
The component of gravitational force acting down the slope is:
$$F_{\text{gravity}} = m g \sin\theta$$

The normal force acting perpendicular to the inclined plane is:
$$N = m g \cos\theta$$

The kinetic friction force opposing the motion down the slope is:
$$f_k = \mu_k N = \mu_k m g \cos\theta$$

#### 2. Calculate the Net Acceleration
Applying Newton's Second Law along the incline:
$$F_{\text{net}} = m a = m g \sin\theta - \mu_k m g \cos\theta$$

Dividing by mass $m$, the acceleration $a$ is:
$$a = g (\sin\theta - \mu_k \cos\theta)$$

Substitute the given values ($\theta = 30^\circ$, $\mu_k = 0.50$, $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$):
$$a = 10.0 \times \left(\sin 30^\circ - 0.50 \cos 30^\circ\right)$$

$$a = 10.0 \times \left(\frac{1}{2} - 0.50 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$$

$$a = 10.0 \times \left(\frac{2 - \sqrt{3}}{4}\right) = \frac{5(2 - \sqrt{3})}{2}\text{ m/s}^2 \approx 0.670\text{ m/s}^2$$

#### 3. Calculate the Time Taken to Travel 5.0 Meters
Using the kinematic equation for motion starting from rest ($u = 0$):
$$s = \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$

Solving for time $t$:
$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}$$

Substituting $s = 5.0\text{ m}$ and $a = \frac{5(2 - \sqrt{3})}{2}\text{ m/s}^2$:
$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 5.0}{\frac{5(2 - \sqrt{3})}{2}}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{2 - \sqrt{3}}}$$

Simplifying the radical expression:
$$t = \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}\text{ seconds} \approx 3.86\text{ seconds}$$

---

### Final Answer
The total time taken by the block to travel $5.0\text{ meters}$ is **$\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}\text{ seconds} \approx 3.86\text{ seconds}$**.

**Description**

The figure shows a block of mass $m = 12.0\text{ kg}$ placed on an inclined plane with an angle of inclination $\theta = 30^\circ$. The block is released from rest at a position $5.0\text{ meters}$ up the incline and slides down along the plane. A kinetic friction force opposes its downward motion. 

The given values are:
- Mass of the block, $m = 12.0\text{ kg}$
- Distance to travel down the incline, $s = 5.0\text{ m}$
- Angle of inclination, $\theta = 30^\circ$
- Coefficient of kinetic friction, $\mu_k = 0.50$
- Acceleration due to gravity, $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$
- Initial velocity, $u = 0\text{ m/s}$

---

**Step 1: Calculate the acceleration of the block**

The normal force acting perpendicular to the incline is:

$$N = m g \cos\theta$$

The force of kinetic friction acting up the incline is:

$$f_k = \mu_k N = \mu_k m g \cos\theta$$

The component of gravity acting down the incline is:

$$F_g = m g \sin\theta$$

The net force acting on the block down the incline is:

$$F_{\text{net}} = m g \sin\theta - \mu_k m g \cos\theta$$

Applying Newton's second law ($F_{\text{net}} = m a$), the acceleration $a$ down the incline is:

$$a = g (\sin\theta - \mu_k \cos\theta)$$

Substitute the given values into the equation:

$$\sin(30^\circ) = 0.5$$

$$\cos(30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.8660$$

$$a = 10.0 \times \left(0.5 - 0.50 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$$

$$a = 10.0 \times (0.5 - 0.4330) = 10.0 \times 0.0670 = 0.670\text{ m/s}^2$$

---

**Step 2: Calculate the total time taken**

Using the kinematic equation for displacement from rest ($u = 0$):

$$s = \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$

Solving for time $t$:

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}$$

Substitute $s = 5.0\text{ m}$ and $a = 0.670\text{ m/s}^2$:

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 5.0}{0.670}} = \sqrt{\frac{10.0}{0.670}} \approx \sqrt{14.925} \approx 3.86\text{ s}$$

---

**Final Answer**

The total time taken by the block to travel $5.0\text{ meters}$ down the incline is approximately $3.86\text{ seconds}$.

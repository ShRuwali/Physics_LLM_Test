### Description of the System

The figure illustrates a system where a small spherical mass is attached to a light string fixed at a lower pivot point. The mass moves along a vertical circular path. 

The given values from the figure are:
- **Mass ($m$):** $10\text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 10^{-11}\text{ kg}$
- **Radius of the mass:** $100\text{ nanometers} = 10^{-7}\text{ m}$ (negligible compared to the string length)
- **Length of the string ($R$):** $5.0\text{ m}$ (effective radius of the circular path)
- **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$
- **Speed of the mass ($v$):** $5.0\text{ m/s}$

---

### Calculation of Tension at the Bottom of the Circle

#### Case 1: Uniform Circular Motion (Constant Speed $v = 5.0\text{ m/s}$)

If the mass rotates at a constant speed of $v = 5.0\text{ m/s}$ throughout the vertical path, the forces acting on the mass at the bottom position are:
1. Downward gravitational force: $F_g = mg$
2. Upward tension force: $T_{\text{bottom}}$

The net force directed toward the center of the circle provides the necessary centripetal acceleration:

$$T_{\text{bottom}} - mg = \frac{m v^2}{R}$$

Rearranging for the tension $T_{\text{bottom}}$:

$$T_{\text{bottom}} = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

Substituting the numerical values:

$$T_{\text{bottom}} = 10^{-11}\text{ kg} \times \left( 10.0\text{ m/s}^2 + \frac{(5.0\text{ m/s})^2}{5.0\text{ m}} \right)$$

$$T_{\text{bottom}} = 10^{-11} \times (10.0 + 5.0) = 1.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}$$

---

#### Case 2: Free Motion under Gravity (Speed at Top $v_{\text{top}} = 5.0\text{ m/s}$)

If the specified speed of $5.0\text{ m/s}$ is measured at the top position (where the mass is depicted) and the object swings down freely under gravity, we calculate the speed at the bottom ($v_{\text{bottom}}$) using the conservation of mechanical energy:

$$\frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{bottom}}^2 = \frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{top}}^2 + m g (2R)$$

$$v_{\text{bottom}}^2 = v_{\text{top}}^2 + 4 g R$$

$$v_{\text{bottom}}^2 = (5.0)^2 + 4(10.0)(5.0) = 25 + 200 = 225\text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2$$

The tension at the bottom position is then given by:

$$T_{\text{bottom}} = m \left( g + \frac{v_{\text{bottom}}^2}{R} \right)$$

$$T_{\text{bottom}} = 10^{-11}\text{ kg} \times \left( 10.0 + \frac{225}{5.0} \right)$$

$$T_{\text{bottom}} = 10^{-11} \times (10.0 + 45.0) = 5.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}$$

---

### Final Result

- **Assuming constant speed:** $T_{\text{bottom}} = 1.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}$ (or $0.15\text{ nN}$)
- **Assuming motion under gravity with speed given at the top:** $T_{\text{bottom}} = 5.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}$ (or $0.55\text{ nN}$)

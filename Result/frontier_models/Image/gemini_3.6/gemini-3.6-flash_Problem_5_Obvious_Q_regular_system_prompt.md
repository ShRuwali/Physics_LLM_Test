### Brief Description

The figure illustrates a one-dimensional collision on a frictionless horizontal surface between two cubical blocks of nanoscale dimensions:

- **Block A**: Has a mass of $m_a = 100\text{ ng}$ and is initially at rest ($v_{a1} = 0\text{ m/s}$).
- **Block B**: Has a mass of $m_b = 200\text{ ng}$ and an initial speed of $v_{b1} = 100\text{ m/s}$. 
- **After Collision**: Block B rebounds to the right at $v_{b2} = 10\text{ km/s} = 10,000\text{ m/s}$. Block A moves to the left with a final velocity $v_{a2}$.

---

### Calculation of Velocity $v_{a2}$

We define the coordinate system such that the **right direction is positive (+)** and the **left direction is negative (-)**.

#### Given Data:
- $m_a = 100\text{ ng}$
- $m_b = 200\text{ ng}$
- $v_{a1} = 0\text{ m/s}$
- $v_{b2} = +10,000\text{ m/s}$ (directed to the right)

#### Initial Velocity of Block B ($v_{b1}$):
- **Based on the directional arrow ($\leftarrow$)**: For Block B to collide with stationary Block A located to its left, Block B must move left towards Block A. Thus, $v_{b1} = -100\text{ m/s}$.

---

#### 1. Conservation of Linear Momentum Equation:

$$P_{\text{initial}} = P_{\text{final}}$$

$$m_a v_{a1} + m_b v_{b1} = m_a v_{a2} + m_b v_{b2}$$

#### 2. Substituting the Given Values:

$$(100 \times 0) + (200 \times -100) = 100 \cdot v_{a2} + (200 \times 10,000)$$

$$-20,000 = 100 \cdot v_{a2} + 2,000,000$$

#### 3. Solving for $v_{a2}$:

$$100 \cdot v_{a2} = -20,000 - 2,000,000$$

$$100 \cdot v_{a2} = -2,020,000$$

$$v_{a2} = \frac{-2,020,000}{100} = -20,200\text{ m/s} = -20.2\text{ km/s}$$

---

*(Note: If the text description "moving to the right" is taken literally despite the arrow and collision geometry, $v_{b1} = +100\text{ m/s}$, yielding $v_{a2} = -19,800\text{ m/s} = -19.8\text{ km/s}$.)*

### Final Answer:
The velocity of Block A after the collision is **$20.2\text{ km/s}$ to the left** (or **$-20.2\text{ km/s}$** / **$-20,200\text{ m/s}$**).

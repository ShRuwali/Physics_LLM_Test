### Brief Description of the Figures

* **Fig (a):** Demonstrates the stretching of a horizontal spring fixed at the left end. A force $F = 6\text{ N}$ is applied to the right using a spring balance, causing the spring to stretch from an initial displacement of $X = 0\text{ meters}$ to a final displacement of $X = 20\text{ meters}$.
* **Fig (b):** Shows the horizontal spring attached to a block of mass $m = 500\text{ nanograms}$ resting on a frictionless surface. The block has dimensions $\text{length} = \text{width} = \text{height} = 400\text{ nanometers}$ and is pulled to a displacement of $X = 0.040\text{ meters}$ before oscillating.

---

### 1. Calculation of the Force Constant ($k$) of the Spring

According to Hooke's Law, the force required to stretch a spring is directly proportional to its displacement:

$$F = k \cdot \Delta x$$

From Fig (a):
* Applied force, $F = 6\text{ N}$
* Change in displacement, $\Delta x = 20\text{ m} - 0\text{ m} = 20\text{ m}$

Rearranging the formula to solve for the spring force constant $k$:

$$k = \frac{F}{\Delta x}$$

$$k = \frac{6\text{ N}}{20\text{ m}} = 0.3\text{ N/m}$$

---

### 2. Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)

First, convert the mass of the block from nanograms to kilograms ($\text{kg}$):

$$m = 500\text{ ng} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 500 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$$

#### **Angular Frequency ($\omega$)**

The formula for the angular frequency of a simple harmonic oscillator is:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Substitute the known values:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{0.3\text{ N/m}}{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}}$$

$$\omega = \sqrt{6 \times 10^8\text{ rad}^2/\text{s}^2} \approx 24,494.9\text{ rad/s}$$

#### **Frequency ($f$)**

The oscillation frequency is related to the angular frequency by:

$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

Substitute $\omega \approx 24,494.9\text{ rad/s}$:

$$f = \frac{24,494.9}{2\pi} \approx 3,898.48\text{ Hz}$$

---

### Final Results

* **Force constant of the spring ($k$):** $0.3\text{ N/m}$
* **Angular frequency ($\omega$):** $24,494.9\text{ rad/s}$
* **Frequency ($f$):** $3,898.48\text{ Hz}$

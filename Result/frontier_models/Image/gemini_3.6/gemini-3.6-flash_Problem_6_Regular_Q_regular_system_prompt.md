### Brief Description of the Figures

* **Fig (a):** Demonstrates Hooke's Law experiment where a horizontal spring fixed at one end is stretched by a force scale applying a force $F = 5.0\text{ N}$, causing a displacement of $x = 0.020\text{ m}$ from its equilibrium position ($x = 0\text{ m}$).
* **Fig (b):** Depicts a simple harmonic oscillator consisting of the spring connected to a block of mass $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$ on a frictionless horizontal surface. The block is pulled to a displacement of $x = 0.040\text{ m}$ from equilibrium and released to oscillate.

---

### 1. Calculation of the Force Constant ($k$) of the Spring

According to Hooke's Law, the force exerted by or applied to a spring is proportional to its displacement from the equilibrium position:

$$F = k x$$

Given values from **Fig (a)**:
* Applied Force, $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
* Displacement, $x = 0.020\text{ m}$

Solving for the spring constant $k$:

$$k = \frac{F}{x}$$

$$k = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = 250\text{ N/m}$$

---

### 2. Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$) of Oscillation

The simple harmonic motion of the block-spring system in **Fig (b)** depends on the spring constant $k$ and the mass of the block $m$.

Given values:
* Mass of the block, $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$
* Spring constant, $k = 250\text{ N/m}$

#### **Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**

The formula for the angular frequency of a spring-mass system is:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Substituting the values:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250}{0.40}} = \sqrt{625} = 25\text{ rad/s}$$

#### **Frequency ($f$):**

The relationship between frequency and angular frequency is given by:

$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

Substituting $\omega = 25\text{ rad/s}$:

$$f = \frac{25}{2\pi} \approx 3.98\text{ Hz}$$

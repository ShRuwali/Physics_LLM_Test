### Description of the Figures

* **Fig (a):** A horizontal spring is fixed to a rigid wall at its left end. A force of $F = 5.0\text{ N}$ is applied to the right using a force meter, stretching the spring from its initial equilibrium position $X = 0\text{ m}$ to a final position of $X = 0.020\text{ m}$.
* **Fig (b):** The spring balance is replaced by a cube-shaped block of mass $m = 500\text{ nanograms}$ and dimensions $\text{length} = \text{width} = \text{height} = 400\text{ nanometers}$. The block rests on a frictionless horizontal surface and the spring is stretched to a displacement of $X = 0.040\text{ m}$.

---

### Calculation of Force Constant ($k$)

Using Hooke's Law:

$$F = k \cdot x$$

Where:
* $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
* $x = 0.020\text{ m}$

Solving for the force constant $k$:

$$k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = 250\text{ N/m}$$

---

### Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)

#### 1. Mass Conversion
Convert mass from nanograms ($\text{ng}$) to kilograms ($\text{kg}$):

$$m = 500\text{ ng} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 500 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$$

#### 2. Angular Frequency ($\omega$)

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}} = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{11}\text{ rad}^2/\text{s}^2} \approx 7.07 \times 10^5\text{ rad/s}$$

#### 3. Frequency ($f$)

$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

$$f = \frac{7.07107 \times 10^5\text{ rad/s}}{2\pi} \approx 1.13 \times 10^5\text{ Hz} \quad (112.54\text{ kHz})$$

---

### Physical Evaluation and Limitations

1. **Unreasonable Density:** 
   The volume of the block is:
   $$V = (400 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m})^3 = 6.4 \times 10^{-20}\text{ m}^3$$
   The calculated density of the block is:
   $$\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}{6.4 \times 10^{-20}\text{ m}^3} = 7.8125 \times 10^9\text{ kg/m}^3$$
   This density is nearly 8 million times denser than lead, which exceeds the density of any known stable matter on Earth by several orders of magnitude (approaching degenerate matter densities).

2. **Scale Incompatibility:** 
   Connecting a macroscopic spring ($k = 250\text{ N/m}$) capable of exerting several Newtons of force to a nanometer-sized block ($400\text{ nm}$) is physically impossible. The atomic structure of a nanoscale block would immediately rupture under such forces, and standard macroscopic spring models do not apply at the nanoscale.

3. **Conclusion:** 
   While the mathematical calculations using standard ideal classical mechanics formulas give $\omega \approx 7.07 \times 10^5\text{ rad/s}$ and $f \approx 1.13 \times 10^5\text{ Hz}$, **the system setup is physically unfeasible and not justifiable under real physical conditions.**

### Description of the Figures

* **Fig (a):** Depicts a horizontal spring fixed at one end to a wall and stretched by a hand using a spring balance with an applied force of $F = 5.0\text{ N}$. This force causes an initial displacement of $x = 0.020\text{ m}$ from its equilibrium position ($X = 0\text{ m}$).
* **Fig (b):** Shows the setup where a cubic block of mass $m = 500\text{ nanograms}$ (and dimensions $400\text{ nm} \times 400\text{ nm} \times 400\text{ nm}$) is attached to the end of the spring on a frictionless surface. The block is displaced by $X = 0.040\text{ m}$ and released to undergo simple harmonic motion (SHM).

---

### 1. Force Constant of the Spring (from Fig a)

According to Hooke's Law, the restoring force is proportional to the displacement:

$$F = k \cdot x$$

Where:
* $F = 5.0\text{ N}$ (Applied force)
* $x = 0.020\text{ m} - 0\text{ m} = 0.020\text{ m}$ (Displacement)

Solving for the spring constant $k$:

$$k = \frac{F}{x}$$

$$k = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = 250\text{ N/m}$$

---

### 2. Angular Frequency and Frequency of Oscillation (from Fig b)

#### **Given Data:**
* Mass of the block, $m = 500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$
* Spring constant, $k = 250\text{ N/m}$

---

#### **Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**

The formula for the angular frequency of a spring-mass system is:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Substituting the given values:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}}$$

$$\omega = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{11}}\text{ rad/s} \approx 7.071 \times 10^5\text{ rad/s}$$

---

#### **Frequency ($f$):**

The relation between frequency $f$ and angular frequency $\omega$ is given by:

$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

Substituting the value of $\omega$:

$$f = \frac{7.071 \times 10^5\text{ rad/s}}{2\pi}$$

$$f \approx 1.125 \times 10^5\text{ Hz} \text{ (or } 112.54\text{ kHz)}$$

---

### Summary of Results

* **Spring Constant ($k$):** $250\text{ N/m}$
* **Angular Frequency ($\omega$):** $7.07 \times 10^5\text{ rad/s}$
* **Frequency ($f$):** $1.13 \times 10^5\text{ Hz}$ ($112.54\text{ kHz}$)

### Description

The diagram illustrates a two-body system consisting of a small body labeled **Planet A** and a larger mass labeled **Moon**, separated by a center-to-center distance $d$. 

Both bodies are assumed to be spherically symmetric, which allows us to treat their gravitational interaction using Newton's law of universal gravitation, acting as if all their mass were concentrated at their respective centers.

---

### Given Data

* **Mass of Planet A ($M$):** $6000\text{ nanograms} = 6000 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 6.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ kg}$
* **Mass of Moon ($m$):** $2.0 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg}$
* **Distance between centers ($d$):** $3.0 \times 10^9\text{ meters}$
* **Gravitational Constant ($G$):** $6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$

---

### Calculation of Gravitational Force

According to Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation, the magnitude of the gravitational force $F$ between two spherically symmetric bodies is given by:

$$F = G \frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}$$

Substitute the given values into the equation:

$$F = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2) \times (6.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ kg}) \times (2.0 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg})}{(3.0 \times 10^9\text{ m})^2}$$

Simplify the numerator and denominator:

$$F = \frac{8.0088 \times 10^3\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{9.0 \times 10^{18}\text{ m}^2}$$

$$F \approx 8.90 \times 10^{-16}\text{ N}$$

---

### Conclusion

The magnitude of the gravitational force exerted by each body on the other is approximately **$8.90 \times 10^{-16}\text{ N}$**.

Brief description:
A 12 kg block on a 30° incline with kinetic friction μk = 0.5 slides down the plane. The weight component along the plane tends to pull it down, while kinetic friction (opposite to motion) reduces the net force, giving a small downward acceleration. The problem asks for the time to travel 5 m along the plane, assuming it starts from rest.

Calculations:
- Normal force: N = m g cosθ = 12 × 10 × cos30° ≈ 103.92 N
- Friction: fk = μk N ≈ 0.5 × 103.92 ≈ 51.96 N (up the plane)
- Down-slope gravitational component: Fg∥ = m g sinθ = 12 × 10 × sin30° = 60 N
- Net force along the plane: Fnet = Fg∥ − fk ≈ 60 − 51.96 ≈ 8.04 N
- Acceleration along the plane: a = Fnet / m ≈ 8.04 / 12 ≈ 0.67 m/s² (down the plane)

Time to travel s = 5 m from rest:
- s = (1/2) a t² ⇒ t = sqrt(2s / a) ≈ sqrt(10 / 0.67) ≈ 3.9 s

Answer: approximately 3.9 seconds (assuming the block starts from rest).

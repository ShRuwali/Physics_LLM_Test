Brief description
- A block of mass m ≈ 1 ng on a plane inclined at θ = 30°. The coefficient of kinetic friction is μk = 0.5 and g = 10 m/s².
- The plane length to be traveled is 5 m along the incline, and the block is assumed to be moving down the plane (toward the bottom) due to gravity, with kinetic friction opposing the motion.

Physics and quantities
- Component of gravity along the plane: mg sinθ
- Kinetic friction along the plane: μk N = μk mg cosθ
- Net force along the plane (downward): F = mg sinθ − μk mg cosθ
- Acceleration along the plane (downward): a = g (sinθ − μk cosθ)

Plugging in numbers (θ = 30°, μk = 0.5, g = 10 m/s²)
- sin 30° = 0.5
- cos 30° ≈ 0.866
- μk cosθ = 0.5 × 0.866 ≈ 0.433
- a = 10 × (0.5 − 0.433) ≈ 10 × 0.067 ≈ 0.67 m/s²

Time to travel s = 5 m starting from rest (u = 0)
- s = (1/2) a t² ⇒ t = sqrt(2s / a) = sqrt(10 / 0.67) ≈ sqrt(14.9) ≈ 3.9 s

Result
- The block takes about 3.9 seconds to travel 5 meters down the incline, assuming it starts from rest and continues to slide downward under the given μk.

Notes on validity
- This uses a Coulomb friction model with kinetic friction μk. If the block were at rest initially, motion would require that the static friction μs be less than tanθ; since μs is not given, one cannot guarantee slipping from rest. The calculation above assumes the block is already in motion downward.
- At nanoscale (mass ~1 ng, dimensions ~1 nm), real friction can deviate from the simple macroscopic μk model. The result is valid within the stated idealized model. No fundamental law is broken within that model.

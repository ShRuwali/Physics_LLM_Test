Brief description:
- A small sphere of mass m = 2.5 ng is suspended by a massless string from a fixed support. The center of the sphere is at a distance L = 2 m below the pivot. For small angular displacements, this is an ideal simple pendulum (the sphere’s finite size gives only a negligible correction to the period).

Time period for small oscillations:
- For a simple pendulum of length L in a uniform gravity field g, T = 2π sqrt(L/g).
- Using L = 2 m and g = 10 m/s^2:
  T = 2π sqrt(2/10) = 2π sqrt(0.2) ≈ 2.81 s.

Notes on possible corrections:
- If treated as a physical pendulum, T = 2π sqrt(I_p / (m g d)) with d ≡ L = 2 m and I_p ≈ I_cm + m d^2. For a solid sphere, I_cm = (2/5) m a^2 with a = D/2 = 0.5 nm. This yields I_cm ≈ 2.5×10^-31 kg·m^2, while m d^2 ≈ 1.0×10^-11 kg·m^2, so I_p ≈ m d^2 to within ~10^-20. Thus the period remains essentially T ≈ 2.81 s (difference negligible here).

Assessment of physics:
- No fundamental laws are broken. The problem is solvable with the small-angle approximation. In practice, for such tiny mass and size, air drag and Brownian motion could dominate non-ideal effects, but in the ideal model the result is valid.

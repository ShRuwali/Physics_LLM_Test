Brief description:
- A solid sphere (rigid body) of mass m = 4 ng and diameter 8 nm is placed at the top of an incline of vertical height h = 6×10^8 m. It rolls down the incline without slipping under gravity g = 10 m/s². The problem statement says the sphere starts from rest (even though the image text mentions a large initial velocity, we follow the rest assumption as stated). Air resistance is neglected.

Conservation of energy (rolling without slipping):
- Kinetic energy = translational + rotational: (1/2) m v^2 + (1/2) I ω^2
- Rolling condition: v = ω r
- For a solid sphere: I = (2/5) m r^2

Thus:
m g h = (1/2) m v^2 + (1/2) I (v^2/r^2)
= (1/2) m v^2 [1 + (I/r^2)/m] = (1/2) m v^2 [1 + 2/5] = (7/10) m v^2

Therefore:
v^2 = (10/7) g h
v = sqrt[(10/7) g h]

Numerical value:
- g = 10 m/s², h = 6×10^8 m
- v = sqrt[(10/7) × 10 × 6×10^8]
- v ≈ sqrt(8.571×10^9) ≈ 9.3 × 10^4 m/s

Notes on possible inconsistencies:
- The image text lists an initial velocity of 3.001×10^8 m/s, which contradicts the stated starting-from-rest condition. If the sphere did start with that velocity, the final speed would be different; the rest-start solution above assumes the correct physical condition per the problem statement.
- The result is independent of the mass and radius (for a solid sphere) and relies only on g and h. In a real scenario, such a large height would make air resistance and changes in g with altitude non-negligible, so the simple energy result is an idealized bound.

Description:
- A solid sphere (mass m = 4 ng, diameter D = 8 nm, so radius r = 4 nm) starts from rest at the top of a frictionful inclined plane of height h = 7 m and rolls without slipping down to the bottom. Gravitational acceleration is g = 10 m/s^2. The ball is a rigid body rolling without slipping, so static friction does not dissipate energy.

Energy analysis (conservation of energy):
- Initial potential energy: U_i = m g h
- Final kinetic energy (rolling): K_f = 1/2 m v^2 + 1/2 I ω^2
- For a solid sphere: I = (2/5) m r^2 and rolling without slipping: v = ω r
- Then K_f = 1/2 m v^2 + 1/2 (2/5 m r^2) (v^2 / r^2) = (7/10) m v^2

Equating U_i and K_f:
m g h = (7/10) m v^2  =>  v^2 = (10/7) g h

Plug in g = 10 m/s^2 and h = 7 m:
v^2 = (10/7) × 10 × 7 = 100  =>  v = 10 m/s

Answer:
- The velocity of the sphere at the bottom is 10 m/s (magnitude; direction along the plane, downward). No physical laws are violated; the problem is solvable with energy conservation and the 2/5 moment of inertia for a solid sphere. The diameter/radius cancels out, so only m, g, and h matter for the final speed.

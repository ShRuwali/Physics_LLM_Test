The figure shows a block initially at rest on a $30^\circ$ inclined plane. It slides downward a distance of $5.0\ \text{m}$ with kinetic friction coefficient $\mu_k=0.50$. The mass and dimensions do not affect the acceleration because the mass cancels out.

The net force down the incline is

$$
mg\sin\theta-\mu_k mg\cos\theta=ma
$$

Thus,

$$
a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)
$$

Substituting the given values:

$$
a=10\left(\sin30^\circ-0.50\cos30^\circ\right)
$$

$$
a=10\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)
=\frac{5}{2}(2-\sqrt{3})\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

Assuming the block starts from rest, use

$$
s=\frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

Therefore,

$$
t=\sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}
=\sqrt{\frac{10}{\frac{5}{2}(2-\sqrt{3})}}
$$

$$
t=2\sqrt{2+\sqrt{3}}\ \text{s}
\approx 3.86\ \text{s}
$$

The total time taken is $\boxed{3.86\ \text{s}}$.

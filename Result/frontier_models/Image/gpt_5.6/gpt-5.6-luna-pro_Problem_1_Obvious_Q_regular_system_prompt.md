The figure shows a $1$-nanogram block on a $30^\circ$ inclined plane. It travels $5$ meters down the plane, starting from rest. The coefficient $-0.50$ indicates that kinetic friction acts opposite the motion, so its magnitude is $0.50$.

The acceleration along the incline is

$$
a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)
$$

$$
a=10\left(\sin30^\circ-0.50\cos30^\circ\right)
$$

$$
a=10\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right)
=\frac{5}{2}(2-\sqrt{3})\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

Using $s=\frac{1}{2}at^2$ with $s=5$ meters and initial velocity $u=0$:

$$
5=\frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

$$
t=\sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}
=\sqrt{\frac{10}{\frac{5}{2}(2-\sqrt{3})}}
$$

$$
t=2\sqrt{2+\sqrt{3}}\ \mathrm{s}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{t\approx 3.86\ \mathrm{s}}
$$

The mass and block dimensions do not affect the travel time because the mass cancels from the force equation.

The figure shows a $1$-nanogram block on a $30^\circ$ inclined plane. The block is stated to travel $5.0$ m. Its mass and dimensions do not affect the acceleration because the frictional acceleration is independent of mass.

Assume the block starts from rest and moves down the incline.

Using the given signed value $\mu_k=-0.50$:

$$
a = g(\sin 30^\circ-\mu_k\cos 30^\circ)
$$

$$
a = 10\left(\frac{1}{2}-\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)
$$

$$
a = 5+\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}
= \frac{5(2+\sqrt{3})}{2}\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

For motion from rest:

$$
s = \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

$$
t=\sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}
=\sqrt{\frac{10}{5(2+\sqrt{3})/2}}
$$

$$
t=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2+\sqrt{3}}}
\approx 1.04\ \text{s}
$$

Therefore, the formal calculated time is:

$$
\boxed{t\approx 1.04\ \text{s}}
$$

However, a negative coefficient of kinetic friction is not physically valid. Friction coefficients represent nonnegative magnitudes, and friction must oppose motion. Thus, the result above is only a mathematical consequence of using the stated negative value and is not physically justifiable.

If the intended value was $\mu_k=+0.50$, the physically meaningful result would be:

$$
a=10\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)
=5-\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

$$
t=\sqrt{\frac{10}{5-5\sqrt{3}/2}}
\approx 3.86\ \text{s}
$$

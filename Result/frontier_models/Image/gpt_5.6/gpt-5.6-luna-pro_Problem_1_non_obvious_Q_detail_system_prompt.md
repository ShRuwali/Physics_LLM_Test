The figure shows a block on a $30^\circ$ inclined plane. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k=0.50$, and the block is to travel a distance of $5.0\ \text{m}$ along the plane.

Assuming the block starts from rest and slides down the plane, the net acceleration is

$$
a=g\left(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta\right)
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
a=10\left(\sin30^\circ-0.50\cos30^\circ\right)
$$

$$
a=10\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right)
=\frac{5}{2}\left(2-\sqrt{3}\right)\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

Using

$$
s=\frac{1}{2}at^2
$$

with $s=5.0\ \text{m}$,

$$
5=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{5}{2}(2-\sqrt{3})\right]t^2
$$

Therefore,

$$
t=\sqrt{\frac{10}{\frac{5}{2}(2-\sqrt{3})}}
=2\sqrt{2+\sqrt{3}}\ \text{s}
$$

$$
t\approx 3.86\ \text{s}
$$

Thus, the total time is

$$
\boxed{t=2\sqrt{2+\sqrt{3}}\ \text{s}\approx 3.86\ \text{s}}
$$

The mass and block dimensions cancel out of the acceleration calculation.

If the arrow in the figure indicates that the block is moving up the incline, an initial speed must be provided. Without that speed, the time cannot be uniquely determined. Also, at nanometer dimensions, using a constant macroscopic friction coefficient is an idealization, although the calculation is mathematically consistent.

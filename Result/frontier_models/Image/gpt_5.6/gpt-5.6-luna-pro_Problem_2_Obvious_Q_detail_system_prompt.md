## Description

The figure shows a spherical bob of mass $m=2.5$ nanograms suspended by a string of negligible mass. The distance from the point of support to the sphere’s center of mass is

$$
L=50\ \mathrm{nm}=5.0\times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

For small angular displacements, the system behaves as a simple pendulum.

## Time period for small oscillations

For a simple pendulum,

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}.
$$

Substituting $L=5.0\times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{m}$ and $g=10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$,

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{5.0\times 10^{-8}}{10}}
$$

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{5.0\times 10^{-9}}
\approx 4.4\times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{T\approx 4.4\times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s}=0.44\ \mathrm{ms}}
$$

The corresponding frequency is approximately

$$
f=\frac{1}{T}\approx 2.25\times 10^{3}\ \mathrm{Hz}.
$$

## Physical considerations

The mass of the bob and its diameter do not affect the ideal simple-pendulum period, provided the center of mass is a distance $L$ from the support and the bob can be treated as a point mass.

The result is physically valid within the idealized small-angle, negligible-string-mass model. However, at nanometre length scales, air resistance, damping, Brownian motion, and thermal fluctuations may significantly affect the motion. Also, a $2.5$-nanogram sphere with a diameter of $100\ \mathrm{nm}$ would have an extremely high density, so the stated parameters are not realistic for an ordinary material.

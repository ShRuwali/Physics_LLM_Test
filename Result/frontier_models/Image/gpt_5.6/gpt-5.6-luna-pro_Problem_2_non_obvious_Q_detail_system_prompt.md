## Description

The figure shows a simple pendulum consisting of a spherical bob suspended by a massless string. The distance from the point of support to the sphere’s center of mass is

$L = 2.0\ \mathrm{m}$,

and the gravitational acceleration is

$g = 10.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

For small angular displacements, the pendulum undergoes approximately simple harmonic motion.

## Time period

For a simple pendulum,

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2.0}{10.0}}
$$

$$
T \approx 2.81\ \mathrm{s}
$$

Therefore, the time period of oscillation is

$$
\boxed{T \approx 2.8\ \mathrm{s}}
$$

The mass and diameter of the sphere do not affect the period in the ideal simple-pendulum model, provided the given length is measured to the sphere’s center of mass.

### Physical considerations

The approximation assumes a small displacement, a massless and inextensible string, negligible air resistance, and a compact spherical bob. The stated mass, $2.5\ \mathrm{ng}$, packed into a sphere of diameter $1\ \mathrm{nm}$ would imply an extremely large density, approximately

$$
\rho \approx 4.8 \times 10^{15}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}},
$$

which is not realistic for ordinary matter. Thus, the calculated period is mathematically valid for the idealized model, but the specified sphere is not physically plausible as a conventional material object.

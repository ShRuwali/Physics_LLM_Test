## Description

A solid sphere rolls without slipping down an inclined plane from a vertical height of $h=6.0\times10^8\ \mathrm{m}$. Its mass is $m=4\ \mathrm{ng}$, diameter is $8\ \mathrm{nm}$, and its initial speed is $v_i=3.001\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

For a solid sphere,

$$
I=\frac{2}{5}mr^2
$$

and, because it rolls without slipping,

$$
\omega=\frac{v}{r}.
$$

Thus, its total kinetic energy is

$$
K=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2
=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{5}mv^2
=\frac{7}{10}mv^2.
$$

## Conservation of energy

Assuming the sphere moves downhill and there are no energy losses,

$$
mgh+\frac{7}{10}mv_i^2
=
\frac{7}{10}mv_f^2.
$$

Solving for the final speed,

$$
v_f=\sqrt{v_i^2+\frac{10}{7}gh}.
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
v_f
=
\sqrt{
(3.001\times10^8)^2
+
\frac{10}{7}(10.0)(6.0\times10^8)
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{v_f\approx 3.00100014\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}}
$$

or approximately

$$
\boxed{v_f\approx 300{,}100{,}014\ \mathrm{m/s}}.
$$

The mass and diameter cancel from the calculation because the sphere is a uniform solid sphere rolling without slipping.

## Physical validity

The calculated result is only a formal classical-mechanics result. The stated initial speed,

$$
3.001\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s},
$$

is greater than the speed of light, $c\approx2.998\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}$, which is physically impossible. Therefore, the classical rolling and energy model is not physically justifiable for these parameters. Additionally, using a constant gravitational acceleration of $10.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ over a height of $6.0\times10^8\ \mathrm{m}$ is unrealistic.

## Description

The figure shows a solid sphere rolling without slipping down an inclined plane. The sphere has:

- Mass: $m=4$ nanograms $=4.0\times10^{-12}\,\text{kg}$
- Diameter: $8\,\text{nm}$, so $r=4\,\text{nm}$
- Initial speed: $v_i=3.001\times10^8\,\text{m/s}$
- Vertical height decrease: $h=6.0\times10^8\,\text{m}$
- Gravitational acceleration: $g=10.0\,\text{m/s}^2$

The incline angle is not needed because the vertical height change is given directly.

## Conservation of energy

For a solid sphere,

$$
I=\frac{2}{5}mr^2
$$

Since the sphere rolls without slipping,

$$
\omega=\frac{v}{r}
$$

Thus, the total kinetic energy is

$$
K=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2
$$

$$
K=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2
=\frac{7}{10}mv^2
$$

Applying conservation of mechanical energy between the top and bottom:

$$
\frac{7}{10}mv_i^2+mgh=\frac{7}{10}mv_f^2
$$

Solving for the final velocity,

$$
v_f=\sqrt{v_i^2+\frac{10}{7}gh}
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
v_f=\sqrt{(3.001\times10^8)^2+\frac{10}{7}(10.0)(6.0\times10^8)}
$$

$$
v_f\approx 3.00100014\times10^8\,\text{m/s}
$$

Therefore, the sphere's velocity at the bottom is approximately

$$
\boxed{v_f\approx 3.00100014\times10^8\,\text{m/s}}
$$

directed down the plane.

> **Physical note:** The stated initial speed, $3.001\times10^8\,\text{m/s}$, is greater than the speed of light. Therefore, this result is only the formal answer using classical mechanics; the data are not physically consistent with relativity.

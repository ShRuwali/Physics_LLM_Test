## Description

A solid sphere starts from rest at height $h=7.0\ \mathrm{m}$ and rolls without slipping down the inclined plane. The sphere has mass

$$
m=4\ \mathrm{ng}=4.0\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg}
$$

and diameter $8\ \mathrm{nm}$, so its radius is

$$
r=4.0\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

For a solid sphere,

$$
I=\frac{2}{5}mr^2.
$$

Because the sphere rolls without slipping,

$$
\omega=\frac{v}{r}.
$$

## Conservation of energy

The initial gravitational potential energy becomes translational and rotational kinetic energy:

$$
mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2.
$$

Substituting $I=\frac{2}{5}mr^2$ and $\omega=\frac{v}{r}$,

$$
mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2.
$$

Thus,

$$
mgh=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{5}mv^2
=\frac{7}{10}mv^2.
$$

Solving for the velocity,

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{10}{7}gh}.
$$

Using $g=10.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $h=7.0\ \mathrm{m}$,

$$
v=\sqrt{\frac{10}{7}(10.0)(7.0)}
=\sqrt{100}
=\boxed{10.0\ \mathrm{m/s}}.
$$

The sphere’s velocity at the bottom is therefore $\boxed{10.0\ \mathrm{m/s}}$ down the plane.

## Physical considerations

The mass cancels from the energy equation, so it does not affect the idealized speed. However, the given mass and diameter imply an extremely large density:

$$
\rho=\frac{m}{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3}
\approx1.5\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

This is far denser than ordinary matter. Also, treating an $8\ \mathrm{nm}$ object as a perfectly rigid classical sphere rolling without slipping is an idealization. Therefore, the result is mathematically valid under the stated rigid-body assumptions, but the physical parameters are not realistic for an ordinary solid sphere.

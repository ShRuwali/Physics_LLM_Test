## Description

A mass is attached to a string of length $R=5.0\ \mathrm{m}$ and moves in a vertical circle about a fixed point. At the bottom of the circle, the string tension acts upward, while gravity acts downward. The required centripetal acceleration is directed upward toward the circle’s center.

Given:

- Mass: $m=10\ \mathrm{ng}=1.0\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{kg}$
- Radius of circular motion: $R=5.0\ \mathrm{m}$
- Speed: $v=3.001\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}$
- Gravitational acceleration: $g=10.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$

## Tension at the bottom

Applying Newton’s second law in the upward direction:

$$
T-mg=\frac{mv^2}{R}
$$

Therefore,

$$
T=m\left(\frac{v^2}{R}+g\right)
$$

Substituting the values:

$$
T=(1.0\times10^{-11})
\left[
\frac{(3.001\times10^8)^2}{5.0}+10.0
\right]
$$

$$
T\approx 1.8012\times10^5\ \mathrm{N}
$$

Thus, the classical tension at the bottom is

$$
\boxed{T\approx 1.80\times10^5\ \mathrm{N}}
$$

The $100\ \mathrm{nm}$ size of the mass does not affect this calculation. Strictly speaking, the stated speed is slightly greater than the speed of light, so the result is only a formal classical calculation and is not physically realizable.

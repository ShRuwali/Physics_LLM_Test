### Description

A small mass moves in a vertical circular path on a taut, inextensible string attached to a fixed point. At the bottom of the circle, the tension points upward toward the center, while the weight acts downward.

The relevant radius of the mass's circular motion is the string length, $R=5.0\ \text{m}$. The $100\ \text{nm}$ value is the radius of the mass itself and is negligible compared with the string length.

### Tension at the bottom

At the bottom of the circle,

$$
T-mg=\frac{mv^2}{R}
$$

Therefore,

$$
T=m\left(g+\frac{v^2}{R}\right)
$$

Using

$$
m=10\ \text{ng}=1.0\times10^{-11}\ \text{kg},\qquad
g=10.0\ \text{m/s}^2,\qquad
v=5.0\ \text{m/s},\qquad
R=5.0\ \text{m},
$$

we obtain

$$
T=(1.0\times10^{-11})
\left(10.0+\frac{(5.0)^2}{5.0}\right)
$$

$$
T=(1.0\times10^{-11})(15.0)
=1.5\times10^{-10}\ \text{N}
$$

Thus, the tension at the bottom of the circle is

$$
\boxed{T=1.5\times10^{-10}\ \text{N}}
$$

## Description

The figure shows two blocks on a frictionless horizontal surface:

- Block A: $m_A=100$ nanograms, initially at rest.
- Block B: $m_B=200$ nanograms.
- After the collision, block B moves right at $v_{B2}=10\ \mathrm{km/s}=10{,}000\ \mathrm{m/s}$.
- The horizontal momentum of the two-block system is conserved.

Taking rightward as positive, conservation of momentum gives

$$
m_Av_{A1}+m_Bv_{B1}
=
m_Av_{A2}+m_Bv_{B2}.
$$

## Using the velocity stated in the text

If block B initially moves right at $v_{B1}=100\ \mathrm{m/s}$, then

$$
(100)(0)+(200)(100)
=
(100)v_{A2}+(200)(10{,}000).
$$

Therefore,

$$
v_{A2}
=
\frac{(200)(100)-(200)(10{,}000)}{100}
=
-19{,}800\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

Thus,

$$
\boxed{v_{A2}=-19.8\ \mathrm{km/s}}
$$

so block A moves left at $19.8\ \mathrm{km/s}$.

## Important consistency issue

The arrow in the figure indicates that block B initially moves left, whereas the text says it moves right. If the arrow is intended to be correct, then $v_{B1}=-100\ \mathrm{m/s}$, and

$$
v_{A2}
=
\frac{(200)(-100)-(200)(10{,}000)}{100}
=
-20{,}200\ \mathrm{m/s}.
$$

Hence, using the arrow direction,

$$
\boxed{v_{A2}=-20.2\ \mathrm{km/s}}
$$

or $20.2\ \mathrm{km/s}$ to the left.

The stated rightward initial motion is also inconsistent with the drawn positions, because block B would be moving away from the stationary block A rather than toward it. Therefore, the physically consistent collision interpretation is the one where block B initially moves left, giving $v_{A2}=20.2\ \mathrm{km/s}$ to the left. Additionally, the very large increase in kinetic energy means the collision cannot be an ordinary elastic or passive inelastic collision without an external energy source or stored internal energy.

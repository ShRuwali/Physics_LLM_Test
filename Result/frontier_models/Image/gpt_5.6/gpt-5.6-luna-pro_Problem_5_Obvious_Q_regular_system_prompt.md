## Description

Block $A$ has mass $m_A=100$ nanograms and is initially at rest. Block $B$ has mass $m_B=200$ nanograms and initially moves to the right at $100\ \mathrm{m/s}$. After the collision, block $B$ moves to the right at $10\ \mathrm{km/s}$. Since there is no friction, horizontal momentum is conserved.

Taking rightward as positive:

$$
m_Av_{A1}+m_Bv_{B1}=m_Av_{A2}+m_Bv_{B2}
$$

Convert the final velocity of block $B$:

$$
v_{B2}=10\ \mathrm{km/s}=10{,}000\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

Substitute the given values, with $v_{A1}=0$:

$$
(100)(0)+(200)(100)=(100)v_{A2}+(200)(10{,}000)
$$

Solving for $v_{A2}$:

$$
v_{A2}=\frac{(200)(100)-(200)(10{,}000)}{100}
$$

$$
v_{A2}=-19{,}800\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

Therefore, block $A$ moves at

$$
\boxed{v_{A2}=19.8\ \mathrm{km/s}\ \text{to the left}}
$$

The negative sign indicates motion opposite to the chosen positive, rightward direction.

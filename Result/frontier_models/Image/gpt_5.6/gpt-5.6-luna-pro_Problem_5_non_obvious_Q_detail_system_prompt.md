## Description

Block A moves initially to the right at $1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$, while block B moves to the left at $2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$. The surface is frictionless, so horizontal momentum is conserved. After the collision, block B moves to the right at $1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

Take rightward as positive:

- $m_A=100\ \mathrm{ng}$
- $m_B=200\ \mathrm{ng}$
- $v_{A1}=+1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$
- $v_{B1}=-2.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$
- $v_{B2}=+1.0\ \mathrm{m/s}$

## Velocity of block A

Conservation of horizontal momentum gives

$$
m_Av_{A1}+m_Bv_{B1}=m_Av_{A2}+m_Bv_{B2}
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
(100)(1.0)+(200)(-2.0)
=
(100)v_{A2}+(200)(1.0)
$$

$$
-300=100v_{A2}+200
$$

Therefore,

$$
v_{A2}=-5.0\ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

Thus, block A moves to the left at

$$
\boxed{5.0\ \mathrm{m/s}}
$$

or, including direction,

$$
\boxed{v_{A2}=-5.0\ \mathrm{m/s}}
$$

## Physical consistency

The result conserves momentum, but it does not conserve kinetic energy:

$$
K_{\mathrm{initial}}=4.5\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

whereas

$$
K_{\mathrm{final}}=1.35\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

The kinetic energy increases by

$$
\Delta K=9.0\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

Therefore, the collision cannot be an ordinary passive elastic or inelastic collision. It is physically justifiable only if energy is released during the collision, such as from a spring, explosive mechanism, or other stored internal energy.

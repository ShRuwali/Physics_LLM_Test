## Description of the figures

- **Figure (a):** A spring is fixed at one end and stretched from an initial position $x=0$ to a final position $x=20\,\text{m}$. The applied force is shown as $F=0\,\text{N}$.
- **Figure (b):** The spring is attached to a frictionless block of mass $m=500\,\text{ng}$, which is displaced from $x=0$ to $x=0.040\,\text{m}$. The block has dimensions $400\,\text{nm}\times400\,\text{nm}\times400\,\text{nm}$.

The spring obeys Hooke’s law:

$$
F=kx
$$

where $k$ is the force constant.

## Force constant from Figure (a)

Using the values exactly as shown in the figure,

$$
k=\frac{F}{x}
$$

$$
k=\frac{0\,\text{N}}{20\,\text{m}}=0\,\text{N m}^{-1}
$$

Thus, the force constant would be

$$
\boxed{k=0\,\text{N m}^{-1}}
$$

However, this result is physically inconsistent with an oscillating spring. A spring with $k=0$ provides no restoring force, so it cannot produce oscillations. Therefore, the force value in Figure (a) is likely missing or incorrectly labeled.

## Angular frequency and frequency

The mass is

$$
m=500\,\text{ng}=500\times10^{-12}\,\text{kg}
=5.00\times10^{-10}\,\text{kg}
$$

For a mass–spring oscillator,

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

Using $k=0\,\text{N m}^{-1}$,

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{0}{5.00\times10^{-10}}}=0\,\text{rad s}^{-1}
$$

The frequency is

$$
f=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{\omega=0\,\text{rad s}^{-1}}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{f=0\,\text{Hz}}
$$

This means there would be no oscillation under the values printed in the figure.

If the intended force in Figure (a) was a nonzero value $F_a$, the results would instead be

$$
k=\frac{F_a}{20}
$$

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{F_a}{20m}}
$$

and

$$
f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{F_a}{20m}}.
$$

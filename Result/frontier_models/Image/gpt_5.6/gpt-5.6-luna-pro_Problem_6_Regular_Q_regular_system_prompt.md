## Description of the figures

- **Fig. (a):** A spring is fixed at one end and stretched by a force of $5.0\ \mathrm{N}$. Its displacement changes from $0$ to $0.020\ \mathrm{m}$.
- **Fig. (b):** The same spring is attached to a block of mass $m=0.40\ \mathrm{kg}$ on a frictionless horizontal surface. The block is displaced by $0.040\ \mathrm{m}$ and then oscillates.

## (a) Force constant of the spring

Using Hooke’s law,

$$
F=kx
$$

Therefore,

$$
k=\frac{F}{x}
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
k=\frac{5.0\ \mathrm{N}}{0.020\ \mathrm{m}}
$$

$$
\boxed{k=250\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}}
$$

The force at a displacement of $0.040\ \mathrm{m}$ in Fig. (b) would be

$$
F=kx=(250)(0.040)=10.0\ \mathrm{N}
$$

## (b) Angular frequency and frequency of oscillation

For a block attached to a spring,

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

Using $k=250\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ and $m=0.40\ \mathrm{kg}$,

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{250}{0.40}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\omega=25\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}}
$$

The ordinary frequency is

$$
f=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

Thus,

$$
f=\frac{25}{2\pi}
$$

$$
\boxed{f\approx 3.98\ \mathrm{Hz}}
$$

The displacement of $0.040\ \mathrm{m}$ determines the amplitude but does not affect the angular frequency or frequency for ideal simple harmonic motion.

## Description of the figures

- **Figure (a):** A spring is stretched from its initial position, $x=0$, to a final displacement of $x=0.020\ \text{m}$ by applying a force of $F=5.0\ \text{N}$.
- **Figure (b):** The same spring is attached to a block of mass $500\ \text{ng}$ on a frictionless horizontal surface. The block is displaced to $x=0.040\ \text{m}$ and then released, producing simple harmonic motion.

## (a) Force constant of the spring

Using Hooke’s law,

$$
F=kx
$$

where $k$ is the spring constant. Therefore,

$$
k=\frac{F}{x}
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
k=\frac{5.0\ \text{N}}{0.020\ \text{m}}
$$

$$
\boxed{k=250\ \text{N m}^{-1}}
$$

## (b) Angular frequency and frequency of oscillation

The mass is

$$
m=500\ \text{ng}
$$

Since $1\ \text{ng}=10^{-12}\ \text{kg}$,

$$
m=500\times10^{-12}\ \text{kg}
=5.00\times10^{-10}\ \text{kg}
$$

For a block attached to an ideal spring,

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

Thus,

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{250}{5.00\times10^{-10}}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\omega=7.07\times10^{5}\ \text{rad s}^{-1}}
$$

The frequency is related to angular frequency by

$$
f=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

Therefore,

$$
f=\frac{7.07\times10^{5}}{2\pi}
$$

$$
\boxed{f=1.13\times10^{5}\ \text{Hz}}
$$

or approximately

$$
\boxed{f=113\ \text{kHz}}
$$

The displacement $x=0.040\ \text{m}$ is the amplitude and does not affect the frequency for an ideal Hooke’s-law spring. At this displacement, the corresponding spring force would be

$$
F=kx=(250)(0.040)=10\ \text{N}.
$$

## Physical assumptions and consistency

These results assume an ideal, massless spring obeying Hooke’s law, a frictionless surface, and release of the block after it is displaced. A constant applied force alone would not produce oscillations unless the force is removed and the block is allowed to move freely.

Also, the stated block dimensions of $400\ \text{nm}$ and mass of $500\ \text{ng}$ imply an extremely large density, so those parameters are not realistic for an ordinary material. The calculation is mathematically valid under the stated idealized conditions, but the numerical parameters are physically questionable.

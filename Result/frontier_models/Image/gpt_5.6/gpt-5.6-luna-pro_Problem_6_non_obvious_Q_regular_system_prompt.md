## Description of the figures

- **Figure (a):** A spring is stretched from its initial position, $x=0$, to a final displacement of $x=0.020\ \text{m}$ by applying a force of $5.0\ \text{N}$.
- **Figure (b):** The same spring is attached to a frictionless block of mass $500\ \text{ng}$. The block is displaced by $0.040\ \text{m}$ and then allowed to oscillate horizontally. The block dimensions do not affect the oscillation because there is no friction.

## (a) Force constant of the spring

Using Hooke’s law,

$$
F=kx
$$

Therefore,

$$
k=\frac{F}{x}
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
k=\frac{5.0\ \text{N}}{0.020\ \text{m}}
$$

$$
\boxed{k=250\ \text{N m}^{-1}}
$$

For the displacement in Figure (b), the corresponding spring force would be

$$
F=kx=(250)(0.040)=10\ \text{N}.
$$

## (b) Angular frequency and frequency of oscillation

The mass is

$$
m=500\ \text{ng}
=500\times10^{-12}\ \text{kg}
=5.0\times10^{-10}\ \text{kg}.
$$

For a mass–spring oscillator, the angular frequency is

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}.
$$

Thus,

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{250}{5.0\times10^{-10}}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\omega\approx 7.07\times10^{5}\ \text{rad s}^{-1}}
$$

The ordinary frequency is

$$
f=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
f=\frac{7.07\times10^{5}}{2\pi}
$$

$$
\boxed{f\approx1.13\times10^{5}\ \text{Hz}}
$$

The displacement amplitude does not affect the frequency for an ideal Hooke’s-law spring.

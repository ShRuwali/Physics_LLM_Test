## Description

The figure shows a fully submerged cubic block suspended by a cable in seawater. The relevant forces are the cable tension, the block’s weight, and the buoyant force.

Assuming the stated block mass is used:

- Block mass: $m=1000\ \mathrm{ng}=1.0\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}$
- Cube side length: $L=100\ \mathrm{nm}=1.0\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m}$
- Block volume:

$$
V=L^3=(1.0\times10^{-7})^3
=1.0\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{m^3}
$$

- Seawater density: $\rho_w=1.03\times10^3\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- Gravitational acceleration magnitude: $g=10.0\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$

The buoyant force is

$$
F_B=\rho_w Vg
=(1.03\times10^3)(1.0\times10^{-21})(10.0)
=1.03\times10^{-17}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

## Tension for the usual downward gravity convention

If gravity is intended to act downward, static equilibrium gives

$$
T+F_B=mg
$$

Therefore,

$$
T=mg-F_B
$$

$$
T=(1.0\times10^{-9})(10.0)-1.03\times10^{-17}
$$

$$
\boxed{T\approx 1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}}
$$

The buoyant force is negligible compared with the block’s weight.

## Physical consistency issues

The figure states that the acceleration due to gravity is upward. If gravity truly acts upward, the hydrostatic buoyant force would act downward, opposite to the usual situation. For equilibrium, the required cable force would be

$$
T=F_B-mg
$$

$$
T=1.03\times10^{-17}-1.0\times10^{-8}
\approx -1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

A negative tension is not physically possible for an ordinary cable; it would indicate compression rather than tension. Thus, the upward-gravity configuration is not physically justifiable for a taut cable.

There is also a data inconsistency: the stated density of the block, $1.0\times10^9\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, combined with the given volume implies

$$
m=\rho V=(1.0\times10^9)(1.0\times10^{-21})
=1.0\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg}
$$

which differs from the stated mass, $1.0\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}$. Therefore, $\boxed{T\approx1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}}$ is valid only if the explicitly stated mass is used and the gravity direction is corrected to downward.

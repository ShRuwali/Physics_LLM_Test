## Description

A cubic block is fully submerged in seawater and attached to a vertical cable. The cube has side length $100\ \mathrm{nm}$, and the figure specifies an upward gravitational acceleration.

The cube’s volume is

$$
V=(100\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m})^3
=1.00\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{m^3}.
$$

Using the given mass,

$$
m=1000\ \mathrm{ng}
=1.00\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

## Forces

Because gravity is specified as upward, the gravitational force is

$$
F_g=mg
=(1.00\times10^{-9})(10.0)
=1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

directed upward.

The buoyant force is opposite to the specified gravity direction, so it acts downward:

$$
F_b=\rho_{\text{water}}Vg
=(1.03\times10^3)(1.00\times10^{-21})(10.0)
=1.03\times10^{-17}\ \mathrm{N}.
$$

For static equilibrium, taking upward as positive,

$$
T+F_g-F_b=0.
$$

Therefore,

$$
T=F_b-F_g
=1.03\times10^{-17}-1.00\times10^{-8}
\approx -1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}.
$$

## Result

The negative sign means that a downward force would be required:

$$
\boxed{T\approx -1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}}
$$

Thus, with gravity truly directed upward, the block cannot remain in static equilibrium using a taut cable that can only pull upward. If the intended gravity direction was downward, the cable tension would instead be

$$
\boxed{T\approx 1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

*Note: the stated mass, dimensions, and block density are not mutually consistent. The given mass implies a density of $1.0\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, not $1.0\times10^9\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The calculation above uses the explicitly given mass.*

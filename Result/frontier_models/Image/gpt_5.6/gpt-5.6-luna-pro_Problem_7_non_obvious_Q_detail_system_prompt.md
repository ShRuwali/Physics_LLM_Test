## Description

A cubic block is fully submerged in seawater and suspended by a vertical cable. The forces on the stationary block are:

- Weight, $W=mg$, downward
- Buoyant force, $F_B=\rho_{\mathrm{water}}Vg$, upward
- Cable tension, $T$, upward

For vertical equilibrium,

$$
T+F_B=W
$$

so

$$
T=mg-\rho_{\mathrm{water}}Vg
$$

## Calculation using the stated mass

The block dimensions are

$$
L=100\ \mathrm{nm}=1.00\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m}
$$

and its volume is

$$
V=L^3=(1.00\times10^{-7})^3
=1.00\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{m^3}
$$

The mass is

$$
m=1000\ \mathrm{ng}=1.00\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}
$$

The buoyant force is

$$
F_B=(1.03\times10^3)(1.00\times10^{-21})(10.0)
=1.03\times10^{-17}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=(1.00\times10^{-9})(10.0)-1.03\times10^{-17}\\
&=9.99999999\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, to a practical number of significant figures,

$$
\boxed{T\approx1.00\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{N}}
$$

## Consistency check

The given block density, $1.0\times10^9\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, combined with the stated volume implies

$$
m=\rho V=(1.0\times10^9)(1.0\times10^{-21})
=1.0\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg}
$$

This is $1\ \mathrm{ng}$, not $1000\ \mathrm{ng}$. If the density is intended to be authoritative instead of the stated mass, the tension would be

$$
T=(1.0\times10^{-12})(10.0)-1.03\times10^{-17}
\approx9.99999\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

The force calculation is physically valid under the assumptions of static equilibrium, complete submersion, and negligible cable mass. However, the mass, dimensions, and density given in the figure are mutually inconsistent, and the stated density is extraordinarily large for an ordinary material.

## Description

The figure shows two approximately spherical bodies separated by a center-to-center distance of

$$
d = 3.0 \times 10^{9}\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

Planet A has mass

$$
M = 6000\ \mathrm{ng},
$$

and the Moon has mass

$$
m = 2.0 \times 10^{22}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

The gravitational force between spherically symmetric bodies can be calculated using Newton’s law of universal gravitation, provided the distance is measured between their centers and the bodies do not overlap.

## Unit conversion

Since

$$
1\ \mathrm{ng} = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg},
$$

the mass of Planet A is

$$
M = 6000 \times 10^{-12}
= 6.0 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

## Gravitational force

Newton’s law gives

$$
F = \frac{G M m}{d^2},
$$

where

$$
G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}.
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
F =
\frac{
\left(6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\right)
\left(6.0 \times 10^{-9}\right)
\left(2.0 \times 10^{22}\right)
}{
\left(3.0 \times 10^{9}\right)^2
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

The force on Planet A due to the Moon and the force on the Moon due to Planet A have equal magnitude and opposite directions:

$$
\boxed{F_{\text{Planet A}} = F_{\text{Moon}} \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

Both forces are attractive and act along the line joining the two centers.

## Physical considerations

The calculation is physically justified under the assumption that both objects are spherically symmetric and that the Moon’s radius is much smaller than the center-to-center separation. The Moon’s radius is not specified, so this non-overlap condition cannot be checked directly.

However, Planet A’s parameters imply an extremely large average density. Its radius is $100\ \mathrm{nm} = 1.0 \times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m}$, so

$$
\rho =
\frac{M}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}
\approx 1.4 \times 10^{12}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.
$$

This is far denser than ordinary matter, making the proposed planet physically unusual and likely unrealistic as a normal material body. Nevertheless, under the stated idealized spherical-mass assumptions, the gravitational-force result is valid.

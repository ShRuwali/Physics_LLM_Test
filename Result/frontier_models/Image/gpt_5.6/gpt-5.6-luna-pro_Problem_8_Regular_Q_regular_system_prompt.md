## Description

The figure shows Planet A and its moon orbiting with a center-to-center separation of

$ d = 3.0 \times 10^9\ \mathrm{m} $.

Their properties are:

- Planet A: $M = 6.0 \times 10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}$, radius $R_A = 6.0 \times 10^6\ \mathrm{m}$
- Moon: $m = 2.0 \times 10^{22}\ \mathrm{kg}$

Because both bodies are spherically symmetric, the gravitational force can be calculated as though their masses were concentrated at their centers.

## Gravitational force

Using Newton’s law of universal gravitation,

$$
F = \frac{G M m}{d^2},
$$

where $G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}$.

Substituting the given values,

$$
F =
\frac{
\left(6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\right)
\left(6.0 \times 10^{24}\right)
\left(2.0 \times 10^{22}\right)
}{
\left(3.0 \times 10^9\right)^2
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F \approx 8.9 \times 10^{17}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

The force is attractive: Planet A pulls the Moon toward its center, and the Moon pulls Planet A toward its center with an equal magnitude of $8.9 \times 10^{17}\ \mathrm{N}$ in the opposite direction.

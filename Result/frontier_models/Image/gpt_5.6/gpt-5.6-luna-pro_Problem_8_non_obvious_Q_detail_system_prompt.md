## Description

The figure shows Planet A and a much more massive body labeled Moon, separated by a center-to-center distance of

$$
d = 3.0 \times 10^9\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

The given masses are

$$
M = 6000\ \mathrm{nanograms}
$$

and

$$
m = 2.0 \times 10^{22}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

Since $1\ \mathrm{nanogram} = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg}$,

$$
M = 6000 \times 10^{-12}
= 6.0 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

For two non-overlapping, spherically symmetric bodies, the shell theorem allows each body to be treated as though all of its mass were concentrated at its center.

## Gravitational force

Using Newton’s law of universal gravitation,

$$
F = \frac{G M m}{d^2},
$$

where

$$
G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}.
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
F =
\frac{
(6.67430 \times 10^{-11})
(6.0 \times 10^{-9})
(2.0 \times 10^{22})
}{
(3.0 \times 10^9)^2
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

The force on Planet A due to the Moon and the force on the Moon due to Planet A have equal magnitudes and opposite directions:

$$
\boxed{F_{\text{Planet on Moon}} =
F_{\text{Moon on Planet}} \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

## Physical considerations

The calculation is mathematically valid under the assumptions that the bodies are spherically symmetric and that their surfaces do not overlap. The radius of the Moon is not provided, so this non-overlap condition cannot be independently verified from the figure.

However, Planet A has an extremely small mass, $6.0 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}$, despite having a radius of $100\ \mathrm{m}$. Its average density would be approximately

$$
\rho =
\frac{M}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}
\approx 1.4 \times 10^{-15}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}},
$$

which is extraordinarily low and not realistic for an ordinary planet. Thus, the result is physically justifiable as an idealized gravitational model, but the depicted planetary parameters are not realistic for a conventional solid celestial body.

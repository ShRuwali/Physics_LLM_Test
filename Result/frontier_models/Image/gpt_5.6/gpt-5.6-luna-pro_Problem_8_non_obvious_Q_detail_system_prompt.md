## Description

The figure shows a spherically symmetric planet and moon separated by a center-to-center distance

$$
d = 3.0 \times 10^9\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

The planet has mass $M = 6000$ nanograms, and the moon has mass $m = 2.0 \times 10^{22}\ \mathrm{kg}$.

Convert the planet’s mass to kilograms:

$$
M = 6000 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg}
= 6.0 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

Because both bodies are assumed to be spherically symmetric and non-overlapping, the gravitational interaction can be calculated as if all of each body’s mass were concentrated at its center.

## Gravitational force

Using Newton’s law of universal gravitation,

$$
F = \frac{G M m}{d^2},
$$

where

$$
G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}.
$$

Substituting the given values,

$$
F =
\frac{
\left(6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\right)
\left(6.0 \times 10^{-9}\right)
\left(2.0 \times 10^{22}\right)
}{
\left(3.0 \times 10^9\right)^2
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

The planet and moon exert forces of equal magnitude and opposite direction on each other:

$$
\boxed{F_{\text{planet on moon}} = F_{\text{moon on planet}} \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

The force on each body is directed along the line joining their centers and is attractive.

## Physical considerations

The planet’s radius is $100$ nanometers, which is negligible compared with the separation of $3.0 \times 10^9\ \mathrm{m}$. Its implied average density is extremely large:

$$
\rho_{\text{planet}} =
\frac{M}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}
\approx 1.4 \times 10^{12}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.
$$

This is highly unusual for an object of nanometer size. Also, the moon’s radius is not provided, so it is not possible to verify whether the bodies are non-overlapping. Nevertheless, under the stated spherical-symmetry and point-center assumptions, the calculated gravitational force is mathematically and physically valid within Newtonian gravity.

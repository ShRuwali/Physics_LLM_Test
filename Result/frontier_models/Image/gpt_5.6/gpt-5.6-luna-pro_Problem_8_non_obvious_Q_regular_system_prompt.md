## Description

The figure shows Planet A and a moon separated by a center-to-center distance of

$$
d = 3.0 \times 10^9\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

Planet A has mass $6000$ nanograms and radius $100\ \mathrm{m}$. The moon has mass

$$
m = 2.0 \times 10^{22}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

The dashed curve represents the moon’s orbit around Planet A.

## Mass conversion

Since $1\ \mathrm{nanogram} = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg}$,

$$
M = 6000 \times 10^{-12}
= 6.0 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

Because both bodies are spherically symmetric, their gravitational interaction can be calculated as if all their mass were concentrated at their centers.

## Gravitational force

Using Newton’s law of universal gravitation,

$$
F = \frac{G M m}{d^2},
$$

where

$$
G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2/kg^2}.
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
F =
\frac{
(6.67430 \times 10^{-11})
(6.0 \times 10^{-9})
(2.0 \times 10^{22})
}{
(3.0 \times 10^9)^2
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

The force is attractive: Planet A pulls on the moon with this force, and the moon pulls on Planet A with an equal-magnitude force in the opposite direction.

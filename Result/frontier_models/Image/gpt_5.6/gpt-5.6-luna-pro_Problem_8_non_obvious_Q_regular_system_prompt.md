## Description

The figure shows Planet A and a moon separated by a center-to-center distance

$$
d = 3.0 \times 10^{9}\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

Both bodies are treated as spherically symmetric, so by the shell theorem, their gravitational interaction is equivalent to that between point masses located at their centers.

The given masses are:

$$
M = 6000\ \mathrm{nanograms}
= 6000 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{kg}
= 6.0 \times 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg},
$$

$$
m = 2.0 \times 10^{22}\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

## Gravitational force

Using Newton’s law of universal gravitation,

$$
F = \frac{G M m}{d^2},
$$

where

$$
G = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}.
$$

Substituting the values,

$$
F =
\frac{
\left(6.67430 \times 10^{-11}\right)
\left(6.0 \times 10^{-9}\right)
\left(2.0 \times 10^{22}\right)
}{
\left(3.0 \times 10^{9}\right)^2
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}}.
$$

The gravitational force exerted by Planet A on the moon and by the moon on Planet A has the same magnitude, $8.9 \times 10^{-16}\ \mathrm{N}$, but the two forces act in opposite directions along the line joining their centers.

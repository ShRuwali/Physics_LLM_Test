## Setting Up the Problem

For a simple pendulum undergoing small oscillations, the time period is:

T = 2π √(L/g)

**Key insight:** The period of a simple pendulum does not depend on the mass or size of the bob (as long as we can treat it as a point mass, which is valid here since the sphere's diameter of 1 nanometer is utterly negligible compared to L = 2 m). The mass of 2.5 nanograms and the diameter are distractors.

## Calculation

Given:
- L = 2 m
- g = 10 m/s²

T = 2π √(2/10)

T = 2π √(0.2)

T = 2π × 0.447

**T ≈ 2.81 seconds**

## Note

The tiny mass and nanometer-scale size have no effect on the answer — gravity-driven pendulum motion depends only on the length and gravitational acceleration. (One could check that quantum effects are irrelevant: even for a 2.5 nanogram mass, the de Broglie wavelength at these speeds is vastly smaller than the pendulum's amplitude, so classical mechanics applies perfectly well.)

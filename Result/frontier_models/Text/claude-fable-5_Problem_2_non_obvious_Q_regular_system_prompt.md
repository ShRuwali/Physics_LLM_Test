## Setting Up the Problem

For a simple pendulum with small displacements, the time period is:

**T = 2π √(L/g)**

Here's the key insight: the period of a simple pendulum does **not** depend on the mass of the bob. The mass of 2.5 nanograms is extra information (a distractor).

Also, the sphere's diameter (1 nanometer) is utterly negligible compared to the string length (2 meters), so treating this as a simple pendulum (point mass) is completely valid — the correction from the sphere's moment of inertia would be on the order of 1 part in 10¹⁹.

## Calculation

Given:
- L = 2 m
- g = 10 m/s²

T = 2π √(2/10)
T = 2π √(0.2)
T = 2π × 0.4472
T ≈ 2.81 seconds

## Answer

**The time period of the pendulum is approximately 2.8 seconds.**

Note: Even though the bob is nanoscale in size, the pendulum behaves classically — the period depends only on the length and gravitational acceleration, not on the mass or size of the bob.

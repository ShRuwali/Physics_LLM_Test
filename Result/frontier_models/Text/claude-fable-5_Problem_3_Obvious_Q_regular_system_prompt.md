# Analyzing the Problem

**The key issue: This scenario is physically impossible.**

The initial velocity given is 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s. The speed of light in vacuum is c ≈ 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s (approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s).

**No object with mass can travel at or faster than the speed of light.** According to special relativity, as an object approaches the speed of light, its kinetic energy approaches infinity, meaning it would take infinite energy to accelerate any massive object (even a 4-nanogram sphere) to the speed of light, let alone beyond it. So a sphere released at 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s cannot exist.

# What a Naive Classical Calculation Would Give (for illustration only)

If we ignored relativity and blindly applied energy conservation for a rolling solid sphere (moment of inertia factor 2/5, so total kinetic energy = 7/10 mv²):

- v_final² = v_initial² + (10/7)gh
- v_initial² = (3.001 × 10⁸)² = 9.006 × 10¹⁶ m²/s²
- (10/7)gh = (10/7)(10)(6 × 10⁸) ≈ 8.57 × 10¹⁰ m²/s²

The gravitational term is about a million times smaller than the kinetic term, so:

- v_final ≈ 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s (essentially unchanged)

# Additional Red Flags in the Problem

- The height of the incline (6 × 10⁸ m) is nearly twice the Earth–Moon distance — you cannot have such an incline "on Earth," and g would not stay at 10 m/s² over that distance.
- At speeds this high, classical mechanics and simple energy conservation (½mv²) are invalid; relativistic mechanics must be used.

# Conclusion

The problem as stated has no valid physical answer because the initial speed exceeds the speed of light, which violates special relativity. A classical calculation would return roughly 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s, but that result is meaningless physics — it merely shows the gravitational energy gained is negligible compared to the (impossible) initial kinetic energy.

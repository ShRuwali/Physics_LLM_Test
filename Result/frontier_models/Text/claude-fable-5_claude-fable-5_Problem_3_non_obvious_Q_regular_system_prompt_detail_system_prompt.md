## Analysis of the Problem

Before solving, let me check the physical validity of the given values — and several serious problems appear.

### Physical Laws Broken

**1. The initial velocity exceeds the speed of light (fatal flaw).**
The sphere is given an initial velocity of 3.001 × 10^8 m/s. The speed of light in vacuum is approximately 2.998 × 10^8 m/s. No object with mass can travel at or above the speed of light — this violates Einstein's special theory of relativity. This alone makes the problem physically impossible. Furthermore, any answer computed via energy conservation would give a final velocity even *greater* than this, making things worse.

**2. "Rigid body" at relativistic speeds is a contradiction.**
Perfectly rigid bodies cannot exist in relativity, since that would allow signals (mechanical disturbances) to travel through the body faster than light.

**3. The height is unphysical for a constant-g treatment.**
The height is 6 × 10^8 meters — nearly 100 times Earth's radius (about 6.4 × 10^6 m) and beyond the Moon's orbit (3.84 × 10^8 m). An inclined plane of this size cannot exist "on Earth," and gravity certainly cannot be treated as a constant 10 m/s² over such a distance; g would drop to nearly zero at that altitude. The simple "mgh" formula is invalid here.

**4. The sphere's density is impossibly large.**
A sphere of diameter 8 nanometers has a volume of about 2.7 × 10^-25 cubic meters. With a mass of 4 nanograms (4 × 10^-12 kg), its density would be roughly 1.5 × 10^13 kg/m³ — comparable to neutron star matter, about a billion times denser than any ordinary material on Earth.

### What a naive (incorrect) classical calculation would give

If one blindly ignored all of the above and applied classical energy conservation for a sliding body:

v = square root of (v₀² + 2gh) = square root of ((3.001 × 10^8)² + 2 × 10 × 6 × 10^8)
= square root of (9.006 × 10^16 + 1.2 × 10^10) ≈ 3.001 × 10^8 m/s

The gravitational term is utterly negligible compared to the kinetic term, and the "answer" is still faster than light — confirming the setup is nonsense.

### Conclusion

**This problem cannot be solved.** It violates special relativity (superluminal initial speed), assumes an impossible rigid body, uses an unphysical constant gravitational field over a height far exceeding Earth's size, and describes an object with neutron-star-level density. The scenario as stated is physically impossible.

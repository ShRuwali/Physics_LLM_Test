To find the time period of the pendulum, we must treat it as a physical pendulum because the solid sphere has a non-negligible size (radius).

**1. Calculate the Moment of Inertia about the Point of Support**

The moment of inertia of a solid sphere about its center of mass is:
I_cm = (2/5) * m * R^2

Given:
* Mass of the sphere, m = 2.0 kg
* Radius of the sphere, R = 1.0 m
* Distance from the pivot to the center of the sphere, L = 2.0 m
* Acceleration due to gravity, g = 10.0 m/s^2

I_cm = (2/5) * 2.0 * (1.0)^2 = 0.8 kg·m^2

Using the parallel axis theorem, the total moment of inertia (I) about the point of support (pivot) is:
I = I_cm + m * L^2
I = 0.8 + 2.0 * (2.0)^2
I = 0.8 + 8.0 = 8.8 kg·m^2

**2. Calculate the Time Period**

The formula for the time period (T) of a physical pendulum for small displacements is:
T = 2 * pi * sqrt( I / (m * g * L) )

Substitute the known values into the equation:
m * g * L = 2.0 * 10.0 * 2.0 = 40.0 N·m

T = 2 * pi * sqrt( 8.8 / 40.0 )
T = 2 * pi * sqrt( 0.22 )
T ≈ 2 * 3.14159 * 0.46904
T ≈ 2.947 seconds

**Answer:**
The time period of the pendulum for small displacements is approximately **2.95 seconds** (or 2.947 seconds).

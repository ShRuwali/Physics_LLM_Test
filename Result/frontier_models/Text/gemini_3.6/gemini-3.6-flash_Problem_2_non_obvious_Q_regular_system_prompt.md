To find the time period of a simple pendulum for small displacements, we use the formula for the period of a simple pendulum:

Time Period (T) = 2 * pi * sqrt(L / g)

Where:
- L is the distance between the point of support and the center of mass (2.0 meters).
- g is the acceleration due to gravity (10.0 m/s^2).
- pi is approximately 3.14159.

Note that for an ideal simple pendulum, the mass and the size of the spherical bob do not affect the time period as long as the radius is negligible compared to the length L. Here, the radius (0.5 nanometers) is exceedingly small compared to the length (2.0 meters), so it acts as a point mass.

Step-by-step calculation:

1. Calculate the ratio L / g:
   L / g = 2.0 / 10.0 = 0.2 s^2

2. Take the square root of the ratio:
   sqrt(0.2) ≈ 0.4472136 seconds

3. Multiply by 2 * pi:
   T = 2 * 3.14159265 * 0.4472136
   T ≈ 2.8099 seconds

The time period of the pendulum is approximately 2.81 seconds.

To calculate the velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane using the principle of conservation of energy, we follow these steps:

1. Identify the given values:
- Mass of the solid sphere (m) = 2.0 kg
- Initial height (h) = 7.0 meters
- Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 10.0 m/s^2

2. Conservation of Mechanical Energy:
Since the sphere starts from rest, its initial mechanical energy is entirely potential energy (PE):
PE = m * g * h

At the bottom of the plane, all the potential energy is converted into kinetic energy. Because the sphere rolls without slipping, its total kinetic energy (KE) consists of both translational kinetic energy and rotational kinetic energy:
Total KE = Translational KE + Rotational KE
Total KE = (1/2) * m * v^2 + (1/2) * I * omega^2

Where:
- v is the linear velocity at the bottom of the plane.
- I is the moment of inertia of a solid sphere, which is given by I = (2/5) * m * r^2 (where r is the radius of the sphere).
- omega is the angular velocity, which relates to linear velocity for rolling without slipping by omega = v / r.

3. Express Total KE in terms of linear velocity (v):
Substitute I and omega into the Rotational KE equation:
Rotational KE = (1/2) * ((2/5) * m * r^2) * (v / r)^2
Rotational KE = (1/5) * m * v^2

Now, combine translational and rotational kinetic energies:
Total KE = (1/2) * m * v^2 + (1/5) * m * v^2
Total KE = (7/10) * m * v^2

4. Equate Initial Energy to Final Energy:
PE = Total KE
m * g * h = (7/10) * m * v^2

Notice that the mass (m) cancels out from both sides:
g * h = (7/10) * v^2

Solve for v^2:
v^2 = (10 / 7) * g * h

5. Calculate the numerical value:
Substitute g = 10.0 m/s^2 and h = 7.0 m into the formula:
v^2 = (10 / 7) * 10.0 * 7.0
v^2 = (10 / 7) * 70.0
v^2 = 100.0

Take the square root of both sides to find v:
v = sqrt(100.0)
v = 10.0 m/s

The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is 10.0 m/s.

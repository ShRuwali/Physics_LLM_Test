To find the velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the inclined plane, we use the principle of conservation of energy. 

1. Initial Potential Energy:
At the height of 7.0 meters, the sphere is at rest, so its energy is entirely gravitational potential energy:
Potential Energy = m * g * h

Where:
- m = mass of the sphere
- g = acceleration due to gravity (10.0 m/s^2)
- h = height of the incline (7.0 m)

2. Final Kinetic Energy:
At the bottom of the plane, the potential energy is converted into kinetic energy. Since the solid sphere rolls down the incline without slipping, its kinetic energy has two components: translational kinetic energy and rotational kinetic energy.

- Translational Kinetic Energy = (1/2) * m * v^2
- Rotational Kinetic Energy = (1/2) * I * omega^2

For a solid sphere:
- Moment of inertia (I) = (2/5) * m * r^2
- Angular velocity (omega) = v / r

Substituting I and omega into the rotational kinetic energy equation:
Rotational Kinetic Energy = (1/2) * [(2/5) * m * r^2] * (v / r)^2 = (1/5) * m * v^2

Combining translational and rotational kinetic energies:
Total Kinetic Energy = (1/2) * m * v^2 + (1/5) * m * v^2
Total Kinetic Energy = (7/10) * m * v^2

3. Setting Potential Energy Equal to Kinetic Energy:
m * g * h = (7/10) * m * v^2

Notice that mass (m) appears on both sides and cancels out (meaning the mass and size of the sphere do not affect the final linear velocity):
g * h = (7/10) * v^2

Solve for velocity (v):
v^2 = (10 / 7) * g * h
v = sqrt((10 / 7) * g * h)

4. Calculation:
Substitute the given values into the equation:
v = sqrt((10 / 7) * 10.0 * 7.0)
v = sqrt((10 / 7) * 70.0)
v = sqrt(100.0)
v = 10.0 m/s

The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is 10.0 meters per second.

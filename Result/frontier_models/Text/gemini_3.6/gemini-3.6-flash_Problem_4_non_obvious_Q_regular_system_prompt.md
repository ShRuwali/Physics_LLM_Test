To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the vertical circle, we can use Newton's second law for circular motion.

1. Convert all given values into standard SI units:
- Mass of the body (m): 10 nanograms = 10 x 10^-12 kg = 10^-11 kg
- Radius of the circle (R): 5.0 meters
- Speed of the object (v): 5 m/s
- Acceleration due to gravity (g): 10.0 m/s^2
- Radius of the body: 100 nanometers = 10^-7 meters (this value is negligible compared to the 5.0 meter radius of the circle).

2. Analyze the forces at the bottom of the circular path:
At the lowest point of the circle:
- The tension (T) acts upwards towards the center of the circle.
- The gravitational force (mg) acts downwards.

The net inward force provides the required centripetal force:
Net Force = Tension - Gravity = (m * v^2) / R

Rearranging the equation to solve for Tension (T):
Tension = m * g + (m * v^2) / R
Tension = m * (g + v^2 / R)

3. Calculate the centripetal acceleration (v^2 / R):
v^2 / R = (5)^2 / 5.0 = 25 / 5.0 = 5.0 m/s^2

4. Calculate the total acceleration felt at the bottom:
Total acceleration = g + v^2 / R = 10.0 m/s^2 + 5.0 m/s^2 = 15.0 m/s^2

5. Compute the tension:
Tension = (10^-11 kg) * (15.0 m/s^2) = 1.5 x 10^-10 Newtons

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is 1.5 x 10^-10 N (or 0.15 nanonewtons).

To calculate the tension in the string at the bottom of the circular path, we analyze the forces acting on the body at that point.

### 1. Identify the Forces
At the bottom of the vertical circle:
- The tension force (T) acts upwards, towards the center of the circle.
- The gravitational force (mg) acts downwards.

The net upward force provides the required centripetal force for the circular motion:
T - m * g = m * (v^2 / R)

Rearranging for tension (T):
T = m * (g + v^2 / R)

---

### 2. Convert Units to SI
- Mass (m) = 10 nanograms = 10 * 10^-12 kg = 1 * 10^-11 kg
- Radius of the circle (R) = 5.0 meters
- Speed (v) = 5 m/s
- Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 10.0 m/s^2
*(Note: The radius of the body is 100 nanometers, which is 1 * 10^-7 meters. This is negligible compared to the 5.0-meter radius of the circle.)*

---

### 3. Calculate Centripetal Acceleration
Centripetal acceleration (a_c) = v^2 / R
a_c = (5)^2 / 5.0 = 25 / 5.0 = 5.0 m/s^2

---

### 4. Calculate the Tension
T = m * (g + a_c)
T = (1 * 10^-11 kg) * (10.0 m/s^2 + 5.0 m/s^2)
T = (1 * 10^-11 kg) * (15.0 m/s^2)
T = 1.5 * 10^-10 Newtons

---

### Final Answer
The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is **1.5 * 10^-10 N** (or 0.15 nanoNewtons / 150 picoNewtons).

To solve this problem, we use the principle of conservation of linear momentum, which states that the total momentum of a closed system before a collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision.

### 1. Identify the Given Information
* Let the positive direction be to the right (and negative to the left).
* Mass of Block A (m_a) = 100 nanograms
* Initial velocity of Block A (v_a1) = 0 m/s
* Mass of Block B (m_b) = 200 nanograms
* Initial velocity of Block B (v_b1) = -100 m/s (since Block B is on the right moving to the left towards Block A)
* Final velocity of Block B (v_b2) = 10 km/s = 10,000 m/s (moving to the right)

*(Note: The physical dimensions of the blocks are extra information and are not needed since the masses are already explicitly given.)*

---

### 2. Set Up the Conservation of Momentum Equation

Total initial momentum = Total final momentum

(m_a * v_a1) + (m_b * v_b1) = (m_a * v_a2) + (m_b * v_b2)

---

### 3. Calculate the Final Velocity of Block A (v_a2)

Substitute the known values into the equation:

(100 * 0) + (200 * -100) = (100 * v_a2) + (200 * 10,000)

0 - 20,000 = 100 * v_a2 + 2,000,000

-20,000 = 100 * v_a2 + 2,000,000

Subtract 2,000,000 from both sides:

-2,020,000 = 100 * v_a2

Divide by 100:

v_a2 = -20,200 m/s

---

### Conclusion

* The final velocity of Block A is **-20,200 m/s** (or **-20.2 km/s**), which means it is moving to the **left** at a speed of **20,200 m/s** (20.2 km/s).

*(Note: If the initial velocity of Block B is strictly interpreted as +100 m/s to the right despite its leftward motion, the final velocity of Block A would be -19,800 m/s or -19.8 km/s).*

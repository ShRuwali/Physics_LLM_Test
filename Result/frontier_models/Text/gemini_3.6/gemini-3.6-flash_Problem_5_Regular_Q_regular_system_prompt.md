To solve this problem, we use the principle of conservation of linear momentum, which states that the total momentum before the collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision (since there is no friction acting on the track).

1. Conservation of Momentum Equation:
(mass of A * initial velocity of A) + (mass of B * initial velocity of B) = (mass of A * final velocity of A) + (mass of B * final velocity of B)

In symbols:
m_a * v_a1 + m_b * v_b1 = m_a * v_a2 + m_b * v_b2

2. Insert the given values:
- m_a = 0.50 kg
- v_a1 = 1.0 m/s
- m_b = 0.20 kg
- v_b1 = -2.0 m/s
- v_b2 = 1.0 m/s

(0.50 * 1.0) + (0.20 * -2.0) = (0.50 * v_a2) + (0.20 * 1.0)

3. Simplify the initial momentum (left side of the equation):
0.50 - 0.40 = 0.10 kg m/s

4. Set up the full equation with the calculated values:
0.10 = 0.50 * v_a2 + 0.20

5. Solve for v_a2:
0.50 * v_a2 = 0.10 - 0.20
0.50 * v_a2 = -0.10
v_a2 = -0.10 / 0.50
v_a2 = -0.20 m/s

The final velocity of block A is -0.20 m/s. This means block A moves to the left at a speed of 0.20 m/s after the collision.

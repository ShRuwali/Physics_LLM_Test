**Mathematical Solution:**

To find the final velocity of block A, we use the Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum, which states that total initial momentum equals total final momentum for an isolated system.

Let:
- ma = mass of block A = 100 nanograms
- va1 = initial velocity of block A = 1.0 m/s
- mb = mass of block B = 200 nanograms
- vb1 = initial velocity of block B = -2.0 m/s
- vb2 = final velocity of block B = 1.0 m/s
- va2 = final velocity of block A

Initial Momentum = (ma * va1) + (mb * vb1)
Initial Momentum = (100 * 1.0) + (200 * -2.0) = 100 - 400 = -300 nanogram·m/s

Final Momentum = (ma * va2) + (mb * vb2)
Final Momentum = (100 * va2) + (200 * 1.0) = 100 * va2 + 200 nanogram·m/s

Setting Initial Momentum equal to Final Momentum:
-300 = 100 * va2 + 200
-500 = 100 * va2
va2 = -5.0 m/s

Thus, based on momentum conservation, block A moves to the left at a speed of **5.0 m/s** (velocity = **-5.0 m/s**).

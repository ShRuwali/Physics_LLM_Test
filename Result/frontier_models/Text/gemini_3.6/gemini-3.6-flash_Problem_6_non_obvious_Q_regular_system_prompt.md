To solve this problem, we will use the principles of simple harmonic motion for an ideal spring system.

---

### Step 1: Calculate the Force Constant of the Spring

According to Hooke's Law, the force exerted by a spring is directly proportional to its displacement:
Force = k * x

Where:
* Force = 5.0 N
* Displacement (x) = 0.020 meters
* k is the force constant of the spring

Rearranging the formula to solve for k:
k = Force / x
k = 5.0 N / 0.020 m
k = 250 N/m

---

### Step 2: Convert Mass to Kilograms

The mass of the block is given as 500 nanograms.
* 1 nanogram = 10^-9 grams = 10^-12 kilograms
* Mass (m) = 500 * 10^-12 kg = 5.0 * 10^-10 kg

*(Note: The dimensions of the block do not affect the frequency or force constant of the linear harmonic oscillator, as long as the mass is known.)*

---

### Step 3: Calculate the Angular Frequency

The angular frequency (omega) of a spring-mass system is given by:
omega = sqrt(k / m)

Plugging in the values:
omega = sqrt(250 / (5.0 * 10^-10))
omega = sqrt(5.0 * 10^11)
omega ≈ 707,106.78 rad/s (or 7.07 * 10^5 rad/s)

---

### Step 4: Calculate the Frequency of Oscillation

The frequency (f) is related to the angular frequency by:
f = omega / (2 * pi)

Plugging in the value of angular frequency:
f = 707,106.78 / (2 * 3.14159265)
f ≈ 112,539.54 Hz (or 112.54 kHz)

---

### Summary of Results:

* **Force constant of the spring:** 250 N/m
* **Angular frequency:** 707,106.78 rad/s (7.07 * 10^5 rad/s)
* **Frequency of oscillation:** 112,539.54 Hz (112.54 kHz)

To calculate the tension in the cable, we need to consider the forces acting on the submerged block in equilibrium (at rest):

1. **Downward force:** Weight of the block (W) acting due to gravity.
2. **Upward forces:** 
   - Tension in the cable (T)
   - Buoyant force (B) exerted by the sea water

### **Given:**
* Mass of the block (m) = 20.0 kg
* Density of the block (D) = 19.0 x 10^3 kg/m^3 = 19000 kg/m^3
* Density of sea water (d) = 1.03 x 10^3 kg/m^3 = 1030 kg/m^3
* Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 10 m/s^2

---

### **Step 1: Calculate the Weight of the Block (W)**
Weight (W) = m * g
W = 20.0 kg * 10 m/s^2 = 200 N

---

### **Step 2: Calculate the Volume of the Block (V)**
Volume (V) = m / D
V = 20.0 kg / 19000 kg/m^3 = 0.0010526 m^3

---

### **Step 3: Calculate the Buoyant Force (B)**
The buoyant force is equal to the weight of the sea water displaced by the submerged volume of the block:
B = d * V * g
B = 1030 kg/m^3 * 0.0010526 m^3 * 10 m/s^2
B = 10.84 N

---

### **Step 4: Calculate the Tension in the Cable (T)**
Since the block is at rest, the total upward forces must equal the total downward forces:
T + B = W

Rearranging to solve for Tension (T):
T = W - B
T = 200 N - 10.84 N
T = 189.16 N

---

### **Answer:**
The tension in the cable is **189.16 N** (or approximately **189 N**).

To calculate the gravitational force between Planet A and its moon, we can use Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:

F = (G * M * m) / d^2

Where:
- F is the gravitational force.
- G is the gravitational constant, approximately equal to 6.6743 x 10^-11 N m^2 / kg^2.
- M is the mass of Planet A.
- m is the mass of the moon.
- d is the distance between the centers of the two bodies.

Step 1: Convert all quantities to standard SI units (kilograms and meters)
- Mass of Planet A (M): 6000 nanograms = 6000 x 10^-12 kg = 6.0 x 10^-9 kg
- Mass of the Moon (m): 2.0 x 10^22 kg
- Center-to-center distance (d): 3.0 x 10^9 m

Step 2: Calculate the square of the distance (d^2)
d^2 = (3.0 x 10^9 m)^2 = 9.0 x 10^18 m^2

Step 3: Substitute the values into Newton's formula
F = (6.6743 x 10^-11 * 6.0 x 10^-9 * 2.0 x 10^22) / (9.0 x 10^18)

Numerator:
6.6743 x 10^-11 * 6.0 x 10^-9 * 2.0 x 10^22 = 8009.16

Divide by the denominator:
F = 8009.16 / (9.0 x 10^18)
F = 8.899 x 10^-16 Newtons

Conclusion:
The gravitational force between Planet A and its moon at this location is approximately 8.9 x 10^-16 Newtons (or 8.899 x 10^-16 N).

To find the gravitational force between Planet A and its moon, we use Newton's law of universal gravitation:

Force = (G * M * m) / d^2

Where:
- G is the gravitational constant, approximately 6.674 x 10^-11 N*m^2/kg^2
- M is the mass of Planet A
- m is the mass of the moon
- d is the distance between the centers of the two bodies

Step 1: Convert all given units to standard SI units (kilograms and meters).
- Mass of Planet A (M): 6000 nanograms 
  1 nanogram = 10^-12 kilograms
  M = 6000 x 10^-12 kg = 6.0 x 10^-9 kg
- Mass of the moon (m): 2.0 x 10^22 kg
- Distance between centers (d): 3.0 x 10^9 meters

Note: Since both bodies are spherically symmetric and the distance provided is center-to-center, the radius of Planet A is not needed for this calculation.

Step 2: Calculate d^2.
d^2 = (3.0 x 10^9 meters)^2 = 9.0 x 10^18 m^2

Step 3: Calculate the gravitational force (Force).
Numerator = G * M * m
Numerator = (6.674 x 10^-11) * (6.0 x 10^-9) * (2.0 x 10^22)
Numerator = 8.0088 x 10^3 N*m^2

Force = (8.0088 x 10^3) / (9.0 x 10^18)
Force ≈ 8.8987 x 10^-16 Newtons

Rounding to two significant figures (matching the given values):
The gravitational force between Planet A and its moon is approximately 8.9 x 10^-16 Newtons.

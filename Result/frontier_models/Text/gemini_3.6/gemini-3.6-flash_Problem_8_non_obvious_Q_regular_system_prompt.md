To calculate the gravitational force between Planet A and its moon, we use Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:

F = (G * M * m) / d^2

Where:
- F is the gravitational force.
- G is the gravitational constant, approximately 6.674 x 10^-11 N·m^2/kg^2.
- M is the mass of Planet A.
- m is the mass of the moon.
- d is the distance between the centers of the two bodies.

**Step 1: Convert units to standard SI units (kilograms and meters)**
- Mass of Planet A (M): 6000 nanograms = 6000 x 10^-12 kg = 6.0 x 10^-9 kg
- Mass of the moon (m): 2.0 x 10^22 kg
- Distance between centers (d): 3.0 x 10^9 meters

*(Note: The radius of the planet is extra information because the distance d is already measured from center to center.)*

**Step 2: Plug the values into the gravitational force formula**
F = (6.674 x 10^-11 * 6.0 x 10^-9 * 2.0 x 10^22) / (3.0 x 10^9)^2

**Step 3: Simplify the numerator and denominator**
- Numerator = 6.674 x 10^-11 * 6.0 x 10^-9 * 2.0 x 10^22 = 8008.8
- Denominator = (3.0 x 10^9)^2 = 9.0 x 10^18

**Step 4: Calculate the final force**
F = 8008.8 / (9.0 x 10^18)
F ≈ 8.899 x 10^-16 Newtons

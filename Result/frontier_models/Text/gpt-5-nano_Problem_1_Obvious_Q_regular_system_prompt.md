Key idea: For a block on an incline with kinetic friction, the acceleration along the plane is
a = g (sin θ − μk cos θ),
where μk is the (positive) coefficient of kinetic friction and g is the gravitational acceleration.

Notes on the given values:
- The coefficient should be nonnegative; use μk = 0.5 (the negative sign in the prompt is not physical for μk).
- g is taken as 10 m/s^2 downward (the direction sign is handled by the sin and cos terms with θ = 30°).

Compute the acceleration:
- sin 30° = 0.5
- cos 30° = 0.866025...
- a = 10 (0.5 − 0.5 × 0.866025...) = 10 (0.5 − 0.4330127) ≈ 10 × 0.0669873 ≈ 0.6699 m/s^2

Distance along the plane is s = 5 m, starting from rest. For constant acceleration:
t = sqrt(2s / a) = sqrt(10 / 0.6699) ≈ sqrt(14.93) ≈ 3.9 s

Answer: about 3.9 seconds.

Note: If you insist on using μk = −0.5 (as written), the model would give a ≈ 9.33 m/s^2 and t ≈ 1.0 s, but that corresponds to an unphysical friction coefficient sign. The physically correct approach uses μk = 0.5.

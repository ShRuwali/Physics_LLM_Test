Short answer: about 3.0010 × 10^8 m/s (classical, sliding without rotation). The given data lead to a result essentially equal to the initial speed because the gravitational energy change mgh is tiny compared with the initial kinetic energy for such a small mass.

What to use
- If the sphere slides without rotation (no rolling): energy is E = (1/2) m v^2, plus gravitational potential mgh. Conservation gives:
  (1/2) m v0^2 + m g h = (1/2) m v^2
  so v^2 = v0^2 + 2 g h.

- If the sphere rolls without slipping (rotational energy included, I = 2/5 m r^2): total initial KE is (1/2) m v0^2 + (1/2) I ω^2 with ω = v0/r, so KE_i = (7/10) m v0^2. At the bottom, KE_f = (7/10) m v^2. Energy conservation gives:
  (7/10) m v0^2 + m g h = (7/10) m v^2
  hence v^2 = v0^2 + (10/7) g h.

Given numbers
- m = 4 ng = 4×10^-12 kg (mass cancels in the sliding case)
- v0 = 3.001×10^8 m/s
- h = 6×10^8 m
- g ≈ 10 m/s^2

Compute 2 g h = 2×10×6×10^8 = 1.2×10^10
v0^2 = (3.001×10^8)^2 ≈ 9.006001×10^16

- Sliding (no rotation): v^2 ≈ 9.006001×10^16 + 1.2×10^10 ≈ 9.0060022×10^16
  v ≈ sqrt(9.0060022×10^16) ≈ 3.00100037×10^8 m/s

- Rolling (without slipping): v^2 ≈ v0^2 + (10/7) g h
  (10/7) g h ≈ (10/7)×6×10^9 ≈ 8.571×10^9
  v^2 ≈ 9.006001×10^16 + 8.571×10^9 ≈ 9.006009×10^16
  v ≈ sqrt(9.006009×10^16) ≈ 3.00100014×10^8 m/s

Notes
- The problem as stated yields speeds near the initial speed; the gravitational term mgh is tiny compared with the initial kinetic energy because the mass is extremely small.
- The numbers imply a speed greater than the actual speed of light, which would violate relativity. A fully accurate treatment would require relativistic mechanics, especially at such high speeds. The Newtonian result is still the answer to the question as posed (classical energy conservation).

Conclusion
- If sliding: bottom velocity about 3.00100037 × 10^8 m/s.
- If rolling without slipping: bottom velocity about 3.00100014 × 10^8 m/s.
In both cases the result is essentially 3.001 × 10^8 m/s.

Assume the sphere rolls without slipping down the incline.

- I for a solid sphere: I = (2/5) m R^2, so for rolling, the kinetic energy is K = (1/2) m v^2 + (1/2) I ω^2 = (1/2 + 1/5) m v^2 = (7/10) m v^2 when v = ωR.
- Energy conservation from top (height h) to bottom gives:
  (7/10) m v_i^2 + m g h = (7/10) m v_f^2
  Therefore v_f^2 = v_i^2 + (10/7) g h.

Plug in the given values:
- v_i = 3.001 × 10^8 m/s
- g = 10 m/s^2
- h = 6 × 10^8 m

Compute (10/7) g h = (10/7) × 10 × 6 × 10^8 ≈ 8.5714 × 10^9.

Then v_f^2 = (3.001 × 10^8)^2 + 8.5714 × 10^9
= 9.006001 × 10^16 + 8.5714 × 10^9.

Thus v_f ≈ sqrt(9.006001 × 10^16 + 8.5714 × 10^9) ≈ 3.00100014 × 10^8 m/s.

So the velocity at the bottom is about 3.001 × 10^8 m/s (more precisely ≈ 3.00100014 × 10^8 m/s). The speed increases by roughly 14 m/s from the initial value.

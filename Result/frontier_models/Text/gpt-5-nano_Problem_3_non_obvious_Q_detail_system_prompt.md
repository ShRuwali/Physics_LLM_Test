Key point: If the sphere rolls without slipping (a solid sphere has I = 2/5 mR^2 and v = ωR), then the energy conservation between the top and bottom gives a different factor than pure translation.

Assuming rolling without slipping:
- Energy at the top: E_top = 1/2 m v0^2 + 1/2 I ω^2 + m g h
  with ω = v0 / R, I = 2/5 mR^2, so E_top = (7/10) m v0^2 + m g h
- Energy at the bottom: E_bottom = 1/2 m v^2 + 1/2 I ω^2 = (7/10) m v^2

Conservation (E_bottom = E_top) gives:
v^2 = v0^2 + (10/7) g h

Plugging in:
- v0 = 3.001 x 10^8 m/s
- g = 10 m/s^2
- h = 6 x 10^8 m

(10/7) g h = (10/7) * 10 * 6e8 = 8.5714286 x 10^9 (m^2/s^2)

v^2 = (3.001e8)^2 + 8.5714286e9
     = 9.006001e16 + 8.5714286e9
     ≈ 9.00600185714e16

v ≈ sqrt(9.00600185714e16) ≈ 3.00100014285 x 10^8 m/s

So the velocity at the bottom is about 3.00100014 x 10^8 m/s (roughly 3.001 x 10^8 m/s).

Important note: This result exceeds the speed of light (c ≈ 3.00 x 10^8 m/s). Classical energy conservation like this is not valid when speeds approach c; relativistic effects would be essential. Hence, although the calculation is straightforward classically, the scenario as stated is not physically realizable. If you instead assume sliding without rotation (no rotation energy), the result would be only slightly different (v ≈ 3.00100002 x 10^8 m/s).

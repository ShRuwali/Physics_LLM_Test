Assumptions:
- The solid sphere rolls without slipping down the incline.
- I, the moment of inertia, for a solid sphere is 2/5 m r^2.
- Rolling without slipping gives ω = v / r, so rotational energy contributes.

Compute:
- Radius r = diameter / 2 = 4 nm = 4 x 10^-9 m.
- Initial translational KE = (1/2) m v0^2.
- Initial rotational KE = (1/2) I ω0^2 = (1/2) (2/5 m r^2) (v0^2 / r^2) = (1/5) m v0^2.
- Initial potential energy: U0 = m g h.

Thus initial total energy E_i = (1/2 + 1/5) m v0^2 + m g h = (7/10) m v0^2 + m g h.

Final energy when speed is v: E_f = (1/2) m v^2 + (1/2) I ω^2 = (7/10) m v^2.

Energy conservation: (7/10) m v^2 = (7/10) m v0^2 + m g h.

Solve for v^2:
- v^2 = v0^2 + (10/7) g h.

Plug in numbers:
- v0 = 3.001 x 10^8 m/s
- g = 10 m/s^2
- h = 6 x 10^8 m

Compute (10/7) g h = (10/7) * 10 * 6 x 10^8 ≈ 8.5714286 x 10^9.

v^2 ≈ (3.001 x 10^8)^2 + 8.5714286 x 10^9
     ≈ 9.006001 x 10^16 + 8.5714286 x 10^9
     ≈ 9.006001857 x 10^16.

v ≈ sqrt(9.006001857 x 10^16) ≈ 3.00100014 x 10^8 m/s.

Answer: The velocity at the bottom is about 3.00100014 x 10^8 meters per second (roughly 3.001 x 10^8 m/s). Note this is extremely close to the initial speed; the potential energy change is tiny compared to the initial kinetic energy. Also, at this speed classical mechanics is not strictly valid (relativity would be important).

Assuming the sphere rolls without slipping, its kinetic energy is the sum of translational and rotational energy. For a solid sphere,

Total kinetic energy = (7/10) m v².

Conservation of energy gives:

(7/10) m vi² + mgh = (7/10) m vf²

Therefore,

vf = square root of [vi² + (10/7)gh]

Substituting vi = 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s, g = 10 m/s², and h = 6 × 10⁸ m:

vf = square root of [(3.001 × 10⁸)² + 8.571 × 10⁹]

vf ≈ 3.00100014 × 10⁸ m/s.

However, this is only a formal classical result. The initial velocity, 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s, is greater than the speed of light, 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s. A massive sphere cannot have this initial velocity, so the stated situation is physically impossible and has no valid relativistic answer.

If rotation is ignored and the sphere is treated as a sliding particle, the formal classical result would instead be approximately 3.00100020 × 10⁸ m/s.

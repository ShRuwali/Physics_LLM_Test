Assuming the block begins sliding and that air resistance is negligible:

Down-slope gravitational acceleration:

g sin(30°) = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m/s²

Acceleration opposing motion due to kinetic friction:

μk g cos(30°) = 0.5 × 10 × 0.866 = 4.33 m/s²

Therefore, the net acceleration is:

a = 5 − 4.33 = 0.670 m/s²

Starting from rest and moving 5 m:

5 = 0.5 × 0.670 × t²

Thus:

t = 3.86 s

Answer: approximately 3.9 seconds.

Strictly, the coefficient of static friction is needed to determine whether the block can begin moving from rest. The answer above follows the statement that it does slide. Also, at a size of 1 nanometer, the classical friction model may not be physically realistic because atomic-scale surface forces become important. The specified mass and dimensions also imply an extraordinarily high density, though this does not affect the idealized calculation.

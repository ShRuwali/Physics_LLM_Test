For small angular displacements, the period of a simple pendulum is

T = 2π times the square root of L divided by g.

Using L = 2 m and g = 10 m/s²:

T = 2π times the square root of 2/10  
T ≈ 2.81 s

The finite radius of the solid sphere gives an utterly negligible correction, about one part in 10²⁰. The mass also cancels out.

Therefore, the time period is approximately 2.81 seconds.

Physical caveat: A mass of 2.5 nanograms compressed into a 1-nanometer-diameter sphere implies a density of about 4.8 × 10¹⁵ kg/m³. No ordinary solid can have this density, so the stated bob is not physically realistic under known material physics. Nevertheless, as an idealized classical pendulum problem, it is mathematically solvable.

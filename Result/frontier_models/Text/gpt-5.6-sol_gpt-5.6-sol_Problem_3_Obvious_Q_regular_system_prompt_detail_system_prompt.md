The stated problem is not physically possible:

- The initial speed is 3.001 × 10^8 m/s, which exceeds the speed of light, approximately 2.998 × 10^8 m/s. A massive sphere cannot reach or exceed light speed.
- A height of 6 × 10^8 m is far beyond Earth’s radius, so treating gravitational acceleration as a constant 10 m/s² over that distance is unrealistic.
- The phrase “solid sphere” suggests rolling, but the problem does not explicitly say whether it rolls without slipping or simply slides.

Using classical conservation of energy anyway:

If the sphere slides without rotating:

Final speed squared = initial speed squared + 2gh

Final speed = square root of [(3.001 × 10^8)² + 2(10)(6 × 10^8)]

Final speed ≈ 3.00100020 × 10^8 m/s.

If the sphere rolls without slipping, its rotational energy must also be included. For a solid sphere, the total kinetic energy is 7/10 times mass times speed squared. This gives:

Final speed squared = initial speed squared + (10/7)gh

Final speed ≈ 3.00100014 × 10^8 m/s.

Thus, the formal classical result is approximately 3.001 × 10^8 m/s, depending slightly on whether it slides or rolls. However, both answers exceed the speed of light, so there is no physically valid velocity for the stated conditions. The problem cannot be solved realistically because its initial condition already violates special relativity.

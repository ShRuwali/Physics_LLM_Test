No physically valid time can be determined from the stated conditions.

For a plane sloping downward at 30°:

- The upward gravitational acceleration has a component of about 5.0 m/s² up the plane, opposing downward motion.
- It also has a component of about 8.66 m/s² away from the plane. A normal force from the plane cannot pull the block toward the plane, so the block would immediately lose contact.
- A kinetic friction coefficient of −0.50 is not physically meaningful. Friction coefficients are nonnegative, and kinetic friction opposes motion. A negative value would represent an active propulsive force rather than friction.

Therefore, the block cannot physically slide 5.0 m along the plane under the stated conditions, and the total travel time is undefined.

If the intended problem had ordinary downward gravity and a positive coefficient, the time could be calculated using constant-acceleration kinematics. But using the given upward gravity and negative friction, any numerical result would not be physically justified.

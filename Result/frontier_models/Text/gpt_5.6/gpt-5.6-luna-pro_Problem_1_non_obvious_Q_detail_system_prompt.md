The forces along the incline are:

- Downward component of gravity: m g sin(30°)
- Kinetic friction upward: μk m g cos(30°)

Thus, the acceleration down the plane is:

a = g[sin(30°) − μk cos(30°)]

Substituting the values:

a = 10[0.5 − 0.50(0.866)]  
a ≈ 0.670 m/s²

Starting from rest and traveling 5.0 m:

s = ½at²

Therefore:

t = √(2s/a)  
t = √(10.0/0.670)  
t ≈ 3.86 s

The total time taken is approximately 3.9 seconds.

The mass cancels out of the calculation. This result is physically valid within the idealized model of a rigid block with a constant kinetic-friction coefficient. However, a 1 nm block with a mass of 1 nanogram would have an extraordinarily unrealistic density, and ordinary macroscopic friction laws may not accurately apply at that scale.

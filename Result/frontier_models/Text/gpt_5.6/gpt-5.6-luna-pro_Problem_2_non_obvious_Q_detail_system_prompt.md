For a simple pendulum undergoing small oscillations, the time period is

T = 2π√(L/g)

Using L = 2.0 m and g = 10.0 m/s²:

T = 2π√(2.0/10.0) ≈ 2.81 s

Therefore, the time period is approximately:

**2.8 seconds**

The mass and diameter of the spherical bob do not affect the ideal simple-pendulum period, provided the bob can be treated as a point mass and the string is massless and inextensible.

However, the stated physical parameters are highly unrealistic. A 1 nanometer diameter sphere with a mass of 2.5 nanograms would have an extraordinarily high density, about 4.8 × 10¹⁵ kg/m³, far beyond the density of ordinary solid matter. Thus, the calculated period is mathematically valid for the ideal pendulum model, but such a physical bob is not realistically realizable as an ordinary solid sphere.

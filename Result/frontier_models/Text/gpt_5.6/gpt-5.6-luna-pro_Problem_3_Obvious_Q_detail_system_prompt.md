Assuming the sphere slides without friction and does not rotate, conservation of Newtonian mechanical energy gives:

Initial kinetic energy + initial potential energy = final kinetic energy

Therefore,

v_final² = v_initial² + 2gh

Using the given values:

v_final² = (3.001 × 10^8)² + 2(10.0)(6.0 × 10^8)

v_final² = 9.0060022 × 10^16

Thus,

v_final ≈ 3.0010002 × 10^8 m/s

So the Newtonian result is approximately:

**v_final ≈ 300,100,020 m/s**

However, this result is not physically valid.

The speed of light is approximately 299,792,458 m/s. The stated initial speed, 300,100,000 m/s, is already greater than the speed of light. A massive object cannot travel at or above the speed of light according to special relativity. Therefore, the given initial condition is physically impossible, and the calculated final velocity is also impossible.

There are additional physical issues:

- A constant gravitational acceleration of 10.0 m/s² cannot be used over a height of 6.0 × 10^8 m. Earth’s gravitational field decreases strongly with altitude.
- A solid sphere with mass 4 nanograms and diameter 8 nanometers would have an extremely large density, approximately 1.5 × 10^13 kg/m³, which is not realistic for an ordinary solid.
- If the sphere rolls rather than slides, rotational kinetic energy must be included, and the result would be different.
- The mass and diameter cancel out in the frictionless sliding calculation.

Thus, **the formal Newtonian answer is 3.0010002 × 10^8 m/s, but it is not physically justifiable because the specified initial speed already exceeds the speed of light.**

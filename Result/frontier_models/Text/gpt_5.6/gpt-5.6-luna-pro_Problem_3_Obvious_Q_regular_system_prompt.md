Assuming the sphere moves without rotation and there is no energy loss, conservation of mechanical energy gives:

Initial kinetic energy + initial gravitational potential energy  
= final kinetic energy + final gravitational potential energy

Taking the bottom as zero gravitational potential:

v_final = sqrt(v_initial² + 2gh)

Substituting:

- Initial velocity = 3.001 × 10^8 m/s
- g = 10.0 m/s²
- h = 6.0 × 10^8 m

v_final = sqrt[(3.001 × 10^8)² + 2(10.0)(6.0 × 10^8)]

v_final ≈ 3.0010002 × 10^8 m/s

Therefore, the classical result is approximately:

**v_final ≈ 3.0010002 × 10^8 m/s**

The mass and diameter cancel out in this calculation. The gravitational potential energy lost is only 0.024 J, which is negligible compared with the initial kinetic energy.

However, there is an important physical issue: the given initial speed, 3.001 × 10^8 m/s, is greater than the speed of light, approximately 2.998 × 10^8 m/s. Thus, this initial condition is not physically possible for a massive sphere. The calculated value is only the result of applying classical Newtonian energy conservation formally.

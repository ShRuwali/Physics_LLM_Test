At the bottom of the circle, the tension must provide the upward centripetal force as well as balance the downward weight:

T − mg = mv²/R

Therefore,

T = m(v²/R + g)

Using:

- m = 10 nanograms = 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ kg
- v = 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s
- R = 5.0 m
- g = 10.0 m/s²

The classical calculation gives:

T = (1.0 × 10⁻¹¹)[(3.001 × 10⁸)²/5.0 + 10.0]

T ≈ 1.80 × 10⁵ N

So, under classical mechanics, the tension would be approximately:

180,000 N

However, this result is not physically valid. The given speed, 3.001 × 10⁸ m/s, is greater than the speed of light, approximately 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s. A body with nonzero mass cannot travel at or above the speed of light. Consequently, the described motion is physically impossible, and no physically meaningful tension exists for the stated parameters.

The body’s radius of 100 nanometers is negligible compared with the 5.0 m circular radius, assuming R refers to the path of the body’s center of mass.

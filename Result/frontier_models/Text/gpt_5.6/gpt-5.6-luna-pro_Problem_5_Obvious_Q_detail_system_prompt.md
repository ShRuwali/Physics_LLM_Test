Take rightward as positive.

There is a contradiction in the statement: block B is described as moving left, but its velocity is also stated as 100 m/s to the right. Since the blocks are supposed to move toward each other, assume:

- Block A: va1 = 0 m/s
- Block B: vb1 = −100 m/s
- Block B after collision: vb2 = +10 km/s = +10,000 m/s
- mA = 100 ng
- mB = 200 ng

Using conservation of momentum:

mA va1 + mB vb1 = mA va2 + mB vb2

Solving for va2:

va2 = [mA va1 + mB vb1 − mB vb2] / mA

Substituting:

va2 = [100(0) + 200(−100) − 200(10,000)] / 100

va2 = −20,200 m/s

Therefore, the final velocity of block A is:

**va2 = −20.2 km/s**

The negative sign means block A moves to the left after the collision.

However, this result is not physically possible for an ordinary passive collision with no external energy input. The final kinetic energy is far greater than the initial kinetic energy, so the collision would need to be explosive or involve stored internal energy. Momentum conservation alone gives the stated result, but the collision is not physically justifiable as a normal elastic or inelastic collision.

If instead block B was really moving right initially at +100 m/s, the result would be −19.8 km/s, but then the blocks would not be moving toward each other.

Take the direction to the right as positive.

Using conservation of linear momentum:

Initial momentum:
- Block A: 100 ng × 1.0 m/s = 100 ng·m/s
- Block B: 200 ng × −2.0 m/s = −400 ng·m/s

Total initial momentum = −300 ng·m/s.

Final momentum:
- Block A: 100 ng × va2
- Block B: 200 ng × 1.0 m/s = 200 ng·m/s

Therefore,

100 va2 + 200 = −300

So,

va2 = −5.0 m/s

The final velocity of block A is:

**va2 = −5.0 m/s**

Thus, block A moves to the left at 5.0 m/s.

Physical note: The block dimensions are not needed for this momentum calculation. Also, the specified final velocities increase the total kinetic energy: the initial kinetic energy is 450 ng·m²/s², while the final kinetic energy is 1350 ng·m²/s². Therefore, this cannot be an ordinary passive collision with no internal energy release. It would require a superelastic collision, such as an explosion or stored energy being released during impact.

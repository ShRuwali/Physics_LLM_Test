The given data are inconsistent, so a unique numerical force constant cannot be determined.

For an ideal spring,

F = kx

where k is the force constant.

If the statement is taken literally, a force of 0 N produces a displacement of 20 m. Then

k = F/x = 0/20 = 0 N/m.

However, a spring with k = 0 has no restoring force. Therefore, the block would not oscillate after being released. The predicted angular frequency and frequency would be

ω = sqrt(k/m) = 0 rad/s

and

f = ω/(2π) = 0 Hz.

This contradicts the statement that the block oscillates.

The physically correct conclusion is that the problem is missing information or contains a typographical error. A nonzero force-displacement measurement, such as a force F at a displacement x, is required to find k. Once k is known:

- Mass: 500 ng = 5.0 × 10^−10 kg
- Angular frequency: ω = sqrt(k/(5.0 × 10^−10)) rad/s
- Frequency: f = [1/(2π)] sqrt(k/(5.0 × 10^−10)) Hz

The 0.040 m displacement is the oscillation amplitude and does not affect the frequency for an ideal linear spring.

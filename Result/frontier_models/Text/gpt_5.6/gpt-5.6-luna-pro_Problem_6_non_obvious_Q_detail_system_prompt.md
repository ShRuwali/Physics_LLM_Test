Given:

- Force, F = 5.0 N
- Displacement, x = 0.020 m
- Mass, m = 500 nanograms = 5.00 × 10⁻¹⁰ kg

Using Hooke’s law:

F = kx

Therefore, the spring constant is:

k = F/x = 5.0/0.020 = 250 N/m

So:

- Spring constant: 250 N/m

For a mass-spring oscillator, the angular frequency is:

ω = √(k/m)

ω = √(250/(5.00 × 10⁻¹⁰))

ω ≈ 7.07 × 10⁵ rad/s

The frequency is:

f = ω/(2π)

f ≈ (7.07 × 10⁵)/(2π)

f ≈ 1.13 × 10⁵ Hz

Therefore:

- Angular frequency: 7.07 × 10⁵ rad/s
- Frequency: 1.13 × 10⁵ Hz, or approximately 113 kHz

The displacement of 0.040 m is the oscillation amplitude. It does not affect the frequency as long as the spring obeys Hooke’s law over that displacement.

Physical considerations:

- The 400 nm dimensions of the block are not needed for the calculation because its mass is already given.
- A 500 nanogram mass with dimensions of 400 nm × 400 nm × 400 nm would have a density of approximately 7.8 × 10⁹ kg/m³, which is extraordinarily high and not realistic for ordinary matter.
- Also, applying a force of 10 N at a displacement of 0.040 m assumes the spring remains perfectly linear far beyond the measured displacement. Thus, the result is mathematically valid under the ideal-spring model, but the physical setup is highly idealized.

For a fully immersed block at rest, the forces are:

- Weight downward: m g
- Buoyant force upward: d V g
- Cable tension upward: T

Thus,

T + d V g = m g

so

T = m g − d V g

The block’s volume is:

V = (100 nm)³ = (1.0 × 10⁻⁷ m)³ = 1.0 × 10⁻²¹ m³

The stated mass is:

1000 nanograms = 1.0 × 10⁻⁹ kg

The buoyant force is:

d V g = (1.03 × 10³)(1.0 × 10⁻²¹)(10)
= 1.03 × 10⁻¹⁷ N

The weight is:

m g = (1.0 × 10⁻⁹)(10)
= 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ N

Therefore,

T = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ − 1.03 × 10⁻¹⁷
≈ 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ N

Answer: T ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ N

However, the data are inconsistent. A cube of side 100 nm with mass 1.0 × 10⁻⁹ kg would have a density of about 1.0 × 10¹² kg/m³, not 1.0 × 10⁹ kg/m³. If the stated density and dimensions are used instead, the mass would be 1.0 × 10⁻¹² kg and the tension would be approximately 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ N. Also, a density of 1.0 × 10⁹ kg/m³ is extraordinarily high and is not physically realistic for an ordinary material.

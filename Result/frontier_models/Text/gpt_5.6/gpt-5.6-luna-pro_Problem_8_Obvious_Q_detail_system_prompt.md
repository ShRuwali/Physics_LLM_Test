Convert the planet’s mass:

- 6000 nanograms = 6.0 × 10⁻⁹ kg
- Moon mass, m = 2.0 × 10²² kg
- Center-to-center distance, d = 3.0 × 10⁹ m

Using Newton’s law of universal gravitation:

F = G M m / d²

with G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²,

F = (6.674 × 10⁻¹¹)(6.0 × 10⁻⁹)(2.0 × 10²²) / (3.0 × 10⁹)²

F ≈ 8.9 × 10⁻¹⁶ N

Therefore, the gravitational force is approximately:

8.9 × 10⁻¹⁶ N

The force on the planet and the force on the moon have this same magnitude but act in opposite directions.

Because the moon’s path is square-shaped, its distance from the planet changes along the path, so the gravitational force would not remain constant. The value above applies at the stated closest distance. A square orbit cannot occur naturally under gravity alone; it would require external forces or constraints. The calculation itself is physically valid provided the given distance is much larger than the bodies’ radii, allowing them to be treated as point masses.

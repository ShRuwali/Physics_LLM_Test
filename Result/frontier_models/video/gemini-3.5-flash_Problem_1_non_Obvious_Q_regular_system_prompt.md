### **Video Description**
The video shows a small, blue cubic block (with dimensions $1\text{ nm} \times 1\text{ nm} \times 1\text{ nm}$ and a mass of $1\text{ nanogram}$) sliding down a $30^\circ$ inclined plane. The block travels a total distance of $5\text{ meters}$ down the slope against a coefficient of kinetic friction ($\mu = 0.5$). 

---

### **Calculation**

#### **1. Given Values:**
*   Distance ($s$) = $5\text{ m}$
*   Angle of incline ($\theta$) = $30^\circ$
*   Coefficient of kinetic friction ($\mu$) = $0.5$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10\text{ m/s}^2$
*   Initial velocity ($u$) = $0\text{ m/s}$ (starts from rest)

*(Note: In classical mechanics, the mass and nanoscale dimensions of the block do not affect its acceleration down the incline and are not required for this calculation).*

---

#### **2. Find the Acceleration ($a$) down the incline:**
The net force acting along the incline is:
$$F_{\text{net}} = mg \sin\theta - f_k$$

Where the kinetic friction force is:
$$f_k = \mu N = \mu mg \cos\theta$$

By Newton's second law ($F_{\text{net}} = ma$):
$$ma = mg \sin\theta - \mu mg \cos\theta$$

Dividing both sides by mass ($m$):
$$a = g(\sin\theta - \mu \cos\theta)$$

Substitute the given values into the equation:
$$a = 10 \cdot \left(\sin(30^\circ) - 0.5 \cdot \cos(30^\circ)\right)$$
$$a = 10 \cdot \left(0.5 - 0.5 \cdot 0.866\right)$$
$$a = 10 \cdot (0.5 - 0.433)$$
$$a \approx 0.67\text{ m/s}^2 \quad (\text{or precisely } 0.6699\text{ m/s}^2)$$

---

#### **3. Find the Time ($t$):**
Using the kinematic equation for distance:
$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$

Since the block starts from rest ($u = 0$):
$$5 = \frac{1}{2}(0.6699)t^2$$
$$t^2 = \frac{10}{0.6699}$$
$$t^2 \approx 14.928$$
$$t \approx \sqrt{14.928} \approx 3.86\text{ seconds}$$

### **Answer:**
The total time taken by the block to travel 5 meters is approximately **$3.86\text{ seconds}$**.

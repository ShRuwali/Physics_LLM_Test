### **Calculation of the Time Period ($T$)**

For small angular displacements, the time period of a simple pendulum is given by the formula:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

**Given values:**
* Distance ($L$) = $2\text{ m}$
* Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10\text{ m/s}^2$

*(Note: The mass and diameter of the sphere are not needed for a simple pendulum calculation as the period is independent of mass and size when $R \ll L$.)*

**Step-by-step substitution:**
1. Substitute the values into the formula:
   $$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2}{10}}$$
2. Simplify the term inside the square root:
   $$T = 2\pi \sqrt{0.2}$$
3. Calculate the numerical value ($\sqrt{0.2} \approx 0.4472$):
   $$T \approx 2 \times 3.1416 \times 0.4472$$
   $$T \approx 2.81\text{ seconds}$$

### **Conclusion**
The time period of oscillation for small displacements is approximately **$2.81\text{ seconds}$** (or exactly $\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{5}}\text{ s}$).

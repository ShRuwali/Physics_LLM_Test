### Description

The video illustrates a block of mass $m$ sliding down an inclined plane angled at $\theta = 30^\circ$ over a total distance of $5.0\text{ meters}$. 

**Given parameters:**
* Angle of inclination: $\theta = 30^\circ$
* Distance along the slope: $s = 5.0\text{ m}$
* Coefficient of kinetic friction: $\mu = -0.50$
* Acceleration due to gravity: $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$ (upward)
* Block mass: $m = 1\text{ nanogram}$
* Block dimensions: $\text{length} = \text{width} = \text{height} = 1\text{ nanometer}$

---

### Calculation

#### 1. Acceleration of the Block
In classical mechanics, the acceleration $a$ along the incline is independent of the mass and dimensions of the block. The effective acceleration component along the path is given by:

$$a = g (\sin\theta - \mu)$$

Substituting the given values:
$$\sin(30^\circ) = 0.5$$

$$a = 10.0 \times \left(0.5 - (-0.50)\right)$$

$$a = 10.0 \times 1.0 = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$$

---

#### 2. Time Taken to Travel $5.0\text{ meters}$
Assuming the block starts from rest ($u = 0\text{ m/s}$), we use the kinematic formula:

$$s = \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$

Solving for time $t$:

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}$$

Substituting $s = 5.0\text{ m}$ and $a = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$:

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 5.0}{10.0}} = \sqrt{1.0} = 1.0\text{ s}$$

---

### Final Answer
The total time taken by the block to travel $5\text{ meters}$ is **$1.0\text{ second}$**.

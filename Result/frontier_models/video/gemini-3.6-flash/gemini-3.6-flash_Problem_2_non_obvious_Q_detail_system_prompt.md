### Description

The video shows a schematic setup of a pendulum consisting of a sphere of mass $m = 2.5\text{ nanograms}$ and diameter $D = 1\text{ nanometer}$, suspended by a string of negligible mass. The distance from the point of support to the center of the sphere is $L = 2.0\text{ meters}$, and the acceleration due to gravity is given as $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$.

---

### Calculation of Time Period

Since the radius of the sphere ($R = 0.5\text{ nm} = 0.5 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$) is extremely small compared to the length of the suspension ($L = 2.0\text{ m}$), the sphere can be modeled as a point mass attached to a string (a simple pendulum).

The formula for the time period $T$ of a simple pendulum undergoing small angular displacements is:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

Substituting the given values:

$$L = 2.0\text{ m}$$
$$g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$$

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2.0}{10.0}} = 2\pi \sqrt{0.2} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{5}} \approx 2.81\text{ seconds}$$

### Physical Validity and Issues

While the mathematical calculation yields $T \approx 2.81\text{ s}$, the given parameters present severe physical impossibilities:

1. **Unreasonable Mass and Density**:
   - The radius of the sphere is $R = 0.5\text{ nm} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m}$.
   - The volume of the sphere is:
     $$V = \frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi (5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m})^3 \approx 5.24 \times 10^{-28}\text{ m}^3$$
   - The mass is $m = 2.5\text{ nanograms} = 2.5 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg}$.
   - The resulting density of the material is:
     $$\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{2.5 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg}}{5.24 \times 10^{-28}\text{ m}^3} \approx 4.77 \times 10^{15}\text{ kg/m}^3$$
   - This density is approximately $4.8 \times 10^{12}$ times denser than water and approaches nuclear matter density ($\sim 10^{17}\text{ kg/m}^3$). No normal atomic matter can exist at such extreme density without collapsing into exotic state matter like neutron star material.

2. **Conclusion**:
   - The given physical parameters are completely unreasonable and violate known material density limits.
   - Therefore, while mathematically straightforward, the model is **not physically justifiable**.

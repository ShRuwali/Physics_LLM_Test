### Description

The video shows a 2D physics setup on a horizontal surface featuring two blocks labeled **Block A** and **Block B**. Block A is a small micro-scale cube with a mass of $100\text{ ng}$ and side lengths of $200\text{ nm}$, initially at rest ($v_{a1} = 0\text{ m/s}$). Block B is significantly larger with a mass of $0.20\text{ kg}$ and side lengths of $400\text{ nm}$, moving towards Block A before a collision occurs.

---

### Calculation of the Velocity of Block A ($v_{a2}$)

To find the velocity of Block A after the collision, we apply the **Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum**:

$$m_a v_{a1} + m_b v_{b1} = m_a v_{a2} + m_b v_{b2}$$

#### Given Parameters:
* Mass of Block A: $m_a = 100\text{ ng} = 100 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$
* Mass of Block B: $m_b = 0.20\text{ kg}$
* Initial velocity of Block A: $v_{a1} = 0\text{ m/s}$
* Initial velocity of Block B: $v_{b1} = -2.0\text{ m/s}$ (moving leftward toward Block A)
* Final velocity of Block B: $v_{b2} = -10\text{ km/s} = -10,000\text{ m/s}$ (continuing leftward)

#### Derivation:

Rearranging the conservation of momentum formula to solve for $v_{a2}$:

$$v_{a2} = \frac{m_a v_{a1} + m_b v_{b1} - m_b v_{b2}}{m_a}$$

Since $v_{a1} = 0\text{ m/s}$:

$$v_{a2} = \frac{m_b (v_{b1} - v_{b2})}{m_a}$$

Substitute the values into the equation:

$$v_{a2} = \frac{0.20\text{ kg} \times \left(-2.0\text{ m/s} - (-10,000\text{ m/s})\right)}{10^{-10}\text{ kg}}$$

$$v_{a2} = \frac{0.20 \times 9998}{10^{-10}}$$

$$v_{a2} = \frac{1999.6}{10^{-10}} = 1.9996 \times 10^{13}\text{ m/s}$$

### Result
The velocity of Block A after the collision is **$1.9996 \times 10^{13}\text{ m/s}$** to the left (in the direction of Block B's motion).

### Brief Description of the Video

The video is divided into two parts:
* **Top Half (Spring and Spring Balance):** Demonstrates the stretching of a spring using a spring balance. The spring is displaced by $x = 20\text{ meters}$ from its equilibrium position ($x = 0\text{ meters}$), resulting in a force reading of $F = 60\text{ N}$ on the spring balance.
* **Bottom Half (Spring and Block):** Replaces the spring balance with a block of mass $m = 500\text{ nanograms}$ (with dimensions $400\text{ nm} \times 400\text{ nm} \times 400\text{ nm}$). The block is displaced by $0.040\text{ meters}$ from equilibrium and released to undergo simple harmonic motion.

---

### 1. Calculation of the Force Constant ($k$)

From the top half of the video:
* Force applied, $F = 60\text{ N}$
* Displacement, $x = 20\text{ m}$

According to Hooke's law:
$$F = kx$$

Solving for the force constant $k$:
$$k = \frac{F}{x}$$

$$k = \frac{60\text{ N}}{20\text{ m}} = 3\text{ N/m}$$

---

### 2. Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)

From the bottom half of the video:
* Mass of the block, $m = 500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$
* Force constant, $k = 3\text{ N/m}$

#### Angular Frequency ($\omega$)
The formula for the angular frequency of a mass-spring system is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Substituting the values:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{3}{5 \times 10^{-10}}}$$

$$\omega = \sqrt{6 \times 10^9} \approx 77459.67\text{ rad/s}$$

#### Frequency ($f$)
The formula for the frequency of oscillation is:
$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

Substituting the angular frequency:
$$f = \frac{77459.67}{2\pi} \approx 12328.1\text{ Hz}$$

### Video Description

The video demonstrates two experimental setups involving a spring system:
1. **Top half (Spring and spring balance):** A spring balance is used to pull a spring. A force of $F = 5.0\text{ N}$ causes the spring to stretch by a displacement of $x = 0.020\text{ m}$ from its equilibrium position ($x = 0\text{ m}$).
2. **Bottom half (Spring and block):** The spring balance is replaced by a block of mass $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$. The block is stretched to a displacement of $0.040\text{ m}$ and released, causing it to undergo simple harmonic motion (oscillation) about its equilibrium position.

---

### Calculation of Force Constant ($k$)

From the top half of the video:
- Applied Force, $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
- Displacement, $x = 0.020\text{ m}$

Using Hooke's Law, $F = k x$:

$$k = \frac{F}{x}$$

$$k = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = 250\text{ N/m}$$

---

### Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)

From the bottom half of the video:
- Mass of the block, $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$
- Spring constant, $k = 250\text{ N/m}$

#### 1. Angular Frequency ($\omega$)

The formula for the angular frequency of simple harmonic motion is:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Substituting the values:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{0.40\text{ kg}}} = \sqrt{625} = 25\text{ rad/s}$$

#### 2. Frequency ($f$)

The frequency of oscillation is given by:

$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

Substituting $\omega = 25\text{ rad/s}$:

$$f = \frac{25}{2\pi}\text{ Hz} \approx 3.98\text{ Hz}$$

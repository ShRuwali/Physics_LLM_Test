### Description of the Video

The video illustrates two spring systems:
1. **Top half ("Spring and spring balance"):** A spring attached to a wall is pulled horizontally by a spring balance. Applying a force $F = 5.0\text{ N}$ causes a displacement of $x = 0.020\text{ m}$ from its equilibrium position ($x = 0\text{ meters}$).
2. **Bottom half ("Spring and block"):** A block of mass $m = 500\text{ nanograms}$ is attached to the same spring. The block is stretched by $0.040\text{ m}$ from the equilibrium position and released, causing it to undergo simple harmonic motion (oscillation) back and forth about $x = 0\text{ meters}$.

---

### Force Constant of the Spring

According to Hooke's Law, the force $F$ required to displace a spring is given by:

$$F = k \cdot x$$

Where:
- $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
- $x = 0.020\text{ m}$
- $k$ is the force constant of the spring.

Rearranging to solve for $k$:

$$k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = 250\text{ N/m}$$

---

### Calculation of Angular Frequency and Frequency

#### Given Data:
- Force constant, $k = 250\text{ N/m}$
- Mass, $m = 500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$

#### 1. Angular Frequency ($\omega$)
The formula for angular frequency in simple harmonic motion is:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Substituting the values:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250}{5 \times 10^{-10}}} = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{11}} \approx 7.071 \times 10^5\text{ rad/s}$$

#### 2. Frequency ($f$)
The relationship between frequency and angular frequency is:

$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

Substituting $\omega$:

$$f = \frac{7.071 \times 10^5}{2\pi} \approx 1.125 \times 10^5\text{ Hz} = 112.5\text{ kHz}$$

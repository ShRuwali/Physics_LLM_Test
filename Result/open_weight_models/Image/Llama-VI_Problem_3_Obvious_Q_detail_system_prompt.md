**Problem Analysis**

The problem involves a solid sphere rolling down an inclined plane without slipping. The initial velocity of the sphere is 3.001 x 10^8 m/s, and we are asked to find its velocity at the bottom of the plane using conservation of energy.

**Conservation of Energy**

Conservation of energy states that the total energy of a closed system remains constant over time. In this case, the total energy of the sphere is the sum of its kinetic energy (KE) and potential energy (PE). Since the sphere starts from rest, its initial kinetic energy is zero. The potential energy is given by:

PE = mgh

where m is the mass of the sphere, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and h is the height of the plane.

**Kinetic Energy**

As the sphere rolls down the plane, its potential energy is converted into kinetic energy. The kinetic energy of the sphere is given by:

KE = (1/2)mv^2

where v is the velocity of the sphere.

**Conservation of Energy Equation**

Since the total energy of the system remains constant, we can set up the following equation:

PE + KE = constant

Substituting the expressions for PE and KE, we get:

mgh + (1/2)mv^2 = constant

**Solving for Velocity**

We are given that the initial velocity of the sphere is 3.001 x 10^8 m/s. We can use this information to find the velocity at the bottom of the plane. Since the sphere starts from rest, we can assume that the initial velocity is zero. Therefore, the constant in the above equation is equal to the initial potential energy:

mgh = (1/2)mv^2

Rearranging the equation to solve for v, we get:

v = sqrt(2gh)

**Calculating Velocity**

Plugging in the values given in the problem, we get:

v = sqrt(2 \* 9.8 m/s^2 \* 6 x 10^8 m) = 1.4 x 10^7 m/s

**Answer**

The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is approximately 1.4 x 10^7 m/s.

**Physical Law Broken**

The problem assumes that the sphere rolls without slipping, which means that the frictional force is zero. However, in reality, there will be some frictional force acting on the sphere, which will slow it down and reduce its velocity. Therefore, the actual velocity of the sphere at the bottom of the plane will be less than the calculated value.

**Conclusion**

The problem can be solved using conservation of energy, but the assumption of no friction is not realistic. Therefore, the calculated velocity is an overestimation of the actual velocity.

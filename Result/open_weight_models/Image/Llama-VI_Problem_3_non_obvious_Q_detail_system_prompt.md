**Problem Analysis**

The problem involves a solid sphere rolling down an inclined plane without slipping. The key information provided includes:

*   Mass of the solid sphere (m) = 4 nanograms
*   Diameter of the sphere = 8 nanometers
*   Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 10 m/s^2
*   Height (h) = 7 meters

**Step 1: Identify the Physical Law**

To solve this problem, we will use the principle of conservation of energy. This principle states that the total energy of a closed system remains constant over time. In this case, the energy of the sphere at the top of the incline is converted into kinetic energy (both translational and rotational) as it rolls down.

**Step 2: Calculate the Potential Energy**

The potential energy (PE) of the sphere at the top of the incline can be calculated using the formula:

PE = m * g * h

where m is the mass of the sphere, g is the acceleration due to gravity, and h is the height of the incline.

PE = 4 ng * 10 m/s^2 * 7 m = 280 J

**Step 3: Calculate the Kinetic Energy**

As the sphere rolls down the incline, its potential energy is converted into kinetic energy. Since the sphere rolls without slipping, we need to consider both the translational and rotational kinetic energies.

Translational Kinetic Energy (TKE) = 0.5 * m * v^2

Rotational Kinetic Energy (RKE) = 0.5 * I * ω^2

where v is the velocity of the sphere, I is the moment of inertia of the sphere, and ω is the angular velocity.

Since the sphere is a rigid body, we can use the following values:

I = (1/2) * m * r^2 = (1/2) * 4 ng * (4 nm)^2 = 0.16 ng*nm^2

We can now calculate the total kinetic energy (KE) as the sum of TKE and RKE:

KE = TKE + RKE = 0.5 * m * v^2 + 0.5 * I * ω^2

**Step 4: Apply Conservation of Energy**

According to the principle of conservation of energy, the total energy at the top of the incline (PE) is equal to the total energy at the bottom of the incline (KE):

PE = KE

Substituting the expressions for PE and KE, we get:

m * g * h = 0.5 * m * v^2 + 0.5 * I * ω^2

Simplifying the equation, we get:

v^2 = 2 * g * h - ω^2

Since the sphere rolls without slipping, the angular velocity (ω) is related to the linear velocity (v) by:

ω = v / r

where r is the radius of the sphere.

Substituting this expression for ω into the previous equation, we get:

v^2 = 2 * g * h - (v / r)^2

Solving for v, we get:

v = sqrt(2 * g * h)

v = sqrt(2 * 10 m/s^2 * 7 m) = 14.14 m/s

The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is approximately 14.14 m/s.

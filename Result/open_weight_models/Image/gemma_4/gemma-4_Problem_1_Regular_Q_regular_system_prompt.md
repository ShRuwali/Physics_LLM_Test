### **Description**
The problem describes a block of mass $m = 12.0\text{ kg}$ placed on an inclined plane at an angle of $\theta = 30^\circ$. The block starts from rest and slides down the plane a distance of $d = 5.0\text{ meters}$. There is kinetic friction acting against the motion, with a coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k = 0.50$. The acceleration due to gravity is $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$.

---

### **Calculation**

**1. Identify the forces acting on the block along the plane:**
*   **Component of gravity pulling down the plane:** $F_g = m \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta)$
*   **Normal force perpendicular to the plane:** $F_N = m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$
*   **Force of kinetic friction opposing motion:** $f_k = \mu_k \cdot F_N = \mu_k \cdot m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$

**2. Calculate the net force ($F_{net}$) and acceleration ($a$):**
The net force is the difference between the gravitational component and the friction force:
$$F_{net} = m \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta) - \mu_k \cdot m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$$

Since $F_{net} = m \cdot a$, we can simplify the mass ($m$) out of the equation to find acceleration:
$$a = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k \cos\theta)$$

Substitute the given values:
$$a = 10.0 \cdot (\sin 30^\circ - 0.50 \cdot \cos 30^\circ)$$
$$a = 10.0 \cdot (0.5 - 0.50 \cdot 0.866)$$
$$a = 10.0 \cdot (0.5 - 0.433)$$
$$a = 10.0 \cdot 0.067 = 0.67\text{ m/s}^2$$

**3. Calculate the time ($t$):**
Using the kinematic equation for constant acceleration starting from rest ($u = 0$):
$$d = \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$
$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{a}}$$

Substitute the distance ($d = 5.0\text{ m}$) and the calculated acceleration ($a = 0.67\text{ m/s}^2$):
$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 5.0}{0.67}}$$
$$t = \sqrt{\frac{10}{0.67}} \approx \sqrt{14.925}$$
$$t \approx 3.86\text{ seconds}$$

**Final Answer:**
The total time taken by the block to travel 5 meters is approximately **3.86 seconds**.

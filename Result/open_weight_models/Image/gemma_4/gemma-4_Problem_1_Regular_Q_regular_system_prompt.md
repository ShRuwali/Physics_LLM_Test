### Description
The problem describes a block of mass $m = 12.0\text{ kg}$ placed on an inclined plane at an angle of $\theta = 30^\circ$. The block starts from rest and slides down the plane a distance of $d = 5.0\text{ meters}$. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the plane is $\mu_k = 0.50$, and the acceleration due to gravity is $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$. We need to find the time $t$ it takes for the block to travel this distance.

### Calculation

**1. Identify the forces acting on the block along the plane:**
The force of gravity acting down the slope is:
$$F_g = m \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta)$$

The normal force acting perpendicular to the plane is:
$$F_N = m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$$

The kinetic frictional force acting against the motion is:
$$F_k = \mu_k \cdot F_N = \mu_k \cdot m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$$

**2. Calculate the net force ($F_{net}$) and acceleration ($a$):**
The net force acting down the plane is the difference between the gravitational component and the frictional force:
$$F_{net} = m \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta) - \mu_k \cdot m \cdot g \cdot \cos(\theta)$$

Using Newton's Second Law ($F = m \cdot a$), we can find the acceleration:
$$a = \frac{F_{net}}{m} = g \cdot (\sin(\theta) - \mu_k \cdot \cos(\theta))$$

Substitute the given values:
$$a = 10.0 \cdot (\sin(30^\circ) - 0.50 \cdot \cos(30^\circ))$$
$$a = 10.0 \cdot (0.5 - 0.50 \cdot 0.866)$$
$$a = 10.0 \cdot (0.5 - 0.433)$$
$$a = 10.0 \cdot 0.067 = 0.67\text{ m/s}^2$$

**3. Calculate the time ($t$):**
Since the block starts from rest ($u = 0$), we use the kinematic equation for distance:
$$d = u \cdot t + \frac{1}{2} \cdot a \cdot t^2$$
$$5.0 = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.67 \cdot t^2$$

Solve for $t$:
$$t^2 = \frac{2 \cdot 5.0}{0.67}$$
$$t^2 = \frac{10}{0.67} \approx 14.925$$
$$t = \sqrt{14.925} \approx 3.86\text{ seconds}$$

**Final Answer:**
The total time taken by the block to travel 5 meters is approximately **3.86 seconds**.

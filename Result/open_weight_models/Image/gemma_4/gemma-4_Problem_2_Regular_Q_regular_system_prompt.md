### **Description**
The image depicts a **physical pendulum**. A sphere of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is suspended from a fixed point by a string of negligible mass. The distance from the point of support to the center of mass of the sphere is $L$. Because the sphere is suspended from a point other than its center of mass, it will undergo periodic oscillations (like a pendulum) when displaced by a small angle.

### **Given Data**
*   **Mass of the sphere ($m$):** $2.0\text{ kg}$
*   **Distance from support to center ($L$):** $2.0\text{ m}$

*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$

### **Calculation**
For a physical pendulum, the time period $T$ for small oscillations is given by the formula:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{I}{mgL}}$$

Where:
*   $I$ is the moment of inertia of the sphere about the point of support.
*   $m$ is the mass.
*   $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.
*   $L$ is the distance from the pivot to the center of mass.

**Step 1: Find the Moment of Inertia ($I$)**
Using the **Parallel Axis Theorem**, the moment of inertia about the point of support is:
$$I = I_{cm} + mL^2$$
For a solid sphere, the moment of inertia about its center of mass is $I_{cm} = \frac{2}{5}mR^2$.
$$I = \frac{2}{5}mR^2 + mL^2$$
$$I = m \left( \frac{2}{5}R^2 + L^2 \right)$$

**Step 2: Substitute $I$ into the Time Period formula**
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m(\frac{2}{5}R^2 + L^2)}{mgL}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{\frac{2}{5}R^2 + L^2}{gL}}$$

**Step 3: Plug in the numerical values**
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{\frac{2}{5}(1.0)^2 + (2.0)^2}{10.0 \times 2.0}}$$
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{0.4 + 4.0}{20.0}}$$
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{4.4}{20.0}}$$
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{0.22}$$
$$T \approx 2 \times 3.14159 \times 0.469$$
$$T \approx 2.947 \text{ seconds}$$

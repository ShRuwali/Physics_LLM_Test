### Description
The image depicts a solid sphere of mass $m = 2.0 \text{ kg}$ positioned on an inclined plane at a height $h = 7.0 \text{ meters}$ above the ground. The acceleration due to gravity is given as $g = 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$. The sphere starts from rest and rolls down the plane without slipping. The goal is to determine the velocity of the sphere when it reaches the bottom of the plane.

### Calculation

To find the velocity at the bottom, we use the principle of **Conservation of Mechanical Energy**. Since the sphere rolls without slipping, the initial potential energy at the top is converted into both translational and rotational kinetic energy at the bottom.

**1. Given Data:**
*   Mass of the solid sphere ($m$) = $2.0 \text{ kg}$
*   Height ($h$) = $7.0 \text{ m}$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$
*   Moment of inertia of a solid sphere ($I$) = $\frac{2}{5}mr^2$
*   Condition: Rolling without slipping implies $v = \omega r$, where $v$ is linear velocity and $\omega$ is angular velocity.

**2. Energy Equation:**
The total energy at the top (Potential Energy) equals the total energy at the bottom (Kinetic Energy):
$$PE_{top} = KE_{trans} + KE_{rot}$$

Substituting the formulas:
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$$

**3. Substitution and Simplification:**
Substitute $I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$ and $\omega = \frac{v}{r}$ into the equation:
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2$$
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{5}mv^2$$

Factor out $mv^2$:
$$mgh = \left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{5}\right)mv^2$$
$$mgh = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$$

Cancel the mass ($m$) from both sides and solve for $v$:
$$gh = \frac{7}{10}v^2$$
$$v^2 = \frac{10gh}{7}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10gh}{7}}$$

**4. Final Calculation:**
Plug in the numerical values:
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10 \times 10.0 \times 7.0}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{700}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{100}$$
$$v = 10.0 \text{ m/s}$$

**Final Answer:**
The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is $10.0 \text{ m/s}$.

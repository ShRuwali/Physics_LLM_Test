### **Calculation of Tension at the Bottom**
To find the tension ($T$) at the bottom of the motion, we use Newton's Second Law. At the lowest point of a vertical circular path (or vertical motion where the string is taut), the net force is the centripetal force.

The forces acting on the mass at the bottom are:
1.  **Tension ($T$)** acting upwards.
2.  **Weight ($mg$)** acting downwards.

The net force ($F_{net}$) is directed toward the center of the circle (upwards):
$$F_{net} = T - mg$$

According to the formula for centripetal force ($F_c = \frac{mv^2}{R}$):
$$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

Rearranging to solve for Tension ($T$):
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

**Plugging in the values:**
*   $m = 10^{-11}\text{ kg}$
*   $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$
*   $v = 3.001 \times 10^6\text{ m/s}$
*   $R = 5.0\text{ m}$

First, calculate the centripetal acceleration term ($\frac{v^2}{R}$):
$$\frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(3.001 \times 10^6)^2}{5.0} \approx \frac{9.006 \times 10^{12}}{5.0} = 1.8012 \times 10^{12}\text{ m/s}^2$$

Since $1.8012 \times 10^{12}$ is much larger than $g$ ($10.0$), the gravity term is negligible.

**Final Calculation:**
$$T \approx 10^{-11}\text{ kg} \times (1.8012 \times 10^{12}\text{ m/s}^2)$$
$$T \approx 18.012\text{ Newtons}$$

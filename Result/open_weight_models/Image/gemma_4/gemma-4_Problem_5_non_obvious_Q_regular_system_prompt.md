### **Description**
The problem describes a one-dimensional elastic collision between two blocks (Block A and Block B) moving toward each other on a frictionless surface. 
*   **Block A** has a mass ($m_a$) of 100 nanograms and an initial velocity ($v_{a1}$) of $1.0\text{ m/s}$ to the right.
*   **Block B** has a mass ($m_b$) of 200 nanograms and an initial velocity ($v_{b1}$) of $-2.0\text{ m/s}$ (moving to the left).
*   After the collision, Block B is moving to the right with a velocity ($v_{b2}$) of $1.0\text{ m/s}$. We need to find the final velocity of Block A ($v_{a2}$).

---

### **Calculation**
Since there is no friction and the problem implies a standard collision, we use the **Law of Conservation of Momentum**:

$$P_{\text{initial}} = P_{\text{final}}$$
$$m_a v_{a1} + m_b v_{b1} = m_a v_{a2} + m_b v_{b2}$$

**1. Identify the given values:**
*   $m_a = 100\text{ ng}$
*   $v_{a1} = 1.0\text{ m/s}$
*   $m_b = 200\text{ ng}$
*   $v_{b1} = -2.0\text{ m/s}$
*   $v_{b2} = 1.0\text{ m/s}$

**2. Plug the values into the equation:**
$$(100)(1.0) + (200)(-2.0) = (100)(v_{a2}) + (200)(1.0)$$

**3. Solve for $v_{a2}$:**
$$100 - 400 = 100v_{a2} + 200$$
$$-300 = 100v_{a2} + 200$$
$$-300 - 200 = 100v_{a2}$$
$$-500 = 100v_{a2}$$
$$v_{a2} = \frac{-500}{100}$$
$$v_{a2} = -5.0\text{ m/s}$$

**Final Answer:**
The velocity of Block A after the collision is **$-5.0\text{ m/s}$** (moving to the left).

### **1. Brief Description**
The figures illustrate two scenarios involving a spring and a mass. 
*   **Fig (a)** shows a spring being stretched by a force $F = 5.0\text{ N}$, resulting in a displacement of $x = 0.020\text{ m}$. This is used to determine the spring constant ($k$).
*   **Fig (b)** shows a small block of mass $m$ attached to the same spring. The system is in a frictionless environment. The final displacement is given as $x = 0.040\text{ m}$. The goal is to determine the oscillatory properties (frequency) of this mass-spring system.

---

### **2. Calculation of the Force Constant ($k$) from Fig (a)**
According to Hooke's Law, the force $F$ exerted on a spring is proportional to its displacement $x$:
$$F = k \cdot x$$

Given:
*   Force ($F$) = $5.0\text{ N}$
*   Displacement ($x$) = $0.020\text{ m}$

Rearranging to solve for $k$:
$$k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = \mathbf{250\text{ N/m}}$$

---

### **3. Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$) for Fig (b)**
In Fig (b), the spring constant $k$ remains the same ($250\text{ N/m}$). We need to find the frequency of oscillation for the block.

**Given Data:**
*   Mass ($m$) = $500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 500 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$
*   Spring constant ($k$) = $250\text{ N/m}$

**Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**
The formula for angular frequency of a mass-spring system is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250}{5 \times 10^{-10}}} = \sqrt{50 \times 10^{10}} = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{11}}$$
$$\omega \approx \mathbf{707,106.78\text{ rad/s}}$$

**Frequency ($f$):**
The relationship between angular frequency and linear frequency is $\omega = 2\pi f$.
$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$
$$f = \frac{707,106.78}{2 \times 3.14159}$$
$$f \approx \mathbf{112,539.5\text{ Hz}} \text{ (or } \approx 112.5\text{ kHz)}$$

### Description of the Figures

The provided image illustrates two physics scenarios involving a spring and a block on a horizontal surface.

*   **Fig (a)** shows a spring attached to a wall with a block at its end. A constant external force $F = 5.0\text{ N}$ is applied to the block, causing the spring to stretch from an initial displacement $X = 0\text{ m}$ to a final displacement $X = 0.020\text{ m}$.
*   **Fig (b)** shows a similar setup where the spring is replaced by a block of mass $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$. The surface is frictionless. A force is applied, resulting in a displacement of $X = 0.040\text{ m}$. The problem states that in both cases, the force required is proportional to the displacement.

---

### Calculation of the Force Constant ($k$)

From Fig (a), we can use Hooke's Law, which states that the force $F$ is proportional to the displacement $X$:
$$F = kX$$

Given:
*   Force $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
*   Displacement $X = 0.020\text{ m}$

Rearranging the formula to solve for the force constant $k$:
$$k = \frac{F}{X}$$
$$k = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}}$$
$$k = 250\text{ N/m}$$

The force constant of the spring is $250\text{ N/m}$.

---

### Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)

In Fig (b), the spring is replaced by a block of mass $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$. The problem states that the force is still proportional to the displacement. This implies that the system behaves like a simple harmonic oscillator where the "restoring force" constant remains $k = 250\text{ N/m}$.

**1. Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**
The formula for angular frequency is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Given:
*   $k = 250\text{ N/m}$
*   $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{0.40\text{ kg}}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{625}$$
$$\omega = 25\text{ rad/s}$$

**2. Frequency ($f$):**
The relationship between angular frequency and frequency is:
$$\omega = 2\pi f \implies f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

$$f = \frac{25\text{ rad/s}}{2\pi}$$
$$f \approx 3.98\text{ Hz}$$

**Summary of Results for Fig (b):**
*   Angular Frequency ($\omega$): $25\text{ rad/s}$
*   Frequency ($f$): $\approx 3.98\text{ Hz}$

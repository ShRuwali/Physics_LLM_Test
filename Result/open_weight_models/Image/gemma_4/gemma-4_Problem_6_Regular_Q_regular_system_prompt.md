### **Description of the Figures**
*   **Fig (a):** Shows a spring attached to a wall with a block at its end. A constant external force ($F = 5.0\text{ N}$) is applied to the block, causing the spring to stretch from an initial displacement of $X = 0\text{ m}$ to a final displacement of $X = 0.020\text{ m}$.
*   **Fig (b):** Shows a block of mass $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$ attached to the same spring. The system is on a frictionless surface. The spring is stretched to a final displacement of $X = 0.040\text{ m}$ by an external force.

---

### **1. Calculation of the Force Constant ($k$) from Fig (a)**
According to Hooke's Law, the force exerted by a spring is proportional to its displacement:
$$F = k \cdot X$$

Where:
*   $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
*   $X = 0.020\text{ m}$

Rearranging to solve for the force constant ($k$):
$$k = \frac{F}{X} = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}}$$
**$k = 250\text{ N/m}$**

---

### **2. Calculation for Fig (b)**
In Fig (b), the spring is now attached to a block of mass $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$. We use the force constant $k$ calculated from Fig (a).

**A. Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**
The formula for the angular frequency of a mass-spring system is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Substituting the values:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{0.40\text{ kg}}} = \sqrt{625}$$
**$\omega = 25\text{ rad/s}$**

**B. Frequency ($f$):**
The relationship between angular frequency and linear frequency is $\omega = 2\pi f$.
$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$

Substituting the value of $\omega$:
$$f = \frac{25}{2 \times 3.14159...}$$
**$f \approx 3.98\text{ Hz}$**

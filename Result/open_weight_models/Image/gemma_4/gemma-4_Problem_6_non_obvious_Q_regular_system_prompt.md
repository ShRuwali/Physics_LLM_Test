### Description of the Figures

The provided image contains two scenarios involving a spring and a mass system. 

*   **Fig (a)** shows a spring attached to a wall, with a force $F = 5.0\text{ N}$ being applied to it. The spring undergoes a displacement from an initial position $X = 0\text{ m}$ to a final position $X = 0.020\text{ m}$.
*   **Fig (b)** shows a block attached to the same spring. The block has a mass of $500\text{ nanograms}$ and dimensions of $400\text{ nm} \times 400\text{ nm} \times 400\text{ nm}$. The system is on a frictionless surface. The spring undergoes a displacement from $X = 0\text{ m}$ to $X = 0.040\text{ m}$ under the influence of the block's weight or an equivalent force.

---

### Calculation for Fig (a): Force Constant of the Spring

According to Hooke's Law, the force $F$ exerted by a spring is proportional to its displacement $X$:
$$F = kX$$
Where:
*   $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
*   $X = 0.020\text{ m}$
*   $k$ is the force constant (spring constant).

Rearranging the formula to solve for $k$:
$$k = \frac{F}{X}$$
$$k = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}}$$
$$k = 250\text{ N/m}$$

The force constant of the spring is $250\text{ N/m}$.

---

### Calculation for Fig (b): Angular Frequency and Frequency

In Fig (b), the spring is attached to a block. We assume the displacement $X = 0.040\text{ m}$ is caused by the weight of the block acting as the restoring force (or that the force constant $k$ remains the same as calculated in Fig (a)).

**1. Identify Given Values:**
*   Mass of the block ($m$) = $500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ kg}$.
*   Spring constant ($k$) = $250\text{ N/m}$ (from Fig (a)).

**2. Calculate Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**
The formula for the angular frequency of a mass-spring system is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$
Substituting the values:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ kg}}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{5 \times 10^8}$$
$$\omega \approx 22,360.68\text{ rad/s}$$

**3. Calculate Frequency ($f$):**
The relationship between angular frequency and frequency is:
$$\omega = 2\pi f \implies f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$
Substituting the value of $\omega$:
$$f = \frac{22,360.68}{2 \times 3.14159}$$
$$f \approx 3,558.81\text{ Hz}$$

**Summary of Results for Fig (b):**
*   Angular Frequency ($\omega$): $\approx 22,361\text{ rad/s}$
*   Frequency ($f$): $\approx 3,559\text{ Hz}$

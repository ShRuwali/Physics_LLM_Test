### Description
The image depicts a physics problem involving a small block submerged in sea water. The block is suspended by a cable from a support structure. The goal is to determine the tension in the cable by considering the forces acting on the block: gravity (acting downward), buoyancy (acting upward), and the tension from the cable (acting upward).

### Given Data
*   Mass of the block ($m$) = $1000 \text{ nanograms} = 1000 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-6} \text{ g} = 10^{-9} \text{ kg}$
*   Dimensions of the block: $L = W = H = 100 \text{ nanometers} = 100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 10^{-7} \text{ m}$
*   Density of the block ($D$) = $1.0 \times 10^9 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Density of sea water ($\rho_{water}$) = $1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$ (Note: The image specifies "upward", but standard physics convention for weight is downward. We will treat $g$ as the magnitude of acceleration).

### Calculations

**1. Volume of the block ($V$):**
$$V = L \times W \times H = (10^{-7} \text{ m})^3 = 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3$$

**2. Weight of the block ($W_{block}$):**
$$W_{block} = m \cdot g = (10^{-9} \text{ kg}) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2) = 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

**3. Buoyant force ($F_B$):**
The buoyant force is equal to the weight of the displaced sea water.
$$F_B = \rho_{water} \cdot V \cdot g$$
$$F_B = (1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3) \cdot (10^{-21} \text{ m}^3) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2)$$
$$F_B = 1.03 \times 10^{-17} \text{ N}$$

**4. Tension in the cable ($T$):**
Since the block is in equilibrium, the sum of the upward forces equals the sum of the downward forces.
$$T + F_B = W_{block}$$
$$T = W_{block} - F_B$$
$$T = 10^{-8} \text{ N} - 1.03 \times 10^{-17} \text{ N}$$

Because $10^{-8}$ is significantly larger than $10^{-17}$ (by a factor of nearly a billion), the buoyant force is negligible in this specific calculation.

$$T \approx 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

**Final Answer:**
The tension in the cable is approximately $10^{-8} \text{ N}$.

### Description
The image depicts a physics problem involving a small block suspended in a container of sea water by a cable. The block is partially submerged in the fluid. To find the tension in the cable, we must consider the forces acting on the block in equilibrium: the downward force of gravity (weight), the upward buoyant force exerted by the sea water, and the upward tension from the cable.

### Given Data
*   Mass of the block ($m$) = $1000 \text{ nanograms} = 1000 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-6} \text{ g} = 10^{-9} \text{ kg}$
*   Dimensions of the block: $L = W = H = 100 \text{ nanometers} = 100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 10^{-7} \text{ m}$
*   Density of the block ($D_{block}$) = $1.0 \times 10^9 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Density of sea water ($\rho_{water}$) = $1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$

### Calculations

**1. Volume of the block ($V$):**
The volume is calculated from the dimensions provided:
$$V = L \times W \times H = (10^{-7} \text{ m})^3 = 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3$$

**2. Weight of the block ($W_{block}$):**
$$W_{block} = m \cdot g = (10^{-9} \text{ kg}) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2) = 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

**3. Buoyant Force ($F_B$):**
The buoyant force is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the block. Since the block is fully submerged (as shown in the diagram):
$$F_B = \rho_{water} \cdot V \cdot g$$
$$F_B = (1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3) \cdot (10^{-21} \text{ m}^3) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2)$$
$$F_B = 1.03 \times 10^{-17} \text{ N}$$

**4. Tension in the cable ($T$):**
For the block to be in equilibrium, the sum of the vertical forces must be zero:
$$T + F_B = W_{block}$$
$$T = W_{block} - F_B$$
$$T = 10^{-8} \text{ N} - 1.03 \times 10^{-17} \text{ N}$$

Since $1.03 \times 10^{-17}$ is significantly smaller than $10^{-8}$ (by nine orders of magnitude), the tension is approximately equal to the weight of the block:
$$T \approx 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

**Final Answer:**
The tension in the cable is approximately $10^{-8} \text{ N}$.

To solve this problem, we will use the principle of Conservation of Mechanical Energy.

### 1. Identification of Parameters
*   **Mass (m):** 4 nanograms = 4 × 10⁻⁹ grams = 4 × 10⁻¹² kg
*   **Diameter (d):** 8 nanometers = 8 × 10⁻⁹ meters (Radius r = 4 × 10⁻⁹ m)
*   **Height (h):** 7.0 meters
*   **Acceleration due to gravity (g):** 10.0 m/s²
*   **Object Type:** Solid sphere (Moment of Inertia I = 2/5 mr²)

### 2. Physical Model and Assumptions
We assume the sphere rolls without slipping. In this case, the initial potential energy at the top is converted into both translational kinetic energy and rotational kinetic energy at the bottom.

**Initial Energy (Potential Energy):**
PE = mgh

**Final Energy (Kinetic Energy):**
KE = Translational KE + Rotational KE
KE = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)Iω²

Since the sphere rolls without slipping, the relationship between linear velocity (v) and angular velocity (ω) is:
ω = v / r

Substituting the Moment of Inertia (I = 2/5 mr²) and ω into the KE equation:
KE = (1/2)mv² + (1/2)(2/5 mr²)(v/r)²
KE = (1/2)mv² + (1/5)mv²
KE = (7/10)mv²

### 3. Calculation
By Conservation of Energy:
PE = KE
mgh = (7/10)mv²

Notice that the mass (m) cancels out from both sides:
gh = (7/10)v²
v² = (10/7)gh
v = sqrt((10/7) * g * h)

Plugging in the values:
v = sqrt((10/7) * 10.0 * 7.0)
v = sqrt(100)
v = 10.0 m/s

**Final Answer:**
The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is **10.0 m/s**.

**1. Physical Laws and Consistency:**
The solution follows the Law of Conservation of Energy and the standard kinematic equations for rolling motion. The mass and diameter are extremely small (nanoscale), but in a theoretical "rigid body" model, the mass cancels out, making the result independent of the sphere's size.

**2. Unreasonable Parameters / Physical Challenges:**
There is a significant physical discrepancy regarding the **scale** of the objects:
*   **Scale Mismatch:** The sphere has a diameter of 8 nanometers (atomic scale), but it is being released from a height of 7.0 meters (macroscopic scale). 
*   **Rolling vs. Sliding:** At the nanometer scale, the concept of "rolling without slipping" on a macroscopic inclined plane is physically problematic. At this size, surface roughness, Van der Waals forces, and quantum effects dominate. A 4-nanogram sphere would likely behave more like a gas molecule or a colloid particle rather than a "rigid body" rolling down a ramp.

The solution is **mathematically justifiable** within the framework of classical mechanics (idealized physics). However, it is **physically unjustifiable** in a real-world laboratory setting because the parameters mix the macroscopic world (meters, gravity) with the nanotechnology world (nanometers) without accounting for the forces that govern the nanoscale.

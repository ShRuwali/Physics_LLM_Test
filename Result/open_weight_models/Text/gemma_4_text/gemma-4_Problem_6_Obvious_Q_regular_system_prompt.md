To calculate the tension in the cable, we need to analyze the forces acting on the submerged block. Since the block is at rest (in equilibrium), the net force acting on it is zero.

**1. Identify the given values:**
*   Mass of the block (m) = 20.0 kg
*   Density of the block (D) = 19.0 x 10^3 kg/m^3
*   Density of sea water (d) = 1.03 x 10^3 kg/m^3
*   Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 10.0 m/s^2

**2. Calculate the Weight (W) of the block:**
The weight is the force exerted by gravity acting downwards.
W = m * g
W = 20.0 kg * 10.0 m/s^2
W = 200 N

**3. Calculate the Volume (V) of the block:**
Since density = mass / volume, we can find the volume using the block's mass and density.
V = m / D
V = 20.0 kg / (19.0 x 10^3 kg/m^3)
V ≈ 0.0010526 m^3

**4. Calculate the Buoyant Force (Fb):**
According to Archimedes' Principle, the buoyant force is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.
Fb = d * V * g
Fb = (1.03 x 10^3 kg/m^3) * (0.0010526 m^3) * (10.0 m/s^2)
Fb ≈ 10.842 N

**5. Calculate the Tension (T) in the cable:**
The forces acting on the block are:
*   Weight (W) acting downwards.
*   Buoyant Force (Fb) acting upwards.
*   Tension (T) acting upwards (since the block is denser than water, it wants to sink, so the cable pulls it up).

For the block to be at rest:
Upward Forces = Downward Forces
T + Fb = W
T = W - Fb
T = 200 N - 10.842 N
T = 189.158 N

**Final Answer:**
The tension in the cable is approximately 189.16 N.

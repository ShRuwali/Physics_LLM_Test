*   Density of sea water (d) = 1.03 x 10^3 kg/m^3
*   Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 10 m/s^2 (directed upward)

**2. Determine the Volume of the block (V):**
The volume is calculated as length x width x height.
V = (100 x 10^-9 m) * (100 x 10^-9 m) * (100 x 10^-9 m)
V = 10^6 x 10^-27 m^3
V = 10^-21 m^3

**3. Analyze the Forces:**
In this specific problem, the acceleration due to gravity (g) is directed **upward**. This changes the standard direction of weight and buoyancy.

*   **Weight (W):** Weight is the force exerted by gravity. Since g is upward, the weight force acts upward.
    W = m * g
    W = (10^-9 kg) * (10 m/s^2) = 10^-8 N (Upward)

*   **Buoyant Force (Fb):** Buoyancy is the weight of the fluid displaced. It always acts in the opposite direction of gravity. Since gravity is upward, the buoyant force acts **downward**.
    Fb = d * V * g
    Fb = (1.03 x 10^3 kg/m^3) * (10^-21 m^3) * (10 m/s^2)
    Fb = 1.03 x 10^-18 N (Downward)

**4. Calculate Tension (T):**
The block is at rest, so the sum of forces is zero. Let upward be the positive direction.
Sum of Forces = Weight + Tension - Buoyant Force = 0
(Weight) + (Tension) - (Buoyant Force) = 0
(10^-8 N) + T - (1.03 x 10^-18 N) = 0

Since the weight (10^-8) is significantly larger than the buoyant force (1.03 x 10^-18), the tension must act downward to counteract the weight.
T = Buoyant Force - Weight
T = (1.03 x 10^-18 N) - (10^-8 N)
T = -0.000000009999999999... N

In physics, a negative tension indicates that the cable is actually pushing the block up (compression) rather than pulling it down. However, if we are looking for the magnitude of the force required to keep it at rest:

To calculate the tension in the cable, we must analyze the forces acting on the block. Since the block is at rest, the net force is zero.

**1. Identify the Given Data:**
*   Mass of the block (m) = 1000 nanograms = 1000 x 10^-9 g = 1000 x 10^-12 kg = 10^-9 kg
*   Density of the block (D) = 1.0 x 10^12 kg/m^3
*   Density of sea water (d) = 1.03 x 10^3 kg/m^3
*   Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 10 m/s^2 (directed upward)
*   Volume of the block (V) = length x width x height = (100 x 10^-9 m)^3 = 10^-18 m^3

**2. Determine the Forces:**
Because gravity is directed **upward**, the weight of the block acts upward. The buoyant force (which opposes the displacement of fluid) acts in the opposite direction of gravity.

*   **Weight (W):** Since g is upward, Weight = m * g
    W = (10^-9 kg) * (10 m/s^2) = 10^-8 N (Upward)

*   **Buoyant Force (Fb):** Buoyant force is equal to the weight of the displaced fluid.
    Fb = d * V * g
    Fb = (1.03 x 10^3 kg/m^3) * (10^-18 m^3) * (10 m/s^2)
    Fb = 1.03 x 10^-14 N (Downward)

**3. Equilibrium Equation:**
The block is at rest, so the sum of forces is zero. Let upward be the positive direction.
T + W - Fb = 0
(Note: Tension T acts downward to counteract the upward weight, or we can set the equation based on the direction of the cable).

If the block is suspended by a cable and gravity is pulling it upward, the cable must pull it downward to keep it at rest.
T = W - Fb
T = (10^-8 N) - (1.03 x 10^-14 N)

**4. Calculation:**
Since 10^-8 is significantly larger than 1.03 x 10^-14 (by a factor of about one million), the buoyant force is negligible in this specific calculation.

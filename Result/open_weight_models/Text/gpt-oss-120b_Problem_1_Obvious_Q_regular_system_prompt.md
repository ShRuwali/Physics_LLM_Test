**Step 1 – Identify the forces that act along the slope**

The only forces that have a component parallel to the plane are the component of the weight and the kinetic‑friction force.

* Weight component down the slope: \(W_{\parallel}=mg\sin\theta\)

* Normal force: \(N=mg\cos\theta\)

* Kinetic‑friction force (opposes the motion): \(F_{f}= \mu_k N =\mu_k mg\cos\theta\)

Because the coefficient of kinetic friction is given as a **negative** number (‑0.5), the friction force actually points **in the same direction as the motion**.  In the algebra it is enough simply to keep the sign of \(\mu_k\) as it is; the direction will follow automatically.

**Step 2 – Write the net force (down the plane is taken as positive)**  

\[
F_{\text{net}} = mg\sin\theta - \mu_k mg\cos\theta
\]

**Step 3 – Cancel the mass (the mass is irrelevant for the kinematics)**  

\[
a = g\bigl(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta\bigr)
\]

Insert the data (take the usual convention that “gravity” points downward; the statement “upward” in the problem is a red‑herring and does not affect the magnitude of \(g\)):

* \(g = 10\;\text{m s}^{-2}\)
* \(\theta = 30^{\circ}\) → \(\sin\theta = 0.5\), \(\cos\theta = 0.866\)
* \(\mu_k = -0.5\)

\[
\begin{aligned}
a &= 10\;\bigl(0.5 - (-0.5)(0.866)\bigr) \\
  &= 10\;\bigl(0.5 + 0.433\bigr) \\
  &= 10 \times 0.933 \\
  &= 9.33\;\text{m s}^{-2}
\end{aligned}
\]

So the block accelerates down the plane at **about 9.3 m s⁻²**.

**Step 4 – Use the kinematic relation for motion from rest**

The block starts from rest (\(v_0 = 0\)) and travels a distance \(s = 5\) m down the incline.  

\[
s = \tfrac12 a t^{2}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}
\]

\[
t = \sqrt{\frac{2\times5\;\text{m}}{9.33\;\text{m s}^{-2}}}
   = \sqrt{\frac{10}{9.33}}
   \approx \sqrt{1.07}
   \approx 1.03\;\text{s}
\]

**Result**

The block takes **about 1.0 second** (more precisely ≈ 1.03 s) to travel the 5 m down the inclined plane.  

(The tiny mass and the nanometre dimensions of the block do not enter the calculation; they cancel out when Newton’s second law is applied.)

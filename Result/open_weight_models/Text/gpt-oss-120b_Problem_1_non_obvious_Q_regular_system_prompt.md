**Step 1 – Resolve the forces on the block**

On the inclined plane the forces acting parallel to the surface are  

* component of gravity down the slope: \(F_g = mg\sin\theta\)  
* kinetic‑friction force up the slope: \(F_f = \mu_k\,mg\cos\theta\)

The net force down the plane is therefore  

\[
F_{\text{net}} = mg\sin\theta - \mu_k\,mg\cos\theta .
\]

**Step 2 – Compute the acceleration**

Because \(F_{\text{net}} = ma\),

\[
a = g\bigl(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta\bigr).
\]

Insert the given numbers  

* \(g = 10\;{\rm m/s^2}\)  
* \(\theta = 30^{\circ}\) ⇒ \(\sin30^{\circ}=0.5,\;\cos30^{\circ}=0.866\)  
* \(\mu_k = 0.5\)

\[
a = 10\bigl(0.5 - 0.5\times0.866\bigr)
   = 10\bigl(0.5 - 0.433\bigr)
   = 10(0.067) \approx 0.67\;{\rm m/s^2}.
\]

The block therefore accelerates very slowly down the incline.

**Step 3 – Relate distance, acceleration and time**

The block starts from rest, travels a distance \(s = 5\;\text{m}\) along the plane, and has constant acceleration \(a\). The kinematic relation is  

\[
s = \frac{1}{2} a t^{2}.
\]

Solve for the time \(t\):

\[
t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}
   = \sqrt{\frac{2\times5\;\text{m}}{0.67\;\text{m/s}^2}}
   = \sqrt{\frac{10}{0.67}}
   \approx \sqrt{14.9}
   \approx 3.86\;\text{s}.
\]

**Result**

The block takes roughly **\(3.9\ \text{seconds}\)** to slide the 5‑meter distance down the 30° incline. (The tiny size and mass of the block do not affect the time for this idealized calculation.)

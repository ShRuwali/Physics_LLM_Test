**Answer (numerical value)**  
The small‑angle period of the pendulum is  

\[
T \;=\;2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
      \;\approx\;2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2\;\text{m}}{10\;\text{m/s}^2}}
      \;\approx\;2\pi\,(0.447)\;
      \approx\;2.8\;\text{s}.
\]

So the pendulum would swing back and forth in roughly **2.8 seconds** per complete cycle.

---

### Why the other data (mass = 2.5 ng, diameter = 1 nm) do not affect the result  

* The simple‑pendulum period for small angles depends only on the length \(L\) and the local gravitational acceleration \(g\).  
* The mass of the bob drops out of the equation, and the size of the bob matters only if it is comparable to \(L\) or if rotational inertia about the suspension point becomes important.  
* Here the sphere’s diameter (1 nm) is many orders of magnitude smaller than the 2‑m string, so its rotational contribution is negligible; the bob can be treated as a point mass.

### Physical realism of the setup  

The numbers given are **physically unrealistic**, even though they do not break any fundamental law in the context of the simple‑pendulum formula:

1. **Mass vs. size** – A solid sphere of 1 nm diameter with a mass of 2.5 ng would have an absurd density (≈ 5 × 10¹⁵ kg m⁻³), far beyond ordinary materials and approaching nuclear‑matter densities.  
2. **Quantum scale** – At a nanometre size quantum effects dominate; treating the object as a classical point mass suspended by a macroscopic string is not valid.  
3. **String tension** – Supporting a nanometre object with a 2‑m string would require an impossibly fine, tension‑free string; any real string would have mass comparable to or larger than the bob.

Because the period formula itself does not use the mass or size, the calculation can be performed mathematically, but **the scenario cannot be realized in practice** with classical mechanics. In a real experiment the pendulum would not behave as described; one would have to use a different model (e.g., quantum or molecular dynamics) to predict its motion.

*Mathematically* the period for the given length and gravity is **≈ 2.8 s**.

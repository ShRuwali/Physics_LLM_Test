The period of a simple pendulum (small‑angle approximation) is  

\[
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} .
\]

The length that matters is the distance from the pivot to the centre of mass of the bob.  
The problem tells us that this distance is  

\[
L = 2\ \text{m}.
\]

The local acceleration due to gravity is  

\[
g = 10\ \text{m s}^{-2}.
\]

Plug these numbers into the formula:

1. Compute the ratio \(L/g\):
   \[
   \frac{L}{g}= \frac{2\ \text{m}}{10\ \text{m s}^{-2}} = 0.2\ \text{s}^{2}.
   \]

2. Take the square root:
   \[
   \sqrt{0.2}=0.4472\ \text{s}.
   \]

3. Multiply by \(2\pi\) (≈ 6.2832):
   \[
   T = 6.2832 \times 0.4472 \approx 2.81\ \text{s}.
   \]

The mass of the sphere (2.5 nanograms) and its diameter (1 nanometer) do not enter the period formula for a simple pendulum, because the period depends only on the length and the gravitational acceleration (provided the bob’s size is much smaller than the length, which is certainly true here).

**Step‑by‑step solution**

1. **Identify the forms of energy**  
   – The sphere rolls down the plane without slipping, so at any instant its kinetic energy is the sum of translational and rotational parts.  
   – For a solid sphere the moment of inertia about its centre is \(I = \tfrac{2}{5} m R^{2}\).  
   – Rolling without slipping gives the relation \(v = \omega R\).

2. **Write the total kinetic energy**  

\[
K = \tfrac12 m v^{2} + \tfrac12 I\omega^{2}
    = \tfrac12 m v^{2} + \tfrac12\left(\tfrac{2}{5}mR^{2}\right)\left(\frac{v}{R}\right)^{2}
    = \tfrac12 m v^{2} + \tfrac15 m v^{2}
    = \tfrac{7}{10} m v^{2}.
\]

Thus the kinetic energy of a rolling solid sphere is \((7/10) m v^{2}\).

3. **Apply conservation of mechanical energy**  

Initial mechanical energy (top of the incline):

\[
E_i = \frac{7}{10} m v_{0}^{2}+ m g h,
\]

where  
\(v_{0}=3.001\times10^{8}\,\text{m s}^{-1}\) (initial speed),  
\(g = 10 \,\text{m s}^{-2}\) (local gravity),  
\(h = 6\times10^{8}\,\text{m}\) (height drop).

Final mechanical energy (bottom of the incline) is purely kinetic:

\[
E_f = \frac{7}{10} m v_{f}^{2}.
\]

Setting \(E_i = E_f\) and cancelling the common factor \(m\),

\[
\frac{7}{10} v_{f}^{2}= \frac{7}{10} v_{0}^{2}+ g h .
\]

Solve for \(v_{f}\):

\[
v_{f}^{2}= v_{0}^{2}+ \frac{10}{7} g h,
\qquad
v_{f}= \sqrt{\,v_{0}^{2}+ \frac{10}{7} g h\, }.
\]

4. **Insert the numbers**

\[
v_{0}^{2}= (3.001\times10^{8})^{2}= 9.006001\times10^{16}\ \text{m}^{2}\text{s}^{-2},
\]

\[
g h = 10 \times 6\times10^{8}= 6.0\times10^{9}\ \text{m}^{2}\text{s}^{-2},
\]

\[
\frac{10}{7} g h = \frac{10}{7}\times6.0\times10^{9}
                = 8.5714\times10^{9}\ \text{m}^{2}\text{s}^{-2}.
\]

Now

\[
v_{f}= \sqrt{ 9.006001\times10^{16}+ 8.5714\times10^{9}}
     \approx \sqrt{ 9.00600109\times10^{16}}
     \approx 3.001000014\times10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}.
\]

5. **Result**

The speed at the bottom of the inclined plane is essentially the same as the launch speed, increased by only about 14 m s⁻¹ because the gravitational gain is tiny compared with the enormous initial kinetic energy.

\[
\boxed{v_{f} \approx 3.001 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m s}^{-1}}
\]

(If only three‑significant‑figure precision is required, the answer can be given simply as \(3.001\times10^{8}\,\text{m/s}\).)

*Note:* The given initial speed exceeds the speed of light, so the problem is purely a mathematical exercise; in a real physical situation relativistic limits would have to be taken into account.

**Given data**  
- Mass of the sphere, \(m = 2\; \text{kg}\) (the mass cancels out, so the actual value isn’t needed for the speed).  
- Height of the start point, \(h = 7\; \text{m}\).  
- Gravitational acceleration, \(g = 10\; \text{m/s}^2\).  
- The sphere rolls without slipping, so translational speed \(v\) and angular speed \(\omega\) are related by \(v = \omega R\).  
- Moment of inertia of a solid sphere about its centre, \(I = \frac{2}{5} m R^2\).

**Energy conservation**

At the top the sphere has only gravitational potential energy:
\[
U_i = m g h .
\]

At the bottom it has translational kinetic energy \(\frac12 m v^2\) and rotational kinetic energy \(\frac12 I \omega^2\).

Using the no‑slip condition \(\omega = v/R\) and substituting \(I\):
\[
\text{Rotational KE} = \frac12 \left(\frac{2}{5} m R^2\right) \left(\frac{v^2}{R^2}\right)
                     = \frac15 m v^2 .
\]

Total kinetic energy at the bottom:
\[
K_f = \frac12 m v^2 + \frac15 m v^2
    = \left(\frac12 + \frac15\right) m v^2
    = \frac{7}{10} m v^2 .
\]

Set the loss in potential energy equal to the gain in kinetic energy:

\[
m g h = \frac{7}{10} m v^2 .
\]

The mass cancels:

\[
g h = \frac{7}{10} v^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad v^2 = \frac{10}{7}\, g h .
\]

Insert the numbers:

\[
v^2 = \frac{10}{7} \times 10 \times 7 = 100 .
\]

Therefore

\[
v = \sqrt{100} = 10\; \text{m/s}.
\]

**Result**  
The solid sphere reaches the bottom of the incline with a speed of **10 m/s**.

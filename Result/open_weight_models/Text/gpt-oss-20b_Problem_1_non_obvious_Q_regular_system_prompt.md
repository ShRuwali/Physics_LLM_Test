Check that block slides down 5 meters: along plane? It says "slides down a total distance of 5 meters before it touches the ground". That implies along the inclined plane. Good.

Thus answer.assistantfinalThe motion is one‑dimensional along the plane and the forces that change the speed are constant, so the block has constant (net) acceleration.  
We find that acceleration first, then use the kinematic formula for distance

\[
s = \tfrac12 a t^{2}
\]

to solve for the time \(t\).

---

**1. Net acceleration along the plane**

The component of gravity pulling the block down the plane is

\[
g_{\text{parallel}} = g \sin \theta = 10 \,\text{m/s}^2 \times \sin 30^\circ = 5 \,\text{m/s}^2 .
\]

The kinetic‑friction force acts up the plane. Its magnitude is

\[
f_k = \mu_k N = \mu_k\, g \cos \theta 
     = 0.5 \times 10 \,\text{m/s}^2 \times \cos 30^\circ 
     = 5 \times 0.866 \approx 4.33 \,\text{m/s}^2 .
\]

The net acceleration is the difference:

\[
a = g_{\text{parallel}} - f_k
   = 5 - 4.33 \approx 0.67 \,\text{m/s}^2 .
\]

(The mass of the block doesn’t enter the acceleration; it cancels out because both the driving and frictional forces are proportional to the mass.)

---

**2. Time to travel 5 m up the plane**

With a starting speed of zero, the distance along the inclined plane after time \(t\) is

\[
s = \tfrac12 a t^2 .
\]

Solve for \(t\):

\[
t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}
    = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 5\,\text{m}}{0.67\,\text{m/s}^2}}
    = \sqrt{ \frac{10}{0.67} } 
    \approx \sqrt{14.93} 
    \approx 3.9 \text{ s}.
\]

---

\[
\boxed{t \;\approx\; 3.9\ \text{seconds}}
\]

So the block takes roughly four seconds to slide 5 m down the inclined plane under the given conditions.

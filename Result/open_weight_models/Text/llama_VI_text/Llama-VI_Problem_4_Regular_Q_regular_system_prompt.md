To solve this problem, we need to consider the forces acting on the object at the bottom of the circle. The forces acting on the object are:

1. Tension (T) in the string
2. Weight (mg) of the object
3. Centripetal force (F_c) required to keep the object in circular motion

At the bottom of the circle, the weight of the object (mg) is acting downwards, and the tension (T) in the string is acting upwards. The centripetal force (F_c) is also acting towards the center of the circle.

We can write the equation for centripetal force as:

F_c = (m * v^2) / R

where m is the mass of the object, v is the speed of the object, and R is the radius of the circle.

Given values:
m = 10 nanograms = 10 * 10^-9 kg
v = 5.0 m/s
R = 5.0 meters

F_c = (10 * 10^-9 * 5^2) / 5
F_c = (10 * 10^-9 * 25) / 5
F_c = 0.05 * 10^-7 N

The weight of the object is:
mg = 10 * 10^-9 * 10
mg = 0.1 * 10^-7 N

At the bottom of the circle, the net force acting on the object is the sum of the tension (T) and the weight (mg) minus the centripetal force (F_c). Since the object is in circular motion, the net force must be equal to the centripetal force.

T - mg = F_c
T - 0.1 * 10^-7 = 0.05 * 10^-7

T = 0.05 * 10^-7 + 0.1 * 10^-7
T = 0.15 * 10^-7 N

Therefore, the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is 0.15 * 10^-7 N.

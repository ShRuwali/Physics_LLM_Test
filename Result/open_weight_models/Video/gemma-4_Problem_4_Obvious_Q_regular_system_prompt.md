The video shows a small body of mass $m$ moving in a vertical circular motion attached to a highly elastic string. The body moves at a constant speed $v$ in a circle of radius $R$.

### Given Data:
- Mass of the body ($m$) = $10 \text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-11} \text{ kg}$
- Radius of the body ($r$) = $100 \text{ nanometers}$ (not needed for tension calculation)
- Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5 \text{ cm} = 0.05 \text{ m}$
- Speed of the body ($v$) = $3.001 \times 10^3 \text{ m/s}$
- Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10 \text{ m/s}^2$

### Calculation of Tension at the Bottom:
When the body is at the bottom of the vertical circle, two forces act on it: the tension ($T$) in the string acting upwards and the weight ($mg$) acting downwards. The net force provides the centripetal acceleration ($a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$) directed towards the center (upwards).

The equation of motion at the bottom is:
$$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

Rearranging to solve for tension ($T$):
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

Substituting the given values:
$$T = 10^{-11} \text{ kg} \times \left( 10 \text{ m/s}^2 + \frac{(3.001 \times 10^3 \text{ m/s})^2}{0.05 \text{ m}} \right)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \times \left( 10 + \frac{9.006 \times 10^6}{0.05} \right)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \times (10 + 1.8012 \times 10^8)$$
$$T \approx 10^{-11} \times 1.8012 \times 10^8$$
$$T \approx 1.8012 \times 10^{-3} \text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is approximately $1.8012 \times 10^{-3} \text{ N}$.

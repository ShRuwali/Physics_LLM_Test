The video shows a mass $m$ moving in a vertical circular motion with a radius $R$. The motion is governed by the forces of gravity and the tension in the string.

### Given Data:
*   Mass of the body ($m$) = $5.0 \text{ kg}$
*   Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5.0 \text{ meters}$
*   Speed of the body ($v$) = $5.0 \text{ m/s}$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$
*   The string is assumed to be inextensible and non-stretchable.

### Calculation of Tension at the Bottom:
At the bottom of the vertical circle, two forces act on the mass:
1.  The tension ($T$) in the string acting upwards toward the center.
2.  The weight of the body ($mg$) acting downwards.

The net force towards the center provides the centripetal acceleration ($a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$). The equation of motion is:
$$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

To find the tension ($T$), we rearrange the formula:
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

Substituting the given values:
$$T = 5.0 \left( 10.0 + \frac{5.0^2}{5.0} \right)$$
$$T = 5.0 (10.0 + 5.0)$$
$$T = 5.0 (15.0)$$
$$T = 75 \text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is **$75 \text{ N}$**.

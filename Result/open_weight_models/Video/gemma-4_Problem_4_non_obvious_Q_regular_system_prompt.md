The video illustrates a body of mass $m$ moving in a vertical circular motion. The body is attached to a string that is neither stretched nor loose. The motion is governed by the following parameters:

*   Mass of the body ($m$) = $10$ nanograms = $10 \times 10^{-12}$ kg
*   Radius of the body = $100$ nanometers
*   Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5.0$ meters
*   Speed of the body ($v$) = $5.0$ m/s
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $100$ m/s$^2$

### Calculation of Tension at the Bottom

At the bottom of the vertical circle, two forces act on the body: the tension ($T$) in the string acting upwards and the weight ($mg$) acting downwards. The net force provides the centripetal acceleration ($a_c$) required for circular motion.

The centripetal acceleration is given by:
$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$$

The equation of motion at the bottom is:
$$T - mg = m \cdot a_c$$
$$T = mg + m \frac{v^2}{R}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

Substituting the given values:
$$T = (10 \times 10^{-12} \text{ kg}) \left( 100 \text{ m/s}^2 + \frac{(5.0 \text{ m/s})^2}{5.0 \text{ m}} \right)$$
$$T = (10 \times 10^{-12}) \left( 100 + \frac{25}{5} \right)$$
$$T = (10 \times 10^{-12}) (100 + 5)$$
$$T = (10 \times 10^{-12}) (105)$$
$$T = 1.05 \times 10^{-9} \text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is $1.05 \times 10^{-9}$ Newtons.

The video shows a physical system consisting of a sphere suspended by a string of negligible mass. The sphere is in a state of equilibrium, and the problem asks to calculate the time period of oscillation for small displacements.

### **Given Data:**
*   **Diameter of sphere ($D$):** $1\text{ nm} = 1 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$
*   **Radius of sphere ($R$):** $\frac{D}{2} = 0.5 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$
*   **Mass of sphere ($m$):** $10^{-15}\text{ kg}$
*   **Distance between point of support and center of sphere ($L$):** $2.0\text{ m}$
*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$

### **Calculation:**
For a simple pendulum (or a sphere suspended by a string), the time period of oscillation ($T$) for small displacements is given by the formula:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

Where:
*   $L$ is the length of the pendulum (distance from support to center of mass).
*   $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

Substituting the given values:
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2.0\text{ m}}{10.0\text{ m/s}^2}}$$
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{0.2}$$
$$T \approx 2 \times 3.14159 \times 0.4472$$
$$T \approx 2.80\text{ seconds}$$

**Note:** The mass and diameter of the sphere are provided in the video but are not required to calculate the time period of a simple pendulum, as the period is independent of mass and amplitude for small oscillations.

**Final Answer:**
The time period of oscillation for small displacements is approximately **$2.80\text{ seconds}$**.

To find the velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane, we use the principle of **Conservation of Mechanical Energy**.

### 1. Given Data:
*   **Mass of the solid sphere ($m$):** $2.0\text{ kg}$
*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$
*   **Initial Height ($h$):** $7.0\text{ m}$
*   **Moment of Inertia of a solid sphere ($I$):** $\frac{2}{5}mr^2$
*   **Condition:** The sphere rolls without slipping from rest.

### 2. Energy Analysis:
Since there is no friction loss (rolling without slipping) and no external work is done, the potential energy at the top is converted into kinetic energy at the bottom.

**Potential Energy at the top ($PE$):**
$$PE = mgh$$

**Kinetic Energy at the bottom ($KE$):**
The total kinetic energy is the sum of translational kinetic energy and rotational kinetic energy:
$$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$$

For a sphere rolling without slipping, the relationship between linear velocity ($v$) and angular velocity ($\omega$) is:
$$\omega = \frac{v}{r}$$

Substituting $I$ and $\omega$ into the $KE$ equation:
$$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2$$
$$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{5}mv^2 = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$$

### 3. Calculation:
Equating $PE$ to $KE$:
$$mgh = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$$
$$gh = \frac{7}{10}v^2$$
$$v^2 = \frac{10gh}{7}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10 \times 10.0 \times 7.0}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{700}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{100}$$
$$v = 10\text{ m/s}$$

**Final Answer:**
The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is **$10\text{ m/s}$**.

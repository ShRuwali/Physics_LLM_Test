To find the velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane, we use the principle of **Conservation of Mechanical Energy**.

### 1. Given Data:
*   **Mass of the solid sphere ($m$):** $2.0\text{ kg}$
*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$
*   **Initial height ($h$):** $7.0\text{ meters}$
*   **Initial velocity ($v_i$):** $0\text{ m/s}$ (starts from rest)
*   **Moment of Inertia of a solid sphere ($I$):** $\frac{2}{5}mr^2$
*   **Condition:** Rolling without slipping (where $v = \omega r$)

### 2. Energy Equations:
At the top of the plane, the sphere has only **Gravitational Potential Energy ($PE$):**
$$PE = mgh$$

At the bottom of the plane, the energy has converted into **Translational Kinetic Energy ($KE_{trans}$)** and **Rotational Kinetic Energy ($KE_{rot}$):**
$$KE_{total} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$$

Since it rolls without slipping, we substitute $\omega = \frac{v}{r}$ and $I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$:
$$KE_{total} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2$$
$$KE_{total} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{5}mv^2 = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$$

### 3. Calculation:
By Conservation of Energy ($PE = KE_{total}$):
$$mgh = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$$

The mass ($m$) cancels out from both sides:
$$gh = \frac{7}{10}v^2$$
$$v^2 = \frac{10gh}{7}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10 \cdot 10.0 \cdot 7.0}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{700}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{100}$$
$$v = 10\text{ m/s}$$

**Final Answer:**
The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is **$10\text{ m/s}$**.

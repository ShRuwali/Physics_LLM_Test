Based on the provided image and video, here is the physical analysis and calculation for the velocity of the solid sphere.

### Description
The video shows a solid sphere (rigid body) starting from rest at a height $h$ and rolling down an inclined plane without slipping. The goal is to find the final velocity of the sphere at the bottom of the plane using the principle of conservation of mechanical energy.

### Given Data
*   **Mass of the sphere ($m$):** $\approx 4 \text{ nanograms} = 4 \times 10^{-12} \text{ kg}$
*   **Diameter of the sphere ($d$):** $8 \text{ nanometers} = 8 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
*   **Radius of the sphere ($r$):** $\frac{d}{2} = 4 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$
*   **Height ($h$):** $7.0 \text{ meters}$
*   **Moment of Inertia of a solid sphere ($I$):** $\frac{2}{5}mr^2$

### Calculation
Since the sphere rolls without slipping, the total mechanical energy at the top (potential energy) is equal to the total mechanical energy at the bottom (kinetic energy). The kinetic energy consists of both translational and rotational components.

**1. Energy at the top:**
$$E_{top} = mgh$$

**2. Energy at the bottom:**
$$E_{bottom} = K_{trans} + K_{rot} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$$

**3. Relationship for rolling without slipping:**
The angular velocity $\omega$ is related to the linear velocity $v$ by:
$$\omega = \frac{v}{r}$$

**4. Conservation of Energy Equation:**
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2$$

Simplify the equation by canceling out the mass $m$ and $r^2$:
$$gh = \frac{1}{2}v^2 + \frac{1}{5}v^2$$
$$gh = \left(\frac{5}{10} + \frac{2}{10}\right)v^2$$
$$gh = \frac{7}{10}v^2$$

**5. Solving for velocity ($v$):**
$$v^2 = \frac{10gh}{7}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10gh}{7}}$$

Substitute the given values:
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10 \cdot 10.0 \cdot 7.0}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{700}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{100}$$
$$v = 10 \text{ m/s}$$

### Conclusion
The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is **$10 \text{ m/s}$**.

**Physical Justification:**
The solution is physically justifiable. While the mass and diameter are extremely small (nanoscale), the conservation of energy principle is independent of the mass $m$ for a rolling object, as $m$ cancels out in the final velocity equation. The result $10 \text{ m/s}$ is a reasonable magnitude for an object falling/rolling from a height of $7$ meters.

To find the velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane, we use the principle of **Conservation of Mechanical Energy**.

### 1. Given Data:
*   **Mass ($m$):** $4 \text{ nanograms} = 4 \times 10^{-12} \text{ kg}$
*   **Diameter ($d$):** $8 \text{ nanometers} = 8 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$
*   **Height ($h$):** $7.0 \text{ meters}$
*   **Condition:** The sphere rolls without slipping from rest.

### 2. Physics Principles:
For a solid sphere rolling without slipping, the total kinetic energy ($K$) is the sum of translational kinetic energy and rotational kinetic energy:
$$K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2$$
Where:
*   $I$ is the moment of inertia for a solid sphere: $I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$
*   $\omega$ is angular velocity: $\omega = \frac{v}{r}$ (where $r$ is the radius)

Substituting these into the kinetic energy equation:
$$K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{5}mv^2 = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$$

### 3. Calculation:
By conservation of energy, the potential energy at the top equals the kinetic energy at the bottom:
$$mgh = \frac{7}{10}mv^2$$
The mass ($m$) cancels out from both sides:
$$gh = \frac{7}{10}v^2$$
$$v^2 = \frac{10gh}{7}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10gh}{7}}$$

Plugging in the values:
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{10 \times 10.0 \times 7.0}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{700}{7}}$$
$$v = \sqrt{100}$$
$$v = 10 \text{ m/s}$$

**Final Answer:**
The velocity of the solid sphere at the bottom of the plane is **$10 \text{ m/s}$**.

The video shows a small body of mass $m$ moving in a circular path of radius $R$ with a constant speed $v$. The body is attached to a string, and the motion is occurring in a gravitational field with acceleration $g$.

To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle, we must analyze the forces acting on the body at that specific point.

### Given Data:
*   Mass of the body ($m$) = $10 \text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10 \times 10^{-12} \text{ kg} = 10^{-11} \text{ kg}$
*   Radius of the body = $100 \text{ nanometers}$ (not needed for tension calculation)
*   Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5.0 \text{ meters}$
*   Speed of the body ($v$) = $3.0011 \times 10^4 \text{ m/s}$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$

### Physics Principles:
At the bottom of the circular path, two main forces act on the body:
1.  The tension ($T$) in the string, acting upwards toward the center of the circle.
2.  The weight of the body ($mg$), acting downwards.

The net force acting toward the center of the circle provides the centripetal force ($F_c$) required for circular motion:
$$F_{net} = F_c$$
$$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

### Calculation:
Rearranging the formula to solve for tension ($T$):
$$T = \frac{mv^2}{R} + mg$$
$$T = m \left( \frac{v^2}{R} + g \right)$$

Substitute the given values:
$$T = 10^{-11} \text{ kg} \times \left( \frac{(3.0011 \times 10^4 \text{ m/s})^2}{5.0 \text{ m}} + 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2 \right)$$

First, calculate the centripetal acceleration term:
$$\frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(3.0011 \times 10^4)^2}{5.0} \approx \frac{9.0066 \times 10^8}{5.0} \approx 1.8013 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}^2$$

Since $1.8013 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}^2$ is much larger than $g$ ($10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$), the gravity term is negligible, but we include it for precision:
$$T = 10^{-11} \text{ kg} \times (1.8013 \times 10^8 + 10.0) \text{ m/s}^2$$
$$T \approx 10^{-11} \times 1.8013 \times 10^8 \text{ N}$$
$$T \approx 1.8013 \times 10^{-3} \text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is approximately $1.80 \times 10^{-3} \text{ N}$.

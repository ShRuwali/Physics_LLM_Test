The video shows a small body of mass $m = 10\text{ nanograms}$ moving in a circular path of radius $R = 50\text{ meters}$. The body is moving at a constant speed of $v = 3.001 \times 10^4\text{ m/s}$. The acceleration due to gravity is $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$.

To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle, we first need to determine the centripetal acceleration ($a_c$) required for the body to maintain its circular motion:
$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$$
Substituting the given values:
$$a_c = \frac{(3.001 \times 10^4\text{ m/s})^2}{50\text{ m}} \approx \frac{9 \times 10^8}{50} = 1.8 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}^2$$

At the bottom of the circle, the net force is the centripetal force, which is provided by the tension ($T$) minus the weight of the body ($mg$):
$$T - mg = m a_c$$
$$T = m(g + a_c)$$

Given that $a_c = 1.8 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}^2$ is significantly larger than $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$, the weight of the body is negligible.
$$T \approx m a_c$$
Converting the mass to kilograms:
$$m = 10\text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 10^{-11}\text{ kg}$$
$$T = (10^{-11}\text{ kg}) \times (1.8 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}^2) = 1.8 \times 10^{-4}\text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is approximately **$1.8 \times 10^{-4}\text{ Newtons}$**.

The video shows a small body of mass $m = 10\text{ nanograms}$ moving in a circular path with a radius $R = 5.0\text{ meters}$ and a speed $v = 3.0011 \times 10^4\text{ m/s}$. The acceleration due to gravity is $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$.

To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle, we use the principles of circular motion and dynamics.

### 1. Identify Given Values:
*   Mass ($m$) = $10\text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 10 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 10^{-11}\text{ kg}$
*   Radius ($R$) = $5.0\text{ m}$
*   Speed ($v$) = $3.0011 \times 10^4\text{ m/s}$
*   Gravity ($g$) = $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$

### 2. Formula for Tension at the Bottom:
At the bottom of the circular path, the tension ($T$) in the string acts upward, while the weight ($mg$) acts downward. The net force provides the centripetal acceleration ($a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$):
$$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R} = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

### 3. Calculation:
First, calculate the centripetal acceleration ($a_c$):
$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(3.0011 \times 10^4\text{ m/s})^2}{5.0\text{ m}} \approx \frac{9.0066 \times 10^8}{5.0} \approx 1.8013 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}^2$$

Now, calculate the tension ($T$):
$$T = 10^{-11}\text{ kg} \times (10.0\text{ m/s}^2 + 1.8013 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}^2)$$
Since $1.8013 \times 10^8$ is much larger than $10.0$, we can approximate:
$$T \approx 10^{-11}\text{ kg} \times 1.8013 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}^2$$
$$T \approx 1.8013 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Newtons}$$

**The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is approximately $1.80 \times 10^{-3}\text{ N}$.**

The video shows a body of mass $m = 5.0\text{ kg}$ moving in a circular path of radius $R = 5.0\text{ meters}$ with a constant speed $v = 5.0\text{ m/s}$. The acceleration due to gravity is $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$. The body is attached to a string, and we need to find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circular motion.

### Given Data:
- Mass of the body ($m$) = $5.0\text{ kg}$
- Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5.0\text{ m}$
- Speed of the body ($v$) = $5.0\text{ m/s}$
- Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$

### Calculation:
At the bottom of the circular path, two forces act on the body:
1. The tension ($T$) in the string acting upwards.
2. The weight ($mg$) of the body acting downwards.

The net force acting towards the center of the circle provides the centripetal force ($F_c$). The centripetal force is given by:
$$F_c = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

At the bottom of the circle, the net force is the difference between the tension and the weight:
$$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

To find the tension ($T$), we rearrange the formula:
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

Substituting the given values:
$$T = 5.0 \left( 10.0 + \frac{5.0^2}{5.0} \right)$$
$$T = 5.0 (10.0 + 5.0)$$
$$T = 5.0 (15.0)$$
$$T = 75.0\text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is $75.0\text{ N}$.

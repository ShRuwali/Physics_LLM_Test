The video shows a body of mass $m = 5.0\text{ kg}$ moving in a circular path of radius $r = 5.0\text{ m}$ with a constant speed $v = 5.0\text{ m/s}$. The acceleration due to gravity is $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$.

To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle, we use the principles of circular motion and dynamics.

**1. Identify the forces acting on the body at the bottom of the circle:**
*   **Tension ($T$):** Acts upwards towards the center of the circle.
*   **Weight ($mg$):** Acts downwards.

**2. Apply Newton's Second Law:**
The net force in the radial direction provides the centripetal acceleration ($a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$):
$$\sum F_{radial} = T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{r}$$

**3. Solve for Tension ($T$):**
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{r}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{r} \right)$$

**4. Substitute the given values:**
*   $m = 5.0\text{ kg}$
*   $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$
*   $v = 5.0\text{ m/s}$
*   $r = 5.0\text{ m}$

$$T = 5.0 \left( 10.0 + \frac{5.0^2}{5.0} \right)$$
$$T = 5.0 \left( 10.0 + 5.0 \right)$$
$$T = 5.0 \times 15.0$$
$$T = 75\text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is **$75\text{ N}$**.

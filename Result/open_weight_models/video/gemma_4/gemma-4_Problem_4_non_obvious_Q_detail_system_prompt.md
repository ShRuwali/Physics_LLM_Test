The video shows a body of mass $m$ moving in a circular path of radius $R$ with a constant speed $v$. The body is attached to a string, and the tension $T$ in the string provides the centripetal force required for circular motion.

### Given Data:
*   Mass of the body ($m$) = $10 \text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10 \times 10^{-12} \text{ kg} = 10^{-11} \text{ kg}$
*   Radius of the body = $100 \text{ nanometers}$ (This is the physical size of the object, not used for the orbital mechanics).
*   Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5.0 \text{ meters}$
*   Speed of the body ($v$) = $5.0 \text{ m/s}$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$

### Calculation of Tension at the Bottom:
When the body is at the bottom of the circular path, two forces act on it:
1.  The tension ($T$) in the string acting upwards toward the center.
2.  The weight of the body ($mg$) acting downwards away from the center.

The net force acting toward the center is the centripetal force ($F_c$). According to Newton's Second Law:
$$F_{net} = F_c$$
$$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$

To find the tension $T$:
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

Substituting the given values:
$$T = 10^{-11} \text{ kg} \times \left( 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2 + \frac{(5.0 \text{ m/s})^2}{5.0 \text{ m}} \right)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \times (10.0 + 5.0)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \times 15.0$$
$$T = 1.5 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N}$$

### Physical Analysis:
The parameters provided in the video present a significant physical inconsistency regarding the scale of the system:
1.  **Mass vs. Speed:** The mass of the body is extremely small ($10^{-11} \text{ kg}$), which is roughly the mass of a large biological cell or a very small dust particle. 
2.  **Air Resistance:** At a speed of $5 \text{ m/s}$ and a mass of $10^{-11} \text{ kg}$, the surface-area-to-mass ratio would be enormous. In any real-world environment (not a perfect vacuum), air resistance would dominate the motion, making it impossible to maintain a steady circular orbit at this speed.
3.  **String Integrity:** While the tension is mathematically calculable, a "string" capable of holding a mass of $10^{-11} \text{ kg}$ at $5 \text{ m/s}$ would be so thin that it would likely behave more like a molecular chain than a macroscopic string.

**Conclusion:** While the mathematical solution is $T = 1.5 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N}$, the model is **physically unjustifiable** in a standard atmosphere due to the negligible mass of the object relative to the forces of drag.

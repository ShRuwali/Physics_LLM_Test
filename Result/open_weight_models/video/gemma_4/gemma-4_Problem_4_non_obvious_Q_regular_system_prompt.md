The video shows a small body of mass $m = 10\text{ nanograms}$ moving in a circular path of radius $R = 5\text{ meters}$ with a constant speed of $v = 5\text{ m/s}$. The acceleration due to gravity is $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$.

To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle, we use the principles of circular motion and dynamics.

### 1. Given Data:
*   Mass of the body ($m$) = $10\text{ ng} = 10 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 10^{-11}\text{ kg}$
*   Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5\text{ m}$
*   Speed of the body ($v$) = $5\text{ m/s}$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10\text{ m/s}^2$

### 2. Formulae:
*   Centripetal force ($F_c$) = $\frac{mv^2}{R}$
*   At the bottom of the circle, the tension ($T$) and the weight ($mg$) are both directed upwards. The net force towards the center (upwards) is the centripetal force:
    $$T - mg = \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
    $$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
    $$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

### 3. Calculation:
Substitute the given values into the equation:
$$T = 10^{-11} \times \left( 10 + \frac{5^2}{5} \right)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \times (10 + 5)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \times 15$$
$$T = 1.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}$$

**The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is $1.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ Newtons}$.**

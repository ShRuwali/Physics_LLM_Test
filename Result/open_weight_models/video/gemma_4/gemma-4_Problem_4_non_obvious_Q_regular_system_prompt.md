The video shows a body of mass $m = 10\text{ nanograms}$ moving in a circular path of radius $R = 5.0\text{ meters}$ at a constant speed of $v = 5.0\text{ m/s}$. The acceleration due to gravity is given as $g = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$.

To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle, we use the principles of circular motion and Newton's second law.

### 1. Identify Given Values:
*   Mass ($m$) = $10\text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-9}\text{ grams} = 10 \times 10^{-12}\text{ kg} = 10^{-11}\text{ kg}$
*   Radius ($R$) = $5.0\text{ m}$
*   Speed ($v$) = $5.0\text{ m/s}$
*   Gravity ($g$) = $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$

### 2. Calculate Centripetal Acceleration ($a_c$):
The centripetal acceleration is given by the formula:
$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$$
$$a_c = \frac{(5.0\text{ m/s})^2}{5.0\text{ m}} = \frac{25}{5} = 5.0\text{ m/s}^2$$

### 3. Calculate Tension ($T$) at the bottom:
At the bottom of the circular path, the tension ($T$) acts upward and the weight ($mg$) acts downward. The net force provides the centripetal acceleration directed toward the center (upward):
$$T - mg = m \cdot a_c$$
$$T = m(g + a_c)$$

Substitute the values:
$$T = 10^{-11}\text{ kg} \cdot (10.0\text{ m/s}^2 + 5.0\text{ m/s}^2)$$
$$T = 10^{-11}\text{ kg} \cdot 15.0\text{ m/s}^2$$
$$T = 1.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ Newtons}$$

**Final Answer:**
The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is **$1.5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}$**.

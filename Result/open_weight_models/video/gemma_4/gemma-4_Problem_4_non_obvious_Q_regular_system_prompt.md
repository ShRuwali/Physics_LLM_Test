The video shows a body of mass $m$ moving in a circular path of radius $R$ with a constant speed $v$. The body is attached to a string, and the motion is occurring in a gravitational field with acceleration $g$.

To find the tension in the string at the bottom of the circle, we must analyze the forces acting on the body at that specific point.

### Given Data:
*   Mass of the body ($m$) = $10 \text{ nanograms} = 10 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10 \times 10^{-12} \text{ kg} = 10^{-11} \text{ kg}$
*   Radius of the body = $100 \text{ nanometers}$ (not needed for tension calculation)
*   Radius of circular motion ($R$) = $5.0 \text{ meters}$
*   Speed of the body ($v$) = $5.0 \text{ m/s}$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$

### Physics Principles:
At the bottom of the circular path, two main forces act on the body:
1.  The tension ($T$) in the string, acting upwards toward the center.
2.  The weight ($mg$) of the body, acting downwards.

The net force acting toward the center of the circle provides the centripetal acceleration ($a_c$). The formula for centripetal acceleration is:
$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{R}$$

The net force equation at the bottom is:
$$F_{net} = T - mg = m \cdot a_c$$
$$T - mg = m \frac{v^2}{R}$$

### Calculation:
Rearranging the formula to solve for tension ($T$):
$$T = mg + \frac{mv^2}{R}$$
$$T = m \left( g + \frac{v^2}{R} \right)$$

Substitute the given values:
$$T = 10^{-11} \text{ kg} \cdot \left( 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2 + \frac{(5.0 \text{ m/s})^2}{5.0 \text{ m}} \right)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \cdot (10.0 + \frac{25.0}{5.0})$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \cdot (10.0 + 5.0)$$
$$T = 10^{-11} \cdot 15.0$$
$$T = 1.5 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N}$$

The tension in the string at the bottom of the circle is $1.5 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N}$.

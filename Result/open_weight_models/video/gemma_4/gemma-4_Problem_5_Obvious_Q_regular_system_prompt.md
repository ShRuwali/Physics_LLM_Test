Based on the video provided, we are analyzing a collision between two blocks, Block A and Block B.

### Description
Block B is moving towards the right with an initial velocity $v_{B1} = 2.0 \text{ m/s}$. Block A is initially at rest ($v_{A1} = 0 \text{ m/s}$). Upon collision, Block B's velocity changes to $v_{B2} = 10 \text{ m/s}$ (moving in the same direction). We need to find the final velocity of Block A ($v_{A2}$) after the collision.

### Given Data
*   **Block A:**
    *   Mass $m_A = 100 \text{ nanograms} = 100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-7} \text{ g} = 10^{-10} \text{ kg}$
    *   Initial velocity $v_{A1} = 0 \text{ m/s}$
*   **Block B:**
    *   Mass $m_B = 0.20 \text{ kg}$
    *   Initial velocity $v_{B1} = 2.0 \text{ m/s}$
    *   Final velocity $v_{B2} = 10 \text{ m/s}$

### Calculation
We use the principle of **Conservation of Momentum**, which states that the total momentum before the collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision:
$$m_A v_{A1} + m_B v_{B1} = m_A v_{A2} + m_B v_{B2}$$

Substitute the known values into the equation:
$$(10^{-10} \text{ kg})(0 \text{ m/s}) + (0.20 \text{ kg})(2.0 \text{ m/s}) = (10^{-10} \text{ kg})v_{A2} + (0.20 \text{ kg})(10 \text{ m/s})$$

Simplify the equation:
$$0 + 0.40 = (10^{-10})v_{A2} + 2.0$$

Isolate the term with $v_{A2}$:
$$(10^{-10})v_{A2} = 0.40 - 2.0$$
$$(10^{-10})v_{A2} = -1.6$$

Solve for $v_{A2}$:
$$v_{A2} = \frac{-1.6}{10^{-10}}$$
$$v_{A2} = -1.6 \times 10^{10} \text{ m/s}$$

**Final Answer:**
The velocity of Block A after the collision is $v_{A2} = -1.6 \times 10^{10} \text{ m/s}$. (The negative sign indicates that Block A moves in the opposite direction of Block B).

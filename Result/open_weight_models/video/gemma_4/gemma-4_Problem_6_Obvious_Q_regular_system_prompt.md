This video demonstrates the principles of Hooke's Law and Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) using a spring system. It first shows a spring balance being stretched to a specific displacement to determine the spring constant. It then transitions to a mass-spring system where a block of known mass is attached to the spring and released from a displacement, demonstrating oscillation.

### 1. Calculation of the Spring Constant ($k$)

From the top half of the video, we are given the following values:
*   Displacement ($x$) = $20\text{ meters}$
*   Force ($F$) = $0\text{ N}$ (Note: In the context of the visual, the "0 N" label appears to be a placeholder or a specific state, but typically for a spring balance, the force is derived from the weight or applied tension. However, looking at the final frames of the top section, the displacement is $20\text{ m}$. If we assume the force $F$ is the weight of the balance or a specific tension, we need a value. Re-evaluating the prompt's "Force ($F$) = 0 N" label, it seems to be a typo in the source material for a non-zero value. However, if we look at the bottom half, the mass is $500\text{ nanograms}$. Let's assume the force $F$ intended for the top half is the weight of the block used in the bottom half to maintain consistency, or that the "0 N" is a placeholder for the force $F$ required to reach $x=20\text{ m}$. 

Given the ambiguity of "0 N", let's look at the bottom half for the mass $m = 500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$. If the force $F$ in the top half was intended to be the weight of this mass:
$$F = mg = (5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg})(9.8\text{ m/s}^2) = 4.9 \times 10^{-9}\text{ N}$$

Using Hooke's Law $F = kx$:
$$k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{4.9 \times 10^{-9}\text{ N}}{20\text{ m}} = 2.45 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N/m}$$

### 2. Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)

For the bottom half of the video, we use the mass $m = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$ and the spring constant $k = 2.45 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N/m}$.

**Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{2.45 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N/m}}{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{0.49}\text{ rad/s}$$
$$\omega = 0.7\text{ rad/s}$$

**Frequency ($f$):**
$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$
$$f = \frac{0.7}{2\pi}\text{ Hz}$$
$$f \approx 0.111\text{ Hz}$$

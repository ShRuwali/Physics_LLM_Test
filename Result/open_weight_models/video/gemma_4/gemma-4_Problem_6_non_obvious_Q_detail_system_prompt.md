The video illustrates a physics experiment involving a spring system. In the first part, a spring balance is attached to a spring, and a force of $5.0\text{ N}$ is applied, resulting in a displacement of $0.020\text{ m}$. In the second part, a block of mass $m = 500\text{ mg}$ is attached to the spring, and it is released from rest.

### 1. Calculation of the Spring Constant ($k$)
From the top half of the video, the force $F$ and displacement $x$ are given as:
*   Force ($F$) = $5.0\text{ N}$
*   Displacement ($x$) = $0.020\text{ m}$

Using Hooke's Law ($F = kx$):
$$k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = 250\text{ N/m}$$

### 2. Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)
From the bottom half of the video, the mass $m$ is given as:
*   Mass ($m$) = $500\text{ mg} = 500 \times 10^{-6}\text{ kg} = 0.0005\text{ kg}$
*   Spring constant ($k$) = $250\text{ N/m}$

The angular frequency ($\omega$) for a mass-spring system is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{250}{0.0005}} = \sqrt{500,000} \approx 707.1\text{ rad/s}$$

The frequency ($f$) is:
$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{707.1}{2\pi} \approx 112.3\text{ Hz}$$

### 3. Physical Analysis and Feasibility
**Physical Laws and Constraints:**
While the mathematical calculations can be performed, the physical parameters provided in the video are highly unrealistic for a standard laboratory setup:
1.  **Mass vs. Force:** A mass of $500\text{ mg}$ (about the weight of a paperclip) is extremely light. To produce a measurable displacement on a spring with a constant of $250\text{ N/m}$ (which is quite stiff), the force exerted by such a small mass would be negligible.
2.  **Damping:** In a real-world scenario, a mass this light would be dominated by air resistance and internal friction (damping) almost instantly, preventing any observable oscillation.
3.  **Frequency:** A frequency of $112\text{ Hz}$ is very high for a mechanical spring system, likely resulting in vibrations that are difficult to observe clearly without high-speed equipment.

**Conclusion:**
The problem can be solved mathematically using the provided values, but the physical setup described is impractical because the mass is too small to overcome the inertia and damping of the system effectively.

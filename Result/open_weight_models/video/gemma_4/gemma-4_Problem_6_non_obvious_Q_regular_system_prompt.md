Based on the video provided, here is the analysis of the physics problems presented:

### **1. Description of the Video**
The video demonstrates two scenarios involving a spring system. 
*   **Top half:** A spring is attached to a spring balance. A force of $5.0\text{ N}$ is applied, resulting in a displacement of $0.020\text{ meters}$.
*   **Bottom half:** The spring balance is replaced with a block of mass $m = 500\text{ nanograms}$ and dimensions $40\text{ nm} \times 40\text{ nm} \times 40\text{ nm}$. The block is pulled to a displacement of $0.07\text{ meters}$ and then released to undergo simple harmonic motion.

---

### **2. Calculation of the Spring Constant ($k$)**
From the top half of the video, we use Hooke's Law:
$$F = k \cdot x$$
Where:
*   $F = 5.0\text{ N}$
*   $x = 0.020\text{ m}$

$$k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = \mathbf{250\text{ N/m}}$$

---

### **3. Calculation for the Spring and Block**
In the bottom half, the block is attached to the same spring ($k = 250\text{ N/m}$).
First, we convert the mass to kilograms:
$$m = 500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ g} = \mathbf{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}$$

**Angular Frequency ($\omega$):**
The formula for angular frequency is $\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$.
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}} = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{11}} \approx \mathbf{707,106.78\text{ rad/s}}$$

**Frequency ($f$):**
The formula for frequency is $f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$.
$$f = \frac{707,106.78}{2\pi} \approx \mathbf{112,539.5\text{ Hz (or } \approx 112.5\text{ kHz)}}$$

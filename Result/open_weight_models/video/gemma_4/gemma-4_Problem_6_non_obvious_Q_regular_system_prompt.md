The video illustrates a physics experiment involving a spring and two different objects: a spring balance and a block.

### **1. Force Constant of the Spring**
From the top half of the video, the spring balance is displaced by a distance $x = 0.020\text{ m}$ when a force of $F = 5.0\text{ N}$ is applied.
The force constant $k$ is calculated using Hooke's Law:
$$F = kx \implies k = \frac{F}{x}$$
$$k = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}} = 250\text{ N/m}$$

### **2. Angular Frequency and Frequency of Oscillation**
In the bottom half of the video, the spring is attached to a block of mass $m = 500\text{ mg}$.
First, convert the mass to kilograms:
$$m = 500 \times 10^{-6}\text{ kg} = 0.0005\text{ kg}$$

The **angular frequency** ($\omega$) for a mass-spring system is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{0.0005\text{ kg}}} = \sqrt{500,000} \approx 707.1\text{ rad/s}$$

The **frequency** ($f$) is:
$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$
$$f = \frac{707.1}{2\pi} \approx 112.5\text{ Hz}$$

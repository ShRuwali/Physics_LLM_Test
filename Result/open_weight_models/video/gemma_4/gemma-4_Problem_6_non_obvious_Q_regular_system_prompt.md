This video demonstrates the principles of Hooke's Law and simple harmonic motion using a spring system. It first shows a spring balance being stretched by a known force to determine the spring constant. Then, it shows a block of known mass attached to the same spring, illustrating how the spring constant relates to the period and frequency of oscillation when the block is released.

### 1. Calculation of the Spring Constant ($k$)

From the top half of the video, we are given:
*   Force ($F$) = $5.0\text{ N}$
*   Displacement ($x$) = $0.020\text{ meters}$

According to Hooke's Law:
$$F = kx$$

Rearranging to solve for the spring constant ($k$):
$$k = \frac{F}{x}$$
$$k = \frac{5.0\text{ N}}{0.020\text{ m}}$$
$$k = 250\text{ N/m}$$

---

### 2. Calculation of Angular Frequency ($\omega$) and Frequency ($f$)

From the bottom half of the video, we are given the properties of the block:
*   Mass ($m$) = $500\text{ nanograms} = 500 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ g} = 5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}$
*   Spring constant ($k$) = $250\text{ N/m}$ (calculated above)

The angular frequency ($\omega$) for a mass-spring system is given by:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{250\text{ N/m}}{5 \times 10^{-10}\text{ kg}}}$$
$$\omega = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{11}\text{ rad}^2/\text{s}^2}$$
$$\omega \approx 707,106.78\text{ rad/s}$$

The frequency ($f$) is related to the angular frequency by:
$$f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$$
$$f = \frac{707,106.78}{2\pi}$$
$$f \approx 112,539.54\text{ Hz}$$

The video shows a block of mass $m$ suspended by a cable above a container of sea water. The block is partially submerged in the water, and the system is in equilibrium.

To find the tension $T$ in the cable, we can analyze the forces acting on the block. The block is subject to three forces:
1. The weight of the block acting downwards: $W = m \cdot g$
2. The buoyant force acting upwards: $F_B = \rho_{water} \cdot V_{submerged} \cdot g$
3. The tension in the cable acting upwards: $T$

Since the block is in equilibrium, the sum of the upward forces must equal the sum of the downward forces:
$$T + F_B = m \cdot g$$
$$T = m \cdot g - F_B$$

- Mass of block ($m$) = $20.0 \text{ kg}$
- Density of block ($D$) = $19.0 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$

- Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$

First, we calculate the volume of the block ($V_{block}$):
$$V_{block} = \frac{m}{D} = \frac{20.0 \text{ kg}}{19.0 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3} \approx 0.0010526 \text{ m}^3$$

Next, we determine the submerged volume ($V_{submerged}$). From the visual representation, the block is submerged to a depth that appears to be exactly half of its height (or half of its volume). Assuming the block is half-submerged:
$$V_{submerged} = \frac{1}{2} V_{block} = \frac{0.0010526 \text{ m}^3}{2} \approx 0.0005263 \text{ m}^3$$

Now, we calculate the buoyant force ($F_B$):
$$F_B = \rho \cdot V_{submerged} \cdot g$$
$$F_B = (1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3) \cdot (0.0005263 \text{ m}^3) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2)$$
$$F_B \approx 5.42 \text{ N}$$

Finally, we calculate the tension ($T$):
$$T = m \cdot g - F_B$$
$$T = (20.0 \text{ kg} \cdot 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2) - 5.42 \text{ N}$$
$$T = 200 \text{ N} - 5.42 \text{ N}$$
$$T = 194.58 \text{ N}$$

The tension in the cable is approximately $194.58 \text{ N}$.

The video shows a block being lowered into sea water. The block is suspended by a cable, and as it enters the water, it experiences an upward buoyant force, which reduces the tension in the cable.

To calculate the tension ($T$) in the cable when the block is fully submerged, we use the following data:
*   Mass of block ($m$) = $29.0\text{ kg}$
*   Density of block ($D$) = $19.0 \times 10^3\text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Density of sea water ($\rho$) = $1.03 \times 10^3\text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$

**1. Calculate the Weight ($W$) of the block:**
$$W = m \cdot g = 29.0\text{ kg} \times 10.0\text{ m/s}^2 = 290\text{ N}$$

**2. Calculate the Volume ($V$) of the block:**
Since $\text{Density } (D) = \frac{\text{Mass } (m)}{\text{Volume } (V)}$, then:
$$V = \frac{m}{D} = \frac{29.0\text{ kg}}{19.0 \times 10^3\text{ kg/m}^3} \approx 1.526 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3$$

**3. Calculate the Buoyant Force ($F_b$):**
The buoyant force is equal to the weight of the displaced fluid:
$$F_b = \rho \cdot V \cdot g = (1.03 \times 10^3\text{ kg/m}^3) \times (1.526 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3) \times 10.0\text{ m/s}^2 \approx 15.72\text{ N}$$

**4. Calculate the Tension ($T$):**
When the block is in equilibrium (moving at constant velocity or stationary), the forces are:
$$\text{Tension } (T) + \text{Buoyant Force } (F_b) = \text{Weight } (W)$$
$$T = W - F_b = 290\text{ N} - 15.72\text{ N} = 274.28\text{ N}$$

The tension in the cable when the block is fully submerged is approximately **$274.3\text{ N}$**.

The video shows a block suspended by a cable above a container of sea water. The block is partially submerged in the water, and the system is in equilibrium. To find the tension in the cable, we must analyze the forces acting on the block.

### Given Data:
*   Mass of the block ($m$) = $1000 \text{ nanograms} = 1000 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-6} \text{ g} = 10^{-9} \text{ kg}$
*   Dimensions of the block: $L = W = H = 100 \text{ nanometers} = 100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 10^{-7} \text{ m}$
*   Density of the block ($\rho_{block}$) = $1.0 \times 10^9 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Density of sea water ($\rho_{water}$) = $1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$

### Physical Analysis:
The block is in a state of static equilibrium. The forces acting on the block are:
1.  **Tension ($T$)** acting upwards.
2.  **Weight ($W$)** acting downwards.
3.  **Buoyant force ($F_B$)** acting upwards.

The equilibrium equation is:
$$T + F_B = W$$
$$T = W - F_B$$

#### 1. Calculate the Weight ($W$):
$$W = m \cdot g$$
$$W = (10^{-9} \text{ kg}) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2) = 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

#### 2. Calculate the Buoyant Force ($F_B$):
The buoyant force is equal to the weight of the displaced fluid.
$$F_B = \rho_{water} \cdot V_{submerged} \cdot g$$
From the video, the block is submerged exactly halfway into the water.
The total volume of the block ($V_{block}$) is:
$$V_{block} = L \cdot W \cdot H = (10^{-7} \text{ m})^3 = 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3$$
The submerged volume ($V_{submerged}$) is:
$$V_{submerged} = \frac{1}{2} V_{block} = 0.5 \times 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3$$
Now, calculate $F_B$:
$$F_B = (1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3) \cdot (0.5 \times 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2)$$
$$F_B = 5.15 \times 10^{-19} \text{ N}$$

#### 3. Calculate the Tension ($T$):
$$T = W - F_B$$
$$T = 10^{-8} \text{ N} - 5.15 \times 10^{-19} \text{ N}$$
Since $5.15 \times 10^{-19}$ is negligible compared to $10^{-8}$:
$$T \approx 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

1.  **Density Discrepancy:** The density of the block is given as $1.0 \times 10^9 \text{ kg/m}^3$. This is extremely high (comparable to the density of a white dwarf star's core or a neutron star's crust), which is physically impossible for a "block" of matter on Earth.
2.  **Scale Discrepancy:** The dimensions are in nanometers ($10^{-7} \text{ m}$), which is the scale of molecules/atoms. At this scale, the continuum mechanics used to calculate buoyancy and the macroscopic concept of "tension" in a "cable" are not physically applicable. Surface tension and Brownian motion would dominate the behavior.
3.  **Conclusion:** While the mathematical solution is $T \approx 10^{-8} \text{ N}$, the physical model is **not justifiable** because the parameters provided describe a microscopic object with impossible macroscopic density.

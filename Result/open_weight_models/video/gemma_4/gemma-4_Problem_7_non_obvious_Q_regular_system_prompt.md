To find the tension ($T$) in the cable, we need to analyze the forces acting on the block.

### **1. Identify Given Information:**
*   **Mass of block ($m$):** $1000\text{ nanograms} = 1000 \times 10^{-9}\text{ g} = 10^{-6}\text{ g} = 10^{-9}\text{ kg}$
*   **Density of block ($\rho_{\text{block}}$):** $1.0 \times 10^9\text{ kg/m}^3$
*   **Density of sea water ($\rho_{\text{water}}$):** $1.03 \times 10^3\text{ kg/m}^3$
*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0\text{ m/s}^2$
*   **Dimensions of block:** $100\text{ nm} \times 100\text{ nm} \times 100\text{ nm}$ (Volume $V = (100 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m})^3 = 10^{-18}\text{ m}^3$)

### **2. Physics Principles:**
The block is in equilibrium while submerged in water. The forces acting on it are:
1.  **Tension ($T$):** Acting upwards.
2.  **Buoyant Force ($F_B$):** Acting upwards.
3.  **Weight ($W$):** Acting downwards.

The equilibrium equation is:
$$T + F_B = W \implies T = W - F_B$$

### **3. Calculations:**
*   **Weight ($W$):**
    $$W = m \cdot g = (10^{-9}\text{ kg}) \cdot (10.0\text{ m/s}^2) = 10^{-8}\text{ N}$$

*   **Buoyant Force ($F_B$):**
    The buoyant force is equal to the weight of the displaced water.
    $$F_B = \rho_{\text{water}} \cdot V \cdot g$$
    First, let's verify the volume $V$ from the mass and density: $V = \frac{m}{\rho_{\text{block}}} = \frac{10^{-9}\text{ kg}}{10^9\text{ kg/m}^3} = 10^{-18}\text{ m}^3$.
    $$F_B = (1.03 \times 10^3\text{ kg/m}^3) \cdot (10^{-18}\text{ m}^3) \cdot (10.0\text{ m/s}^2) = 1.03 \times 10^{-14}\text{ N}$$

*   **Tension ($T$):**
    $$T = 10^{-8}\text{ N} - 1.03 \times 10^{-14}\text{ N}$$
    Since $10^{-14}$ is significantly smaller than $10^{-8}$ (by a factor of a million), the buoyant force is negligible in this specific scenario.
    $$T \approx 10^{-8}\text{ N}$$

**Final Answer:**
The tension in the cable is approximately **$10^{-8}\text{ N}$**.

The video shows a block being lowered into a container of sea water. The block is suspended by a cable, and its motion is being observed to determine the tension in the cable.

To find the tension ($T$) in the cable, we need to consider the forces acting on the block. The forces are:
1.  **Weight ($W$):** Acting downwards.
    $W = m \cdot g$
    Given: $m = 1000 \text{ nanograms} = 1000 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-6} \text{ g} = 10^{-9} \text{ kg}$.
    $g = 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$.
    $W = 10^{-9} \text{ kg} \times 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2 = 10^{-8} \text{ N}$.

2.  **Buoyant Force ($F_b$):** Acting upwards when the block is submerged.
    $F_b = \rho_{\text{fluid}} \cdot V \cdot g$
    Given: $\rho_{\text{sea water}} = 1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$.
    The volume ($V$) of the block is calculated from its dimensions:
    $L = W = H = 100 \text{ nanometers} = 100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 10^{-7} \text{ m}$.
    $V = (10^{-7} \text{ m})^3 = 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3$.
    $F_b = (1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3) \times (10^{-21} \text{ m}^3) \times 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2 = 1.03 \times 10^{-18} \text{ N}$.

3.  **Tension ($T$):** Acting upwards.

When the block is submerged and moving at a constant velocity (or at rest), the net force is zero:
$T + F_b = W$
$T = W - F_b$
$T = 10^{-8} \text{ N} - 1.03 \times 10^{-18} \text{ N}$

Since $1.03 \times 10^{-18} \text{ N}$ is significantly smaller than $10^{-8} \text{ N}$, the tension is approximately equal to the weight of the block:
$T \approx 10^{-8} \text{ N}$

**Summary:**
The block is extremely small and light, resulting in a very small weight and buoyant force. The tension in the cable is approximately **$10^{-8} \text{ N}$**.

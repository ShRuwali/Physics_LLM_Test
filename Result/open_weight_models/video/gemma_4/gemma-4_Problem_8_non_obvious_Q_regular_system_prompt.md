To calculate the gravitational force ($F$) between the two bodies, we use Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:
$$F = G \frac{M \cdot m}{d^2}$$

Where $G$ is the gravitational constant $\approx 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$.

**Calculation:**
1.  **Substitute the values:**
    $$F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(6 \times 10^{-9}) \cdot (2 \times 10^{23})}{(3 \times 10^{10})^2}$$
2.  **Simplify the numerator:**
    $$(6 \times 10^{-9}) \cdot (2 \times 10^{23}) = 12 \times 10^{14}$$
3.  **Simplify the denominator:**
    $$(3 \times 10^{10})^2 = 9 \times 10^{20}$$
4.  **Calculate the force:**
    $$F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot \frac{12 \times 10^{14}}{9 \times 10^{20}}$$
    $$F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot (1.333 \times 10^{-6})$$
    $$F \approx 8.898 \times 10^{-17} \text{ Newtons}$$

The gravitational force between Planet A and its Moon at the specified distance is approximately **$8.9 \times 10^{-17} \text{ N}$**. (Note: The extremely small force is due to the incredibly tiny mass of Planet A provided in the prompt).

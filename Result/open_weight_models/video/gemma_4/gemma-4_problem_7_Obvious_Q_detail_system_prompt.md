*   **Dimensions:** $L = W = H = 100\text{ nanometers} = 10^{-7}\text{ m}$

There is a significant error in the problem statement:

If gravity were upward, the block would "fall" toward the ceiling, and the concept of "submerging" into a liquid at the bottom of a container would not occur as shown.
2.  **Mass vs. Density Consistency:** 
    *   Volume of the block ($V$) = $L \times W \times H = (10^{-7}\text{ m})^3 = 10^{-21}\text{ m}^3$.
    *   Calculated Mass = $\text{Density} \times \text{Volume} = 10^5\text{ kg/m}^3 \times 10^{-21}\text{ m}^3 = 10^{-16}\text{ kg}$.
    *   However, the given mass is $10^{-9}\text{ kg}$. This means the provided mass and the provided dimensions/density are mathematically inconsistent.

### **Conclusion**
**The problem cannot be solved accurately** because the provided data is physically impossible (upward gravity) and mathematically inconsistent (the mass does not match the volume and density).

The video shows a block being lowered into a container of sea water. The block is held by a cable, and the tension in the cable is determined by the forces acting on the block.

### **Given Data:**
*   **Mass of block ($m$):** $1000 \text{ nanograms} = 1000 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-6} \text{ g} = 10^{-9} \text{ kg}$
*   **Dimensions of block:** $L = W = H = 100 \text{ nanometers} = 10^{-7} \text{ m}$
*   **Density of block ($\rho_b$):** $10 \times 10^5 \text{ kg/m}^3 = 10^6 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   **Density of sea water ($\rho_w$):** $-1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$ (Note: The negative sign in the prompt is likely a typo for magnitude, as density is positive. We will use $1030 \text{ kg/m}^3$).
*   **Acceleration due to gravity ($g$):** $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$ (upward). This indicates the coordinate system is defined such that "upward" is positive.

### **Analysis:**
1.  **Volume of the block ($V$):**
    $V = L \times W \times H = (10^{-7} \text{ m})^3 = 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3$

2.  **Weight of the block ($W_b$):**
    $W_b = m \cdot g = (10^{-9} \text{ kg}) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2) = 10^{-8} \text{ N}$ (acting downwards).

3.  **Buoyant Force ($F_b$):**
    $F_b = \rho_w \cdot V \cdot g = (1030 \text{ kg/m}^3) \cdot (10^{-21} \text{ m}^3) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2) = 1.03 \times 10^{-18} \text{ N}$ (acting upwards).

4.  **Tension ($T$):**
    Since the block is being lowered (or held stationary) in the water, the forces are:
    $\sum F = F_b + T - W_b = 0$
    $T = W_b - F_b$
    $T = 10^{-8} \text{ N} - 1.03 \times 10^{-18} \text{ N} \approx 10^{-8} \text{ N}$

Because the weight of the block is significantly larger than the buoyant force (due to the extremely small volume of the block), the tension in the cable is approximately equal to the weight of the block.

**Final Answer:**
The tension in the cable is approximately **$10^{-8} \text{ N}$**.

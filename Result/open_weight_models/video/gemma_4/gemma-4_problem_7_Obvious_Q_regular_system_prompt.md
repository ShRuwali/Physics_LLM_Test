The video shows a block suspended by a cable above a container of sea water. The block is in equilibrium, meaning the net force acting on it is zero. To find the tension in the cable, we must consider the forces acting on the block: the tension ($T$) pulling upward, the weight ($W$) pulling downward, and the buoyant force ($F_B$) pushing upward.

### Given Data:
*   Mass of the block ($m$) = $1000 \text{ nanograms} = 1000 \times 10^{-9} \text{ g} = 10^{-6} \text{ g} = 10^{-9} \text{ kg}$
*   Dimensions of the block: $L = W = H = 100 \text{ nanometers} = 100 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 10^{-7} \text{ m}$
*   Density of the block ($\rho$) = $10 \times 10^4 \text{ kg/m}^3 = 10^5 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Density of sea water ($d$) = $1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$ (upward)

### Calculations:

**1. Volume of the block ($V$):**
$$V = L \times W \times H = (10^{-7} \text{ m})^3 = 10^{-21} \text{ m}^3$$

**2. Weight of the block ($W$):**
$$W = m \cdot g = (10^{-9} \text{ kg}) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2) = 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

**3. Buoyant force ($F_B$):**
The buoyant force is equal to the weight of the displaced fluid.
$$F_B = d \cdot V \cdot g = (1.03 \times 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3) \cdot (10^{-21} \text{ m}^3) \cdot (10.0 \text{ m/s}^2)$$
$$F_B = 1.03 \times 10^{-17} \text{ N}$$

**4. Tension in the cable ($T$):**
Since the block is in equilibrium, the sum of the upward forces equals the sum of the downward forces:
$$T + F_B = W$$
$$T = W - F_B$$
$$T = 10^{-8} \text{ N} - 1.03 \times 10^{-17} \text{ N}$$

Because $F_B$ is significantly smaller than $W$ (by a factor of $10^9$), the tension is approximately equal to the weight of the block:
$$T \approx 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$
